The LiHoF4 Soft-Mode Paradox: Does Incomplete Softening Contradict Thermodynamics?

Generalized (Kubo) susceptibilities throughout. v2.3 adds the second-round review corrections: zero-energy support stated as necessary, not sufficient (with per-realization sufficiency conditions); the damped-oscillator pole motion qualified and unified with the Debye form; the elastic FDT normalization fixed ($I_{\rm el}=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm el}$, Matsubara amplitude $\beta I_{\rm el}\delta_{n0}$); demagnetization added as a third mechanism for apparent incomplete softening; the elastic-sector static/dynamic consistency condition and the multilevel (whole-$\chi_0$ vs dominant-sector) scope note; direct meromorphic root finding replacing Padé. v2.4 distinguishes the experimentally observed microwave softening trend from a model-dependent extrapolation to complete zero-energy softening, and states how the preliminary $1/z$ incomplete-softening result can be tested against thermodynamics. Prepared 18 July 2026.

Summary

The exact constraint. A continuous QCP requires the full intrinsic order-parameter susceptibility to be singular at zero frequency: combining the Kramers–Kronig sum rule with the finite first moment gives $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)\le M_1/\omega_g^{2}$ for any true spectral gap $\omega_g$ (Eq. 3a), so a divergent static susceptibility forces zero-energy spectral support. Support is necessary, not sufficient: criticality holds iff the weighted integral (2) itself diverges. Within that criterion the realization is open — a vanishing-residue pole (critical only if $w_p/\omega_p\to\infty$), an overdamped pole ($r\to0$ at finite $A$), a sufficiently singular zero-threshold continuum (a bounded-amplitude $\chi''\sim\omega^{s}$ with $s>0$ does not suffice), or, isothermally at finite $T$, elastic critical weight. A finite-energy maximum of $\chi''$ is compatible with criticality; a true finite lower gap in the complete order-parameter spectrum is not.

Four things to keep distinct (§3–§4): (1) the exact requirement above; (2) the RPA baseline, which realizes it as a sharp lowest electronuclear pole of small residue softening to zero (Eqs. 6, 14) — the branch that is experimentally elusive; (3) possible beyond-RPA realizations — residue collapse, overdamping (the coupled-mode mechanism of §2, a labelled possible mechanism), zero-threshold continuum, elastic weight; (4) what the implemented first-order $1/z$ code actually computes: a self-energy that is real below threshold ($A=0$ as $\eta\to0$), which shifts the critical condition (Eq. 16) and renormalizes the spectrum but generates no dissipative channel — $\eta$-broadening is not physics.

The plotted finite $1/z$ peak is not yet evidence either way (§4c): the solver stitches to a bare-mean-field ordered branch at $B_{c,\mathrm{MF}}$ above the true $1/z$ boundary; the peak extractor reports one global maximum with a path-dependent floor (0.05 meV on the $q$-path — excluding the $\sim$0.01 meV hyperfine branch whose softening is at issue — zero on the spectrum map, where global-maximum selection still misses small-residue poles); and a nonzero ion.demag saturates the measured uniform response even when the intrinsic one diverges. Immediate program (§9): work on the intrinsic response, approach from the paramagnetic side, drive $D(0,H)=1+\Sigma(0)-J(\mathbf q_c)\chi_0(0)\to0$, and run a lowest-singularity search ($\eta\to0$, grid floor $\ll0.01$ meV, all zeros of $D$ with residues) — not a peak-maximum readout. The microwave experiments do not by themselves settle this question: they resolve a low-energy branch and a strong downward trend at finite probe frequencies, while complete closure at zero energy is inferred through RPA and near-critical broadening/inhomogeneity models rather than directly measured (§8). The decisive quantities are $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$ and the lowest spectral support with its weight, not the position of the strongest maximum.

1Linear-response primer: what thermodynamics actually constrains

All susceptibilities are retarded Kubo functions. The Lehmann (spectral) representation, for a Hermitian observable $\hat O_{\mathbf q}$ with eigenstates $H|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle$, occupations $p_n=e^{-\beta E_n}/Z$, and $E_{mn}=E_m-E_n$, is

$$ \chi''(\mathbf q,\omega)\;=\;\pi\sum_{nm}\,(p_n-p_m)\,\bigl|\langle n|\hat O_{\mathbf q}|m\rangle\bigr|^2\,\delta(\omega-E_{mn}), \qquad \chi''(-\omega)=-\chi''(\omega),\quad \omega\,\chi''(\omega)\ge 0. \tag{1} $$

Analyticity of $\chi(\mathbf q,z)$ in the upper half-plane (causality) plus $\chi\to0$ at $|z|\to\infty$ gives the Cauchy representation $\chi(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int d\omega'\,\chi''(\omega')/(\omega'-z)$; setting $z\to0$ and using the oddness of $\chi''$ yields the thermodynamic (Kramers–Kronig) sum rule

$$ \chi(\mathbf q,0)\;=\;\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!d\omega\,\frac{\chi''(\mathbf q,\omega)}{\omega} \;=\;\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\omega\,\frac{\chi''(\mathbf q,\omega)}{\omega}. \tag{2} $$

The quantity that must diverge at $\mathbf q_c$ at a continuous transition is this $1/\omega$-weighted integral. One qualification belongs here, not in a footnote: (2) defines the dynamic Kubo susceptibility; strictly elastic (nonergodic) weight — a $\delta(\omega)$ component from frozen or degenerate degrees of freedom — augments the isothermal susceptibility only and never enters $\chi''(\omega\neq0)$ (§7, item 6). Divergence of the Kubo $\chi(0)$ therefore constrains the inelastic/quasi-elastic spectrum; divergence of the isothermal susceptibility can additionally be carried by elastic critical scattering. Contrast the f-sum (first-moment) rule. Taking the first moment of (1),

$$ \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!d\omega\,\omega\,\chi''(\mathbf q,\omega) =\sum_{nm}(p_n-p_m)\,E_{mn}\,|O_{nm}|^2 =2\sum_n p_n\sum_m E_{mn}|O_{nm}|^2 =\bigl\langle[\hat O_{\mathbf q},[H,\hat O_{-\mathbf q}]]\bigr\rangle, \tag{3} $$

where the second equality follows by relabeling $n\leftrightarrow m$ in the $p_m$ term, and the third from $\langle n|[O,[H,O]]|n\rangle=2\sum_m E_{mn}|O_{nm}|^2$ (insert a complete set: $\langle n|OHO|n\rangle=\sum_m E_m|O_{nm}|^2$ and $\langle n|\{O^2,H\}|n\rangle/1=2E_n\sum_m|O_{nm}|^2$).

Equation (3) is finite (nondivergent) — its expectation values may vary sharply through the transition, but they do not diverge. Combining (2) and (3) turns the folklore into a one-line theorem. Suppose $\chi''(\mathbf q_c,\omega)$ had no spectral support below a true gap $\omega_g>0$; then, using $1/\omega\le\omega/\omega_g^{2}$ on the support,

$$ \chi(\mathbf q_c,0)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{\omega_g}^{\infty}\frac{\chi''(\mathbf q_c,\omega)}{\omega}\,d\omega \;\le\;\frac{2}{\pi}\,\frac{1}{\omega_g^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\omega\,\chi''(\mathbf q_c,\omega)\,d\omega \;=\;\frac{M_1(\mathbf q_c)}{\omega_g^{2}}\;<\;\infty, \tag{3a} $$

with $M_1$ the finite first moment (3). A divergent static Kubo susceptibility therefore forces zero-energy spectral support — but support is necessary, not sufficient: touching zero does not by itself make (2) diverge. The exact criterion is the divergence of the weighted integral (2), and each realization carries its own sufficiency condition: a pole with $\omega_p\to0$ is critical only if $w_p/\omega_p\to\infty$ (Eq. 6); a zero-threshold continuum $\chi''\sim c\,\omega^{s}$ with bounded amplitude contributes $\int_0 c\,\omega^{s-1}\,d\omega\lt\infty$ for $s>0$ and becomes critical only through $s\to0^{+}$, $s\le0$, or a diverging amplitude; a relaxational pole is critical only via $r\to0$ at finite $A$ (Eqs. 21–22), not via $\Gamma_r\to0$ at fixed $\chi(0)$. Within these conditions, a vanishing-residue pole, an overdamped pole, a sufficiently singular continuum, or (for the isothermal susceptibility at finite $T$) elastic critical weight are all admissible realizations. A finite-energy maximum of $\chi''$ is compatible with criticality; a true finite lower gap in the complete order-parameter spectrum is not. Two statements should also be kept separate. At the $T=0$ QCP, the thermodynamic-limit many-body spectrum must become gapless — though the gapless object need not be a sharp neutron-active pole. Along the finite-$T$ critical line (the 0.31 K neutron regime), equilibrium thermodynamics requires divergent static correlations, realizable as a vanishing relaxation rate, a quasi-elastic central component, or elastic critical scattering — not necessarily the closure of a propagating Hamiltonian eigenmode. Conflating the two makes the apparent paradox look stronger than the theorem warrants. That is the entire loophole, stated exactly.

Where poles may live

Propagating (underdamped) pole — damped-harmonic-oscillator (DHO) form:

$$ \chi(\omega)=\frac{f}{\Omega_0^2-\omega^2-i\omega\Gamma}, \qquad \chi(0)=\frac{f}{\Omega_0^2}, \tag{4} $$

with poles at

$$ \omega_{\pm}\;=\;-\,\frac{i\Gamma}{2}\;\pm\;\sqrt{\Omega_0^{2}-\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{4}}\,. \tag{4a} $$

The textbook soft mode — real-axis softening — is the underdamped statement and requires $\Gamma/\Omega_0\to0$ as $\Omega_0\to0$. At fixed nonzero $\Gamma$ the poles stop moving along the real axis once $\Omega_0\lt\Gamma/2$: both become purely imaginary, the slow one approaching $-i\,\Omega_0^{2}/\Gamma$. Indeed, expanding (4) at small $\omega$, $\chi\approx(f/\Omega_0^{2})/(1-i\omega\Gamma/\Omega_0^{2})$ — exactly the Debye form (5) below, with $\chi(0)=f/\Omega_0^{2}$ and $\Gamma_r=\Omega_0^{2}/\Gamma$. A softening oscillator with fixed damping therefore converts itself into the relaxational pole near criticality: (4) and (5) are two limits of one pole pair, not competing models.

Relaxational (overdamped) pole — Debye form:

$$ \chi(\omega)=\frac{\chi(0)}{1-i\omega/\Gamma_r}, \qquad \chi''(\omega)=\chi(0)\,\frac{\omega\,\Gamma_r}{\omega^2+\Gamma_r^2}, \qquad \frac{\chi''(\omega)}{\omega}=\frac{\chi(0)\,\Gamma_r}{\omega^2+\Gamma_r^2}, \tag{5} $$

a single pole at $z=-i\Gamma_r$ on the imaginary axis. $\chi''/\omega$ is a Lorentzian of width $\Gamma_r$ centered at $\omega=0$ — the quasi-elastic central peak. Check against (2): $\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty \frac{\chi(0)\Gamma_r}{\omega^2+\Gamma_r^2}d\omega=\frac{2}{\pi}\chi(0)\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}=\chi(0)$, exactly. Note that $\Gamma_r\to0$ by itself is not critical — in (5), $\chi(0)$ is an independent parameter. Criticality enters through the microscopic relation of Eqs. (21)–(22): $\chi(0)=1/r$ and $\Gamma_r=r/A$ with finite Ohmic $A$, so that $r\to0$ drives $\chi(0)\to\infty$ and $\Gamma_r\to0$ together, the pole sliding down the imaginary axis to the origin. No inelastic feature ever appears, yet the divergence is delivered just as legitimately.

What is fixed by the approximation class

RPA — and any theory whose self-energy is purely real at low $\omega$ — is a Hermitian eigenvalue problem: poles sit on the real axis with infinite lifetime. Within such a theory the only mathematically available realization of $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)\to\infty$ is a real zero of $\chi^{-1}$ reaching $\omega=0$, possibly with collapsing residue (Eq. 6). In RPA for LiHoF4 that zero exists and is physical: the sharp lowest electronuclear pole, whose complete softening is the baseline prediction and whose existence and field dependence microwave spectroscopy corroborates (§8). What a real-$\Sigma$ theory cannot do is realize criticality relaxationally; the overdamped/central-peak route requires $\operatorname{Im}\Sigma(\omega\to0)\neq0$, which must be demonstrated — the implemented first-order kernel does not supply it (§4). Whether the sharp-pole realization survives once interactions, decay channels, domains, phonons, and finite-$T$ dynamics are included is precisely the open question; thermodynamics constrains only the zero-energy support, not the realization.

A pole can soften with vanishing visibility

For a sharp pole at $\omega_p$ with weight $w_p$, i.e. $\chi''(\omega)=\pi w_p\bigl[\delta(\omega-\omega_p)-\delta(\omega+\omega_p)\bigr]$, Eq. (2) gives immediately

$$ \chi(0)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{\pi w_p\,\delta(\omega-\omega_p)}{\omega}\,d\omega=\frac{2\,w_p}{\omega_p}. \tag{6} $$

Divergence requires only $w_p/\omega_p\to\infty$; $w_p$ itself may vanish. Neutron intensity carries the Bose factor: the pole's one-sided integrated intensity is $\propto[1+n(\omega_p)]\,w_p$ — at $T=0$ simply $w_p$ (matrix-element suppressed), while for $\omega_p\ll k_BT$ it grows as $(k_BT/\omega_p)\,w_p=\tfrac12 k_BT\,\chi(0)$ by (6): the soft branch's intensity then tracks the diverging static susceptibility, but sits at $\omega_p\sim$ GHz, far below conventional INS resolution, and registers as quasi-elastic/elastic intensity rather than a resolved mode (§8). Even the RPA electronuclear soft branch — whose character becomes nuclear-dominated near $H_c$, with direct neutron coupling down by $\gamma=g_n\mu_n/g_L\mu_B\approx1/550$ (McKenzie & Stamp 2018) and visibility only through the small admixed electronic component — is nearly invisible as a resolved excitation while carrying the full divergence. "Elusive in $S(\mathbf q,\omega)$" and "critical in $\chi(0)$" are compatible even before any damping is switched on.

2Coupled-mode algebra of the central peak (SrTiO3 paradigm)

Couple the order-parameter mode (bare frequency $\Omega_b$, damping $\Gamma$) linearly to a slow relaxing variable with time $\tau$ and coupling $\delta$ — the Halperin–Varma structure [Phys. Rev. B 14, 4030 (1976)]:

$$ \chi^{-1}(\omega)\;=\;\Omega_b^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\omega\Gamma-\frac{\delta^{2}}{1-i\omega\tau}. \tag{7} $$

Static limit. $\chi^{-1}(0)=\Omega_b^2-\delta^2\equiv\Omega_0^2$: the transition is where the renormalized stiffness $\Omega_0^2\to0$, and $\chi(0)=1/\Omega_0^2$ diverges.

$$ \chi^{-1}(0)=\Omega_b^{2}-\delta^{2}\equiv\Omega_0^{2}. \tag{8} $$

Fast probe ($\omega\tau\gg1$): $\dfrac{\delta^2}{1-i\omega\tau}=\dfrac{\delta^2(1+i\omega\tau)}{1+\omega^2\tau^2}\;\xrightarrow{\ \omega\tau\gg1\ }\;\dfrac{i\delta^2}{\omega\tau}+\dfrac{\delta^2}{\omega^2\tau^2}\to0$, so the relaxor cannot follow and

$$ \chi^{-1}(\omega)\;\approx\;\Omega_b^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\omega\Gamma \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{visible peak at }\ \Omega_b=\sqrt{\Omega_0^{2}+\delta^{2}}\ \xrightarrow{\ \Omega_0\to0\ }\ \delta\neq0. \tag{9} $$

The visible mode saturates at the coupling scale $\delta$.

Slow probe ($\omega\tau\ll1$): expand $\dfrac{\delta^2}{1-i\omega\tau}=\delta^2\bigl(1+i\omega\tau-\omega^2\tau^2+\dots\bigr)$:

$$ \chi^{-1}(\omega)\;\approx\;\Omega_0^{2}-i\omega\,(\Gamma+\delta^{2}\tau)+\mathcal O(\omega^2) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Gamma_{\rm cp}=\frac{\Omega_0^{2}}{\Gamma+\delta^{2}\tau}\;\xrightarrow{\ \delta^2\tau\gg\Gamma\ }\;\frac{\Omega_0^{2}}{\delta^{2}\tau}\to0, \tag{10} $$

a Debye pole whose rate collapses and whose $\chi''/\omega$ weight $\propto1/\Omega_0^2$ diverges.

Weight-partition identity. The high-frequency sector sees stiffness $\Omega_b^2$ and contributes $1/\Omega_b^2$ to the KK integral (2); the remainder resides in the central peak:

$$ \chi(0)=\frac{1}{\Omega_0^{2}} =\underbrace{\frac{1}{\Omega_b^{2}}}_{\text{DHO branch — saturates}} +\underbrace{\frac{\delta^{2}}{\Omega_0^{2}\,\Omega_b^{2}}}_{\text{central peak — diverges}}, \qquad\text{since }\ \frac{1}{\Omega_b^{2}}+\frac{\Omega_b^{2}-\Omega_0^{2}}{\Omega_0^{2}\Omega_b^{2}}=\frac{1}{\Omega_0^{2}}. \tag{11} $$

Scope of (11): it is the well-separated-scales partition of model (7), valid for $\Gamma_{\rm cp}\ll1/\tau\ll\Omega_b$, where the central peak and the DHO branch are spectrally distinct. For general $(\tau,\Gamma)$ the two terms are not independently identifiable components of $\chi''$, and (11) is model-based bookkeeping, not an exact spectral theorem. Note also that (7) retains the bounded-observable high-frequency asymptotics $\chi\sim-1/\omega^{2}$ (finite first moment), which an additive DHO$+$Debye ansatz does not (§6, item 6). Within its regime: one response function, two channels — saturation of the propagating branch and divergence of $\chi(0)$ are two faces of the same self-energy. The entire construction (7)–(11) is a possible dissipative mechanism, requiring a slow relaxing variable coupled to the order parameter; it is not the implemented $1/z$ theory (§4). This is the measured SrTiO3 phenomenology [Riste et al., Solid State Commun. 9, 1455 (1971); Shapiro, Axe, Shirane & Riste, PRB 6, 4332 (1972)]: the soft zone-boundary phonon stalls near $T_c$ while a resolution-limited ($<0.02$ meV, never frequency-resolved since) central component grows and carries the diverging intensity, interpreted as "a low-frequency resonance in the self-energy of the soft mode."

3RPA baseline for LiHoF4 and the built-in weight partition

RPA [Rønnow et al., PRB 75, 054426 (2007); McKenzie & Stamp, PRB 97, 214430 (2018)]:

$$ \chi(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{\chi_0(\omega)}{1-J(\mathbf q)\,\chi_0(\omega)}, \qquad\text{criticality: } 1=J(\mathbf q_c)\,\chi_0(0). \tag{12} $$

The hyperfine coupling inserts an extra low-frequency pole into the single-ion $\chi_0(\omega)$ at $\approx0.01$ meV ($\approx2.4$ GHz) — Rønnow 2007 state explicitly that the crystal-field branch keeps a gap near $H_c$ while "the critical fluctuations at low frequencies derive from the low-frequency pole." This scale matches the 1–3 GHz electronuclear ladder later resolved by microwave spectroscopy [Libersky et al., PRL 127, 207202 (2021); Stamp et al., PRB 110, 134420 (2024)].

The puzzle, stated precisely. The genuine problem is sharper than a comparison between the neutron-visible electronic branch and RPA. RPA predicts that the lowest electronuclear branch itself reaches zero at the QCP; the recent $1/z$ spectra appear to leave that same branch at finite peak energy, and it is that branch which is experimentally elusive. If the transition is continuous, this demands a precise reconciliation — and by (3a) the admissible reconciliations are exactly: the sharp pole survives (renormalized, with possibly collapsing residue), or it converts into an overdamped pole / zero-threshold continuum / elastic weight; a true finite lower gap in the full response is excluded. Which of these the $1/z$ theory selects is an open question that the current calculation has not yet answered (§4c).

Derivation of the saturation-gap formula

Split the single-ion response into electronic and nuclear-derived parts, $\chi_0(\omega)=\chi_0^{J}(\omega)+\chi_n(\omega)$, where $\chi_n$ is the hyperfine pole at $\omega_n\approx0.01$ meV. For $\omega\gg\omega_n$ (but of order the crystal-field splitting $\Delta$), $\chi_n(\omega)=2w_n\omega_n/(\omega_n^2-\omega^2)\to0$, so $\chi_0(\omega)\approx\chi_0^{J}(\omega)$. Model the electronic sector by the dominant doublet transition,

$$ \chi_0^{J}(\omega)\;\approx\;\chi_0^{J}(0)\,\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\Delta^{2}-\omega^{2}}. \tag{13} $$

The crystal-field branch obeys $1=J(\mathbf q_c)\,\chi_0^{J}(E_c)$; imposing the critical condition $J(\mathbf q_c)=1/\chi_0(0)$ from (12):

$$ \chi_0^{J}(0)\,\frac{\Delta^{2}}{\Delta^{2}-E_c^{2}}=\chi_0(0) \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,E_c=\Delta\,\sqrt{1-\frac{\chi_0^{J}(0)}{\chi_0(0)}}\,} \tag{14} $$

— the saturation gap of the visible branch directly measures the fraction of static single-ion weight residing in the low-frequency electronuclear channel. Rønnow 2007: $\chi_0^{J}/\chi_0\approx0.77$ at 0.31 K (23% of the critical static weight is nuclear-derived), giving $E_c\approx0.19$ meV (their full calculation: 0.193 meV) against the measured $\approx0.24$ meV before the global $\times1.15$ scale [Rønnow et al., Science 308, 389 (2005)]. Within the $1/z$ theory (14) acquires mild $\Sigma$-corrections but the structure — gap$^2$ $\propto$ nuclear share — is unchanged. McKenzie & Stamp's RPA statement that "one hybridized mode of the Ising and bath spins always goes soft" refers to the tiny-weight electronuclear branch of Eq. (6); its spectral weight in $\chi_{xx}/\chi_{yy}$ vanishes at the QCP and survives only in $\chi_{zz}$ — the order-parameter channel.

4The first-order 1/z kernel: implemented facts vs proposed extensions

To first order in $1/z$ [Jensen, J. Phys. C 17, 5367 (1984); Jensen, PRB 49, 11833 (1994); applied to LiHoF4 in Rønnow 2007], the $cc$ channel is renormalized by dividing the noninteracting susceptibility by $1+\Sigma(\omega)$:

$$ \chi^{-1}(\mathbf q,\omega)\;=\;\frac{1+\Sigma(\omega)}{\chi_0(\omega)}\;-\;J(\mathbf q), \qquad \Sigma(\omega)=\Sigma'(\omega)+i\,\Sigma''(\omega). \tag{15} $$

(a) The critical condition shifts. Criticality is now

$$ J(\mathbf q_c)\,\chi_0(0;H_c)\;=\;1+\Sigma(0;H_c), \tag{16} $$

not the RPA condition. Rønnow's fitted values $\Sigma_c(0)=0.3004$ (zero field), $0.0932$ (0.31 K, 43.0 kOe), $0.0493$ ($T\to0$, 52.9 kOe) show $\Sigma(0)$ is a few to thirty percent — so evaluating mode softening at the RPA critical field will always show a residual gap simply because the true $H_c$ of the $1/z$ theory has moved. Check whether your "incomplete softening" persists at the self-consistently shifted $H_c$ before invoking anything deeper.

(b) What the implemented kernel actually computes. Per the Codex review of the local implementation (invz_projected: invz_g.m 1–3, invz_sigma.m 1–6, invz_chi_realaxis.m 31–64/82–90, invz_spectra_map.m 136–143/185–189): the reduced two-level Green's function is $g(z)=2n_{01}\Delta/(\Delta^{2}-z^{2})$ — even in $z$ and real below its pole; the effective-medium coupling is initialized at its static Matsubara value (Kw = pt.K(1)) in the standard spectra path, becoming frequency dependent only via an optional Jfull argument that this path does not supply. The self-energy is therefore $\Sigma(z)=\alpha+\gamma\,g(z)$ (with the ordered-phase modification) — purely real below threshold as $\eta\to0$, i.e. $A=0$ identically. The substitution $z=\omega+i\eta$ produces $\operatorname{Im}\Sigma\propto\eta\,\omega$: artificial line broadening, not a derived relaxation channel, and not evidence of a central peak. The implemented theory does two things and only two: it shifts the critical condition (16) and renormalizes the multimode spectrum. An earlier claim in this document — that thermal population of the hyperfine ladder gives $\Sigma''\propto\omega$ at 0.31 K — is withdrawn as a statement about this implementation: the full electronuclear ladder enters only the bare $\chi_0$ (chi0cc_w), while $\Sigma$ is built from the reduced two-level quantities (tl, g, lambda, K); it stands only as a hypothesis for an extended kernel with the full ladder and a genuine dissipative mechanism. Conversely, none of this makes a central peak or zero-threshold continuum invalid as a reconciliation — it has simply not been derived from this implementation; a future $\eta$-independent $-iA\omega$ term or nonanalytic continuum reaching zero would reconcile a finite-energy maximum with the static transition without a sharp RPA-like pole.

Multilevel scope note. One further implementation approximation, flagged by the review: the documented Jensen construction renormalizes only the dominant transition sector, $\widetilde\chi_0=\chi_{\rm dom}/(1+\Sigma)+\chi_{\rm rest}$, leaving weaker transitions bare, whereas the code builds the full 136-state single-ion $\chi_0$ and divides the entire result by $1+\Sigma$ (invz_chi_realaxis.m 32–65). Per Rønnow 2007, the higher transitions contribute $\lesssim1\%$ of the zero-frequency response at the largest field, so this is immaterial for the static soft-mode question — but it should be labelled an implementation approximation rather than an exact transcription of the framework, and it matters more for high-energy spectra and moment/sum-rule accounting (§7, dominant-sector audit).

(c) Three implementation mechanisms that can manufacture "incomplete softening." All are documented in the code and must be excluded before the plotted finite peak is read as physics:

  1. Premature phase stitching. invz_solve_point_ordered.m uses the bare mean-field spontaneous moment, which onsets at the mean-field boundary $B_{c,\mathrm{MF}}$ — above the true $1/z$ boundary $B_{c,1/z}$ — and invz_solve_auto.m tries this ordered solution first, accepting it whenever it converges. Sweeping down from the paramagnet, the spectral map can therefore switch to a nonzero-moment, gapped ordered response at $B_{c,\mathrm{MF}}$ before the paramagnetic $1/z$ stiffness has reached zero. This premature symmetry breaking can make the low-energy branch appear to avoid zero even when the paramagnetic denominator would vanish at its own critical field. Remedy: approach $B_{c,1/z}$ strictly from the paramagnetic side (invz_solve_point, warm-started), monitoring the scalar $$ D(0,H)\;=\;1+\Sigma(0,H)-J(\mathbf q_c)\,\chi_0(0,H)\;=\;\chi_0(0,H)\,\bigl[\,r(H)\,\bigr]\;\longrightarrow\;0 , \tag{15a} $$ (the code's pt.crit), which is Eq. (15) rearranged: $\chi(\mathbf q_c,z)=\chi_0(z)/D(z)$. Since $\chi_0(0)\neq0$, an internally consistent continuation with $D(0,H_c)=0$ must contain a zero-frequency singularity of $\chi(\mathbf q_c,z)$ — provided the static and dynamic sectors use the same single-ion susceptibility, elastic contribution included. In the code they currently do not: invz_solve_point.m builds the instability diagnostic with elastic=true, while invz_chi_realaxis.m uses elastic=false. At the finite-$B_x$ paramagnetic boundary the $\pi$-rotation about $x$ ($\tau^z,I^z\to-\tau^z,-I^z$; $\tau^{\pm},I^{\pm}\to\tau^{\mp},I^{\mp}$) is a symmetry of every Hamiltonian term except the small stray $\Delta_n^{y}$, forcing the diagonal elements $c_{mm}$ — and hence the elastic term — to vanish barring degeneracies; the two routes are therefore expected to agree there, but this should be verified numerically, not assumed. If a pole search still reports no singularity after the elastic conventions are matched, the static and dynamic sectors are not using the same approximation, or the continuation has missed it — an internal inconsistency, not evidence of a gapped critical response.
  2. Peak-maximum readout is not lowest spectral support. invz_peak_energy.m returns the position of a single global maximum of $\chi''$ in a chosen window and censors boundary maxima to NaN; the window floor is path dependent — invz_spectra_qpath.m retains peak_wmin = 0.05 meV, above the $\approx0.01$ meV hyperfine scale and excluding the branch whose softening is at issue, while invz_spectra_map.m now defaults to peak_wmin = 0. Even with a zero floor, global-maximum selection is insufficient: a small-residue soft pole can lie below a stronger finite-energy maximum and be reported as a finite Epeak. Remedy: the lowest-singularity search of §6 step 3 — all zeros of $D(z)$ with residues, frequency-grid floor well below 0.01 meV, $\eta\to0$.
  3. Demagnetization saturates the measured uniform response. The code can transform the intrinsic response by the shape correction $$ \chi_{\rm meas}\;=\;\frac{\chi_{\rm int}}{1+J_{\rm shape}\,\chi_{\rm int}}\;\xrightarrow[\ \chi_{\rm int}\to\infty\ ]{}\;\frac{1}{J_{\rm shape}}\,, \tag{15b} $$ applied in invz_chi_realaxis.m and entering spectrum maps whenever ion.demag is nonzero: the measured uniform susceptibility saturates even while the intrinsic one diverges — a third, purely geometric source of apparent incomplete softening. (Consistent with Rønnow 2007's remark that the demagnetization field cancels in the critical condition and ordering occurs at wavevectors infinitesimally away from zero.) Remedy: run all thermodynamic soft-mode tests on the intrinsic response — ion.demag = 0, or a well-defined finite-$q$ route with $q\to0^{+}$ — and plot intrinsic and shape-corrected results separately, labelled.

5Conditional low-frequency diagnostic (the core logic)

The expansion below presupposes a regular, Ohmic low-frequency structure; it is a conditional diagnostic, not a generic theorem. Step 0 — establish the $\eta\to0$ analytic structure first. Compute $\chi^{-1}(\mathbf q_c,\omega)$ at successively smaller $\eta$ and classify the $\omega\to0$ behavior: analytic and even (the below-threshold response of a gapped, real-$\Sigma$ system — and the current implementation: $A=0$ exactly); Ohmic ($\operatorname{Im}\chi^{-1}\propto-\omega$, i.e. $A>0$ — to be independently demonstrated, never assumed); or nonanalytic ($|\omega|^{s}$, $\omega^{2}\ln\omega$, or a branch cut reaching $\omega=0$, as can occur at a continuum threshold or at the QCP itself). A complex self-energy at some finite frequency does not imply $\Sigma''\propto\omega$ as $\omega\to0$; and a static divergence may be carried by a branch point rather than an isolated pole — "the critical object" is a pole or branch point reaching $z=0$. Only in the first two cases does the following regular expansion apply. At $\mathbf q_c$, write at small $\omega$:

$$ \chi^{-1}(\mathbf q_c,\omega)\;=\;r\;-\;iA\,\omega\;-\;B\,\omega^{2}\;+\;\mathcal O(\omega^{3}), \qquad r=\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)^{-1}. \tag{17} $$

Poles: $B\omega^{2}+iA\omega-r=0$, i.e.

$$ \omega_{\pm}\;=\;\frac{-\,iA\;\pm\;\sqrt{\,4Br-A^{2}\,}}{2B}. \tag{18} $$
  1. $A=0$ (real $\Sigma$ at low $\omega$ — the current implementation). $\chi^{-1}=r-B\omega^{2}+\dots$, and the lowest zero sits at $\omega_p=\sqrt{r/B}$, reaching zero iff $r\to0$. In the multimode electronuclear response there are many zeros of $D(z)$: at $r\to0$ it is the weak low-energy electronuclear zero that closes — with residue that may simultaneously collapse (Eq. 6) — while the strong crystal-field zero saturates per (14). Before calling the closing zero critical, report both its residue scaling and its contribution $2w_p/\omega_p$ to $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$: support is necessary, sufficiency is the divergence of (2) (§1). Consequences: track all zeros, not one intensity maximum (§4c-ii); apparent saturation of a tracked feature with $r>0$ means the tracked feature is not the critical one, or the sweep is sitting on the premature ordered branch / away from the shifted critical field (§4a, §4c-i). Within a real-$\Sigma$ theory, incomplete softening of the lowest zero can never coexist with $r\to0$ — Eq. (15a) forbids it.
  2. $A>0$, independently established, and $r\to0$. For $4Br<A^{2}$ both roots of (18) are purely imaginary. Expanding the slow root, $\sqrt{A^{2}-4Br}=A\bigl(1-2Br/A^{2}+\dots\bigr)$: $$ \omega_{\rm slow}\;=\;-\,\frac{i}{2B}\Bigl[A-\sqrt{A^{2}-4Br}\Bigr]\;=\;-\,i\,\frac{r}{A}\,\bigl[1+\mathcal O(Br/A^{2})\bigr]\;\longrightarrow\;0 . \tag{19} $$ Sufficiently close to criticality the critical pole is then always overdamped (crossover at $A^{2}=4Br$): relaxation rate $\Gamma_r=r/A=1/[A\,\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)]\to0$, central-peak weight $\to\infty$, while the dispersive branch — which lives outside the small-$\omega$ expansion, cf. Eq. (7) — saturates. A conditional result: legitimate if and only if Step 0 has demonstrated the Ohmic coefficient.
  3. Nonanalytic low-$\omega$ structure. Neither pole analysis applies; fit the appropriate continuum form (e.g. power-law $\chi''\sim|\omega|^{s}$ with a threshold collapsing to zero), extract $r$ from the static sector only, and treat the critical object as a branch point reaching $z=0$.
  4. $r(H)>0$ for all $H$ (all cases). $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$ never diverges: the transition is destroyed or displaced at this order — or the sweep never left the stitched ordered branch (§4c-i). Only this outcome conflicts with the experimentally established continuous transition; treat it as a probable artifact and run the audits of §4c and §7 before believing it.

6Practical extraction of $(r,\,A,\,\Gamma_r)$ from your $\Sigma(\omega)$ output

Inputs already in the code: the MF single-ion $\chi_0(\omega)=\chi_0'(\omega)+i\chi_0''(\omega)$ (real below the lowest single-ion transition at $T=0$; complex at finite $T$), $\Sigma(\omega)$, and $J(\mathbf q)$. From (15), writing $1/\chi_0=(\chi_0'-i\chi_0'')/|\chi_0|^2$:

$$ r\;=\;\operatorname{Re}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf q_c,0)\;=\;\frac{1+\Sigma'(0)}{\chi_0(0)}\;-\;J(\mathbf q_c) \qquad(\chi_0''(0)=0), \tag{20} $$ $$ \operatorname{Im}\chi^{-1}(\mathbf q_c,\omega)\;=\;\frac{\Sigma''(\omega)\,\chi_0'(\omega)-\bigl[1+\Sigma'(\omega)\bigr]\chi_0''(\omega)}{|\chi_0(\omega)|^{2}}\;\equiv\;-\,A\,\omega+\mathcal O(\omega^{3}), $$ $$ A\;=\;\lim_{\omega\to0}\;\frac{\bigl[1+\Sigma'(\omega)\bigr]\chi_0''(\omega)\;-\;\Sigma''(\omega)\,\chi_0'(\omega)}{\omega\,|\chi_0(\omega)|^{2}} \;\;\xrightarrow[\ \chi_0''\to0\ ]{T=0,\ \omega\ \text{below threshold}}\;\; -\,\lim_{\omega\to0}\frac{\Sigma''(\omega)}{\omega\,\chi_0(0)} . \tag{21} $$

Near criticality the low-$\omega$ response is then the Debye form by construction:

$$ \chi(\mathbf q_c,\omega)\;\approx\;\frac{1}{r-iA\omega}\;=\;\frac{\chi(0)}{1-i\omega/\Gamma_r}, \qquad \chi(0)=\frac1r,\qquad \Gamma_r=\frac{r}{A}=\frac{1}{A\,\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)} . \tag{22} $$

Passivity check built in: from (22), $\chi''(\omega)=A\omega/(r^2+A^2\omega^2)\ge0\iff A\ge0$. A negative $A$ anywhere is an analytic-structure bug, full stop. Conditionality: Eqs. (21)–(22) presuppose the Ohmic form; they apply only after §5 Step 0 has established $A>0$ from an $\eta$-independent calculation. In the current implementation $\Sigma''$ vanishes below threshold as $\eta\to0$ (§4b), so (21) correctly returns $A=0$ — there is at present nothing to extract, and any nonzero fitted $A$ at finite $\eta$ is the artificial $\eta\omega$ term.

Steps (in priority order)

  1. $r(H)$ from the static sector — the primary diagnostic. The $n=0$ Matsubara value is exact: $r(H)=[1+\Sigma(0;H)]/\chi_0(0;H)-J(\mathbf q_c)$, Eq. (20), equivalently $D(0,H)/\chi_0(0,H)$ with $D$ the code's pt.crit, Eq. (15a). No continuation, no $\eta$. Approach strictly from the paramagnetic side (invz_solve_point, warm-started), bypassing the bare-MF ordered stitch (§4c-i); the zero crossing is the true $1/z$ critical field $B_{c,1/z}$. This single curve answers the thermodynamic question: $r\to0\iff\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)\to\infty\iff$ continuous transition intact at this order. Run it on the intrinsic response — ion.demag = 0, or a finite-$q$ route with $q\to0^{+}$ (§4c-iii): a demagnetization-saturated uniform susceptibility is not $r(H)$.
  2. $\eta\to0$ classification of the low-frequency structure (§5, Step 0). Build $\chi^{-1}(\mathbf q_c,\omega)$ from (15) on a logarithmic grid with floor well below the hyperfine scale (down to $\sim10^{-3}\times0.01$ meV) at successively smaller $\eta$; extrapolate. Expected for the current implementation: real, even, $A=0$ below threshold, with $\operatorname{Im}\propto\eta\omega$ vanishing.
  3. Lowest-singularity search at $B_{c,1/z}$ (replaces any Epeak readout). The implemented $\chi_0(z)$ and $\Sigma(z)$ are explicitly meromorphic with closed forms, so evaluate the analytically defined $D(z)$ directly in the complex plane and root-find: locate every zero — real and purely imaginary — with continuation in field, contour / argument-principle root counting for completeness, a frequency mesh well below 0.01 meV, the peak_wmin floor removed, and residues tracked separately from positions. Padé or other data-driven continuation is at most a cross-check, not the primary method: at 0.31 K the first nonzero Matsubara point sits at 0.168 meV (step 5), far above the target scale, whereas direct evaluation is better conditioned and transparent. Alongside, run the elastic-sector closure (§4c-i): compare the static susceptibility of the instability test with the $z\to0$ limit of the dynamic response, once with and once without the elastic contribution, and confirm agreement under a matched convention. Then confirm (15a) — $D(0,B_{c,1/z})=0$ accompanied by the corresponding zero-frequency singularity — and verify the weak electronuclear zero closes as $r\to0$ while the crystal-field zero obeys (14).
  4. Conditional $A(H)$, $\Gamma_r(H)$. Only if step 2 demonstrates a genuine Ohmic term — which requires a frequency-dependent effective-medium kernel (the Jfull path with the full electronuclear ladder) or an explicit bath/coupled-mode mechanism — apply (21)–(22) with the passivity check, and verify $\Gamma_r\to0$ as $H\to B_{c,1/z}$ (the central peak in $\chi''/\omega$ is then a Lorentzian of width $\Gamma_r$, integrated weight $\pi\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$, by (5)).
  5. Matsubara-axis use — corrected. The retarded expansion (17) continues via $\omega\to i\nu$ ($\nu>0$) to $$ \chi^{-1}(\mathbf q_c,i\nu)\;=\;r\;+\;A\,\nu\;+\;B\,\nu^{2}\;+\;\dots \tag{23} $$ but two facts limit its use for $A$. First, at $T=0.31$ K the first nonzero bosonic frequency is $\nu_1=2\pi k_BT\approx0.168$ meV $\approx40.6$ GHz — about $17\times$ the 0.01 meV hyperfine scale — so there are no nonzero Matsubara samples inside the relevant window (the discrete grid of invz_matsubara.m is correct; the window is simply empty). Second, an Ohmic retarded term maps to $A|\nu_n|$ on the imaginary axis — nonanalytic at $\nu=0$ — so a Taylor fit across $\nu=0$ is invalid in principle. Use the imaginary axis for $r$ (the $n=0$ point, step 1) always; for $A$ only with dense, effectively-$T{=}0$ data; and use numerical analytic continuation only with synthetic-data validation and uncertainty estimates.
  6. Two-component decomposition — window-restricted. The additive fit $$ \chi(\omega)=\frac{f}{\Omega_0^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\omega\Gamma}\;+\;\frac{\chi_{\rm cp}}{1-i\omega/\Gamma_r} $$ is a phenomenological form for a clearly limited low-frequency interval $\omega\ll\Omega_0$ only: the Debye term falls as $1/\omega$ ($\chi''\to\chi_{\rm cp}\Gamma_r/\omega$), so used over the full spectrum it carries a divergent first moment and violates (3) and the bounded-observable $\sim M_1/\omega^{2}$ asymptotics. For a causal full-spectrum description fit the coupled-mode form (7) — correct $-\omega^{2}$ asymptotics — and verify (2) and (3) on the fitted function. Within the window: track ($\Omega_0$ saturating, $f$ finite) vs ($\chi_{\rm cp}$ diverging, $\Gamma_r\to0$); identity (11) is the consistency check in its regime of validity.

7Numerical sum-rule and analyticity battery

Run these on the same $\chi''(\mathbf q,\omega)$ the code outputs; each failure mode has a distinct signature.

  1. KK / thermodynamic consistency, Eq. (2). Compare the directly computed $\chi(\mathbf q,0)$ with $\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\chi''/\omega\,d\omega$ at several $\mathbf q$. Disagreement that grows as the real-axis broadening $\eta$ is reduced $\Rightarrow$ the low-$\omega$ spectrum is being misrepresented (common when Matsubara sums are shortcut on the real axis). Extrapolate $\eta\to0$.
  2. f-sum rule with a sharp $q$-independence test. Every term of the truncated electronuclear Hamiltonian either commutes with $\tau^z$ or flips it. For any pure $\tau$-flipping term $V$ (i.e. $[\tau^z,V]=\pm2V$, e.g. $V\propto\tau^{\pm}\otimes(\text{anything nuclear})$): $$ [\tau^z,[V,\tau^z]]=[\tau^z,\mp2V]=\mp2(\pm2V)=-\,4V \qquad\text{(flip lemma)} . \tag{24} $$ All Ising–Ising interaction terms ($V_{ij}\tau^z_i\tau^z_j$), the longitudinal hyperfine $A_z\tau^zI^z$, and the nuclear field $\boldsymbol\Delta_n\!\cdot\!\mathbf I$ commute with $\tau^z$ and drop out; hence the double commutator in (3) is purely single-site and $q$-independent: $$ \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!d\omega\,\omega\,\chi''_{zz}(\mathbf q,\omega) =\bigl\langle[\tau^z,[H,\tau^z]]\bigr\rangle =-4\bigl\langle H_{\rm flip}\bigr\rangle =4\Delta(B_x)\langle\tau^x\rangle-4A_\perp\langle\tau^+I^-{+}\tau^-I^+\rangle-4A_{++}\langle\tau^+I^+{+}\tau^-I^-\rangle . \tag{25} $$ (Pauli check for the field term: $[\tau^x,\tau^z]=-2i\tau^y\Rightarrow[H_\Delta,\tau^z]=2i\Delta\tau^y\Rightarrow[\tau^z,[H_\Delta,\tau^z]]=2i\Delta\,[\tau^z,\tau^y]=4\Delta\tau^x$, consistent with (24) for $H_\Delta=-\Delta\tau^x$.) For the physical $\chi_{J^zJ^z}$ multiply by $C_{zz}^2(B_x)$. Evaluate the expectation values numerically in the 16-dim (or your $3\times8$-dim) MF eigenbasis, at the same order as the spectrum. RPA saturates this moment exactly (single-mode check: weight $\times$ energy $=2c^2E$, $q$-independent); the $1/z$ spectrum must (a) reproduce it to $\mathcal O(1/z)$ and (b) be flat in $q$. Any $q$-dependence of the computed first moment $=$ spectral-weight bookkeeping error at that order; a violation that grows approaching $H_c$ is precisely the signature of weight being lost rather than transferred to $\omega\approx0$. Scope: exact $q$-flatness holds for the projected longitudinal model as written; if transverse, off-diagonal dipolar, exchange, or higher-crystal-field-level couplings (the $\sim$1% correction terms of Tabei et al. noted by McKenzie & Stamp) are restored dynamically, they need not commute with $\tau^z$ and reintroduce weak $q$-dependence — treat percent-level structure as the model's floor, not a bug.
  3. Total-moment / fluctuation–dissipation closure. The FDT gives the equal-time correlator as $\langle\delta O_{\mathbf q}\delta O_{-\mathbf q}\rangle=\frac1\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,[1{+}n(\omega)]\chi''\,+\,I_{\rm el}(\mathbf q)$, where $I_{\rm el}$ is the strictly elastic (nonergodic, diagonal/degenerate) weight that never enters $\chi''(\omega\neq0)$; folding with $[1{+}n(-\omega)]=-n(\omega)$ and $\chi''(-\omega)=-\chi''(\omega)$: $$ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf q}\Biggl[\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\omega\,\coth\!\Bigl(\frac{\beta\omega}{2}\Bigr)\,\chi''_{zz}(\mathbf q,\omega)\;+\;I_{\rm el}(\mathbf q)\Biggr] \;=\;1-\langle\tau^z\rangle^{2}\quad\text{per site}\qquad\bigl((\tau^z)^2=1\bigr). \tag{26} $$ One convention, stated explicitly and used throughout: $I_{\rm el}(\mathbf q)$ is the equal-time elastic variance, $I_{\rm el}=\operatorname{Var}_{\rm el}(O)=\sum_m c_{mm}^{2}p_m-\bigl(\sum_m c_{mm}p_m\bigr)^{2}$ per mode (dimensionless), while the corresponding Matsubara amplitude carries the explicit $\beta$: $\chi_{\rm el}(i\omega_n)=\beta\,I_{\rm el}\,\delta_{n0}$ — this is the elastic $\delta_{\omega_r,0}$ term of item 6, and $I_{\rm el}=\beta^{-1}\chi_{\rm el}(i\omega_0)$. Identifying the equal-time intensity with $\beta\operatorname{Var}\,\delta_{n0}$ itself conflates an equal-time correlator with a Matsubara susceptibility — a factor-of-$\beta$ error. $I_{\rm el}$ vanishes at $T\to0$ in the para phase, where the inelastic-only form holds. The closure bounds the total inelastic + quasi-elastic + elastic budget and catches double counting between a central peak and the elastic line.
  4. KK on $\Sigma$ itself. $\Sigma(\omega)$ inherits retarded analyticity and the reality condition $\Sigma(-\omega)=\Sigma^*(\omega)$ on the real axis, hence the dispersion relation $$ \Sigma'(\omega)-\Sigma'(\infty)\;=\;\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathcal P\!\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\nu\;\frac{\nu\,\Sigma''(\nu)}{\nu^{2}-\omega^{2}} . \tag{27} $$ KK-transform the computed $\Sigma''$ and compare with the computed $\Sigma'$. Mismatch $\Rightarrow$ the self-energy was assembled with inconsistent analytic structure (principal-value pieces at finite $\eta$, real-axis-only shortcuts) — the single most likely source of a spurious "no divergence" result.
  5. Passivity everywhere: $\omega\,\chi''(\mathbf q,\omega)\ge0$ for all $\mathbf q,\omega,H$. Negative spectral weight anywhere invalidates the pole analysis downstream.
  6. Ergodicity / elastic-term bookkeeping. The MF Green's function in the Rønnow/Jensen formalism contains an explicitly elastic piece, $-\beta\bigl[\sum_m c_{mm}^2p_m-(\sum_m c_{mm}p_m)^2\bigr]\delta_{\omega_r,0}$, vanishing at $T\to0$ and in the para phase. This is the isothermal-minus-Kubo (nonergodic) weight: in the ordered phase, and in any regime with quasi-static degrees of freedom (frozen nuclear polarization, pinned domain walls), part of the divergence migrates into a strictly elastic $\delta(\omega)$ component that no inelastic probe resolves — consistent with the strong quasi-elastic scattering Rønnow et al. report at $\mathbf Q=(2,0,0)$ at the critical field. When comparing $\chi(\mathbf q,0)$ against a thermodynamic (isothermal) measurement, add this term back; the Kubo $\chi$ alone can undercount.
  7. Dominant-sector audit. Quantify the whole-$\chi_0$ vs dominant-transition-only renormalization (§4b, multilevel scope note): recompute with $\widetilde\chi_0=\chi_{\rm dom}/(1+\Sigma)+\chi_{\rm rest}$ and compare $r(H)$, the low-frequency spectrum, and the first-moment budget. The difference should be at the $\lesssim1\%$ level at $\omega=0$ (Rønnow 2007) and larger at high energies; a bigger static discrepancy flags a bug, not physics.
  8. Demagnetization audit. Show intrinsic (ion.demag=0) and shape-corrected responses separately at each field, and confirm the transform (15b) is absent from every quantity entering the criticality diagnostics.
  9. Root completeness. Confirm by contour / argument-principle counting that no low-residue real or purely imaginary zero of $D(z)$ is missed by the frequency mesh or peak finder (§6 step 3).

8Experimental picture and precedents

Two probes, two regimes — keep them distinct. What the microwave measurements directly establish. Libersky et al. [PRL 127, 207202 (2021)] observe a set of coherent GHz electronuclear excitations, resonant crossings on both sides of the transition, and enhanced dissipation near the critical field. These data establish the existence of the low-energy electronuclear branch, its strong downward field dependence, and its sensitivity to a longitudinal field. The measurement window, however, is approximately 0.9–5.0 GHz: the lowest reported resonance is near 0.93 GHz, not zero. In their Fig. 5 the measured $E_{21}$ points remain at finite probe frequencies and are joined by a guide to the eye; the curve that reaches zero is the finite-temperature RPA calculation. Thus the experiment demonstrates substantial softening toward the QCP, but it does not track a sharp pole continuously to zero energy.

Reservation about the claim of complete softening. The distinction becomes explicit in Stamp et al. [PRB 110, 134420 (2024)]. Their Fig. 3 compares a weak-damping calculation ($\Gamma_1=1\ \mu$K) and a strong-damping calculation ($\Gamma_1=500$ mK) with data at 55 mK; the paper states that the strong-damping limit best reproduces the approximately linear experimental approach to the transition. The same work invokes additional infrared dissipation from soft photons and phonons, plus longitudinal-field inhomogeneity that can smear the nominal zero mode, and explicitly notes that a complete quantitative theory of those infrared contributions remains unresolved. These are plausible explanations for why an RPA pole that reaches zero may become experimentally invisible, but they make complete zero-energy softening a model-dependent inference, not a direct observation. Accordingly, the microwave work should be cited as evidence for an electronuclear branch and pronounced softening, not as experimental proof that its gap closes completely.

Relevance of the present $1/z$ result. The preliminary $1/z$ calculation at $T\sim1$ mK appears instead to leave the tracked lowest electronuclear feature at finite energy near the transition. This is precisely the alternative raised here, but “feature” must remain the cautious term until §6 has located the actual lowest pole or branch point and its residue; a finite maximum is not yet a finite gap. If a direct root calculation confirms that the propagating electronuclear pole does not close, thermodynamics does not require that particular pole to reach zero: criticality may instead be carried by an overdamped pole, a zero-threshold continuum, or strictly elastic weight. In the current standard $1/z$ implementation, however, $\Sigma$ is real below threshold and supplies none of those dissipative alternatives. Therefore, if the matched intrinsic static calculation has $D(0,H_c)=0$, the same meromorphic response must contain a zero-frequency singularity. A genuinely finite lower gap in every channel together with divergent $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$ would signal a mismatched static/dynamic approximation, an incorrectly identified $1/z$ critical field, or a missed small-residue zero—not a new thermodynamic possibility. A first-order transition would remove the soft-mode requirement, but would be a separate physical claim requiring independent evidence. These works quote the susceptibility-derived quantum critical field $H_c\approx4.93$ T ($T\to0$), versus the neutron-experiment 42.4 kOe $\approx4.24$ T at 0.31 K; the value must be matched to probe and temperature.

Neutron scattering at 0.31 K: intensity transfers from the electronic excitation to much lower-energy ($\sim10\ \mu$eV) electronuclear modes, with strong, genuine quasi-elastic scattering at $\mathbf Q=(2,0,0)$, $\omega\approx0$, at the critical field (Rønnow et al. 2005/2007); the original report attributes this response to thermalization and dephasing of the composite modes. Two non-exclusive readings put the critical weight at $\omega\approx0$ without invoking the implemented $\Sigma$: the Bose-enhanced intensity of the coherent GHz branch (§1, $\propto k_BT\,\chi(0)/2$, unresolvable from the elastic line at conventional resolution), and additional finite-$T$ dissipative physics of the §2 type. Which of the two dominates is exactly what neutron spin echo can decide; identifying the quasi-elastic response with the implemented Jensen self-energy is not warranted without further calculation (§4b).

"Extreme broadening" vs central peak. Broadening from the nuclear spin bath [Prokof'ev & Stamp, Rep. Prog. Phys. 63, 669 (2000)] is real but is a symptom; the defensible core statement is spectral-weight transfer into an overdamped zero-frequency channel. The two are distinguishable: a broadened underdamped mode has $\operatorname{Re}\omega_{\rm pole}>0$; a central peak has $\operatorname{Re}\omega_{\rm pole}=0$. Neutron spin echo (neV resolution) at $H_c$, $\mathbf Q=(2,0,0)$, directly discriminates — and note that in SrTiO3 the central peak has never been frequency-resolved (nearly static), the opposite of "extremely broad."

Precedents: SrTiO3 (§2); order-disorder ferroelectrics (KDP family), where Debye relaxational components carry criticality while optic modes stay gapped; TlCuCl3, where the longitudinal mode is critically well-defined because strongly damped [Rüegg et al., PRL 100, 205701 (2008); Merchant et al., Nat. Phys. 10, 373 (2014)]; hyperfine-arrested partial softening in other non-Kramers singlet systems (e.g. Ho3Ga5O12-type).

9Recommendations (staged)

Stage 1 — simulation consistency audit (before any interpretation). Do not read the plotted finite $1/z$ peak as proof that the electronuclear soft mode remains gapped until: (0) the test runs on the intrinsic response — ion.demag = 0 or a finite-$q$ route with $q\to0^{+}$, Eq. (15b); (i) the premature bare-MF ordered stitch near $B_c$ is bypassed — approach $B_{c,1/z}$ strictly from the paramagnetic side (invz_solve_point, warm-started) and drive $D(0,H)\to0$, Eq. (15a); (ii) the elastic-sector closure is passed — static instability diagnostic and $z\to0$ dynamic limit agree under a matched elastic convention (§4c-i); (iii) the lowest singularity or spectral threshold of that same response is located as $\eta\to0$, with the peak_wmin floor removed and the grid floor $\ll0.01$ meV — every zero of $D(z)$ with its residue, not one intensity maximum (§4c, §6 step 3). Decision rule: if the strongest maximum stays finite while spectral support (a zero of $D$, a vanishing threshold, or an established overdamped pole) reaches zero with a residue budget that makes (2) diverge — a legitimate beyond-RPA reconciliation, classified by §5. If only the shape-corrected uniform response stays finite while the intrinsic one diverges — demagnetization saturation, Eq. (15b), not a gap. If every pole and continuum threshold remains finite while $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$ diverges after the elastic conventions are matched — the approximation or continuation is internally inconsistent, by (15a). If $r(H)$ never reaches zero — case 4 of §5.

Stage 2 — audits and kernel extension (§7, §4). Priorities: KK-on-$\Sigma$ (item 4) and $q$-flatness of the first moment (item 2) — these catch essentially all known ways a first-order implementation fakes or destroys a transition; the closure (26) with $I_{\rm el}$ catches elastic double counting. Test sensitivity near quasi-degenerate hyperfine-split MF levels, where the expansion parameter can locally blow up; benchmark the phase boundary against QMC [Chakraborty et al., PRB 70, 144411 (2004)] and experiment, recalling the known $1/z$ overestimate of fluctuation suppression at small transverse field (Rønnow 2007 vs QMC). Only then extend the kernel — the Jfull frequency-dependent path with the full electronuclear ladder, or an explicit bath/coupled-mode term — and only if that produces an $\eta$-independent Ohmic (or continuum) structure, extract $A$, $\Gamma_r$ via (21)–(22). Add the dominant-sector audit (§7): quantify whole-$\chi_0$ vs dominant-transition-only renormalization before trusting sum-rule budgets. Throughout, report the lowest pole, relaxation rate, continuum threshold, spectral maximum, and elastic weight as separate observables — never one number standing in for another.

Stage 3 — experiment (two probes, two regimes). (i) Neutron spin echo / quasi-elastic scattering at $H_c$, $\mathbf Q=(2,0,0)$, neV-scale resolution: a resolution-limited central component with diverging weight $\Rightarrow$ critical weight at $\omega\approx0$ (Bose-enhanced coherent branch and/or dissipative central component, §8); a resolved finite-energy Lorentzian tracking $\Gamma_r$ or a mode gap $\Rightarrow$ the corresponding §5 case. (ii) Direct static/low-frequency $\chi_{zz}(\mathbf q\to\mathbf q_c,\omega\to0)$ (ac susceptibility / loop-gap resonator), tracking the divergence itself — the probe-level analogue of $r(H)$. (iii) Correlate the microwave soft-branch coupling strength with the residue $w_p$ of Eq. (6) as $H\to H_c$: residue collapse vs survival discriminates the sharp-pole and vanishing-residue realizations.

Bottom line

A continuous QCP requires the full intrinsic electronuclear order-parameter susceptibility to be singular at zero frequency — by (2)+(3), any true lower spectral gap $\omega_g$ caps the static response at $M_1/\omega_g^{2}$, Eq. (3a). RPA realizes the singularity as a sharp lowest electronuclear pole whose energy vanishes, with small residue (Eq. 6) and a visible crystal-field gap that measures the nuclear share of the static weight (Eq. 14, $\sim$23% at 0.31 K). The microwave experiments establish a low-energy electronuclear branch and pronounced softening toward the transition, but do not directly observe a pole at zero energy; complete closure is inferred through RPA together with strong-damping and field-inhomogeneity arguments (§8). Beyond RPA that sharp-pole realization is not compulsory: the residue may collapse, the pole may become overdamped (§2 — a possible mechanism, contingent on a demonstrated Ohmic term), or the spectrum may develop a zero-threshold continuum or, isothermally, elastic critical weight. What is compulsory is zero-energy spectral support sufficient to produce the divergent static susceptibility: a finite-energy maximum of $\chi''$ is compatible with criticality; a true finite lower gap in the complete order-parameter response is not. The preliminary $T\sim1$ mK $1/z$ result—an apparently finite lowest feature—is therefore a genuine and experimentally motivated question, but it is not yet a demonstrated finite gap. The current calculation has not distinguished these possibilities, because its phase stitching, peak selection, demagnetization handling, and real-axis continuation do not constitute a lowest-singularity calculation (§4c). The decisive computations are $r(H)=D(0,H)/\chi_0(0,H)$ on the intrinsic response approached from the paramagnetic side, Eqs. (15a)/(20), and the lowest-singularity search as $\eta\to0$ with the spectral floor removed. Decision rule: if the strongest maximum stays finite while spectral support reaches zero with a residue budget that makes (2) diverge, the result is a legitimate beyond-RPA reconciliation, classified by §5; if only the shape-corrected uniform response stays finite, that is demagnetization saturation (15b), not a gap; if every pole and continuum threshold remains finite while $\chi(\mathbf q_c,0)$ diverges under matched elastic conventions, the approximation or continuation is internally inconsistent, by (15a). Claims of a central peak wait for an $\eta$-independent dissipative term or zero-threshold continuum.
