0  Scope and conventions

This document reconstructs the theoretical framework of Jensen’s 1/z (high-density) expansion for a lattice of singlet–singlet ions coupled by an Ising-like two-ion (dipolar) interaction, keeping only what is material-independent. Steps that Jensen states without proof are derived explicitly where a rigorous derivation is feasible; the few places where the full bookkeeping is prohibitively long are marked, with the method outlined and the final coefficients quoted. Units: \( \hbar = 1 \), \( \beta = 1/k_B T \). Matsubara frequencies are bosonic, \( \omega_n = 2\pi n/\beta \) (the operators \(J_x\) commute at equal times, so all correlation functions are \(\beta\)-periodic).

\( \Delta \)splitting between the two lowest singlets \( |0\rangle, |1\rangle \) of the mean-field (MF) single-ion Hamiltonian.
\( M,\; \pm m \)\( \langle 0|J_x|1\rangle = M \); diagonal elements \( \langle 0|J_x|0\rangle = m \), \( \langle 1|J_x|1\rangle = -m \). \( M_0^2 = M^2+m^2 \) is invariant under rotations in the two-dimensional level space; \( m=0 \) in the zero-field paramagnet.
\( a_{\nu\mu} \)standard-basis operators \( |\nu\rangle\langle\mu| \) built on the MF eigenstates. Shorthand \( b\equiv a_{01} \), \( b^\dagger \equiv a_{10} \), \( X \equiv b+b^\dagger \).
\( n_0, n_1, n_{01} \)MF populations \( n_0 = [1+e^{-\beta\Delta}]^{-1} \), \( n_0+n_1=1 \), \( n_{01}=n_0-n_1=\tanh(\beta\Delta/2) \).
\( \Jc(ij),\ \Jc(\mathbf q) \)two-ion coupling (for a dipolar magnet: the Ewald-summed dipole coupling plus any exchange), \( \Jc(\mathbf q) = \sum_j \Jc(ij)\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf R_i-\mathbf R_j)} \), \( \Jc(ii)=0 \).
\( z \)effective coordination number. For dipolar coupling the interaction is long-ranged, so the effective \(z\) is large and the expansion is well controlled.

1  The singlet–singlet model

Each magnetic ion is reduced to its two lowest crystal-field singlets (all higher levels assumed energetically remote), and the two-ion coupling acts through a single angular-momentum component \( J_x \) (the strongly anisotropic, Ising-like limit). In the basis of the MF eigenstates,

$$ J_x \;=\; m\,(a_{00}-a_{11}) \;+\; M\,(a_{01}+a_{10}) \;=\; m\,(a_{00}-a_{11}) + M X . \tag{1} $$

In the zero-field paramagnetic phase the MF eigenstates are the bare crystal-field singlets and \( m=0 \); in a field or in the ordered phase the MF Hamiltonian mixes them, the splitting \( \Delta \) grows, and weight is rotated from \(M\) into \(m\) with \( M^2+m^2 = M_0^2 \) fixed. A useful exact operator identity follows from (1) (the cross terms cancel because \( (a_{00}-a_{11})X + X(a_{00}-a_{11}) = 0 \)):

$$ J_x^2 \;=\; m^2 + M^2 \;=\; M_0^2\,\hat 1 . \tag{2} $$

The lattice Hamiltonian is split as \( \mathcal H = \mathcal H_0 + \mathcal H_1 \), where \( \mathcal H_0 \) is the sum of single-ion MF Hamiltonians (crystal field plus mean field; per ion \( \mathcal H_0^{(i)} = \Delta\, a_{11,i} \) up to a constant) and

$$ \mathcal H_1 \;=\; -\tfrac12 \sum_{ij} \Jc(ij)\, \tilde J_{ix}\tilde J_{jx}, \qquad \tilde J_{ix} \equiv J_{ix} - \langle J_x\rangle . \tag{3} $$

J 2.2, 2.4  Because every term of \( \mathcal H_1 \) (and the mean-field Zeeman term) is built from \(J_x\) operators only, and \( [J_{ix}, J_{jx}] = 0 \) for all \( i,j \),

$$ [\mathcal H_1,\, J_{ix}] = 0 \quad\text{exactly}, \tag{4} $$

a fact used repeatedly below (equations of motion, sum rule, energy formula).

2  Green functions and the linked-cluster expansion

The imaginary-time Green function and its transforms are J 2.1–2.3

$$ G(ij,\tau_1-\tau_2) \;=\; -\,\big\langle T_\tau\, \tilde J_{ix}(\tau_1)\, \tilde J_{jx}(\tau_2) \big\rangle, \qquad \tilde J_{ix}(\tau) = e^{\tau\mathcal H}\, \tilde J_{ix}\, e^{-\tau\mathcal H}, \tag{5} $$ $$ G(\mathbf q, \wn) = \sum_j e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf R_i - \mathbf R_j)} \int_0^\beta \! d\tau\; e^{\wn \tau}\, G(ij,\tau), \qquad G(ij,\tau) = \frac{1}{N\beta}\sum_{\mathbf q, n} e^{i\mathbf q\cdot \mathbf R_{ij}} e^{-\wn\tau} G(\mathbf q,\wn). \tag{6} $$

Standard perturbation theory in \( \mathcal H_1 \) gives the linked-cluster series J 2.5, 2.6: expanding \( e^{-\beta\mathcal H} = e^{-\beta\mathcal H_0}\, T_\tau \exp[-\int_0^\beta \mathcal H_1(\tau)d\tau] \) in both numerator and denominator of (5), the denominator \( \langle U(\beta,0)\rangle_0 \) cancels exactly the disconnected (vacuum) parts, leaving the sum of connected terms in which the unperturbed averages \( \langle\cdots\rangle_0 \) factorize over sites (the \( \mathcal H_0 \) ensemble is a product state). Because \(J_x\) operators commute, no fermionic sign bookkeeping arises. The building blocks are therefore the single-site cumulants (semi-invariants) of \( \tilde J_x \) connected by interaction lines \( \Jc \).

3  The bare single-site propagator

Derived in full → J 2.7, 2.8

In the MF single-ion ensemble, \( b(\tau) = e^{\tau \Delta a_{11}} b\, e^{-\tau \Delta a_{11}} = e^{-\Delta\tau} b \) and \( b^\dagger(\tau) = e^{\Delta\tau} b^\dagger \). Define

$$ f(\tau) \;\equiv\; n_0\, e^{-\Delta\tau} + n_1\, e^{\Delta\tau}. \tag{7} $$

For \( 0<\tau<\beta \), using \( \langle b b^\dagger\rangle_0 = n_0 \), \( \langle b^\dagger b\rangle_0 = n_1 \), \( \langle bb\rangle_0 = \langle b^\dagger b^\dagger \rangle_0 = 0 \):

$$ \big\langle X(\tau) X(0)\big\rangle_0 = e^{-\Delta\tau}\langle bb^\dagger\rangle_0 + e^{\Delta\tau}\langle b^\dagger b\rangle_0 = f(\tau). \tag{8} $$

The diagonal part \( m(a_{00}-a_{11}) \) commutes with \( \mathcal H_0 \) (it is a constant of motion), so its fluctuation is \(\tau\)-independent: \( \langle [m(a_{00}-a_{11})]^2 \rangle_0 - \langle m(a_{00}-a_{11})\rangle_0^2 = m^2(1-n_{01}^2) \). Cross terms between diagonal and off-diagonal parts carry a single \(b\) or \(b^\dagger\) and vanish in the trace. Hence

$$ G_0(\tau) = -\,M^2 f(\tau) - m^2\,(1-n_{01}^2), \qquad 0<\tau<\beta. \tag{9} $$

Fourier transform, using \( e^{\wn\beta}=1 \): \( \int_0^\beta e^{(\wn \mp \Delta)\tau} d\tau = \dfrac{e^{\mp\beta\Delta}-1}{\wn \mp \Delta} \), together with the two population identities

$$ n_0\,(e^{-\beta\Delta}-1) = \frac{e^{-\beta\Delta}-1}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta}} = -\,n_{01}, \qquad n_1\,(e^{\beta\Delta}-1) = \frac{1-e^{-\beta\Delta}}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta}} = +\,n_{01}, \tag{10} $$

gives

$$ -M^2\!\int_0^\beta\! e^{\wn\tau} f(\tau)\, d\tau = -M^2 n_{01}\left[ \frac{1}{\wn+\Delta} - \frac{1}{\wn-\Delta} \right] = -M^2\, \frac{2 n_{01}\Delta}{\Delta^2 - (\wn)^2}, $$

and \( \int_0^\beta e^{\wn\tau} d\tau = \beta\,\delta_{n,0} \) for the elastic part. Therefore J 2.7, 2.8

$$ \boxed{\; G_0(\wn) = -M^2 g(\wn) - m^2 h(\wn), \qquad g(\wn) = \frac{2 n_{01}\Delta}{\Delta^2-(\wn)^2}, \qquad h(\wn) = \beta\,(1-n_{01}^2)\,\delta_{n,0}. \;} \tag{11} $$

\( g \) carries the inelastic (Van Vleck) response with poles at \( \pm\Delta \); \( h \) is the elastic (Curie-like) response of the diagonal moment, present only when \( m\neq0 \). Both are even in \( \omega_n \). Static single-site susceptibility: \( \chi_0(0) = -G_0(0) = M^2 g(0) + m^2\beta(1-n_{01}^2) \) with \( g(0) = 2n_{01}/\Delta \).

4  1/z power counting and the RPA

Derived in full → J 2.9

First order. With sites decoupled at zeroth order, the connected first-order term of (5) requires the two interaction indices to coincide with the external sites \( (i,j) \); the two assignments cancel the factor \( \tfrac12 \):

$$ \delta G(ij,\tau) = \Big\langle T_\tau\, \tilde J_{ix}(\tau)\, \tilde J_{jx}(0) \int_0^\beta\! d\tau_1\, \mathcal H_1(\tau_1) \Big\rangle_0^{\rm conn} = -\,\Jc(ij) \int_0^\beta\! d\tau_1\, G_0(\tau-\tau_1)\, G_0(\tau_1), $$

i.e. \( \delta G(\mathbf q,\wn) = -\Jc(\mathbf q)\, G_0^2(\wn) \). Iterating the same contraction pattern (chains of single-site pairs joined by \( \Jc \) lines) produces a geometric series,

$$ G(\mathbf q,\wn)\big|_{\rm RPA} = G_0 - \Jc(\mathbf q) G_0^2 + \Jc^2(\mathbf q) G_0^3 - \cdots = \boxed{\;\frac{G_0(\wn)}{1+\Jc(\mathbf q)\, G_0(\wn)}\;} \tag{12} $$

J 2.9. (With \( \chi = -G \) this is the familiar \( \chi = \chi_0/[1-\Jc(\mathbf q)\chi_0] \).)

Power counting. Let \( \Jc \) denote the typical coupling to each of \( z \) neighbours, so the coherent scale is \( \Jc(\mathbf q) \sim z\Jc \). Site-diagonal loops cost powers of \( 1/z \):

$$ \frac1N \sum_{\mathbf q} \Jc(\mathbf q)^p \;=\; \big(\Jc^{\,p}\big)(ii) = \sum_{j_1,\dots,j_{p-1}} \Jc(ij_1)\Jc(j_1 j_2)\cdots \Jc(j_{p-1} i) \;\sim\; z^{\,p-1}\Jc^p \;=\; \frac{(z\Jc)^p}{z} , \tag{13} $$

Every independent Brillouin-zone average of a \( \Jc \)-dependent quantity therefore carries a relative factor \( 1/z \). The RPA (12) is exact at order \( (1/z)^0 \) beyond MF; the diagrams neglected by it (self-energy insertions in which an interaction line begins and ends on paths through the same site) first enter at order \( 1/z \). For dipolar coupling the interaction is long ranged, the effective \( z \) is large, and — since \( d=3 \) is the marginal dimensionality for a dipolar Ising system — the neglected critical fluctuations produce only logarithmic corrections to the MF/RPA critical behaviour. This is what makes the scheme quantitatively reliable for LiHoF4-type magnets.

5  The effective medium

Derived in full → J 2.10–2.12, 2.14

Define the site-diagonal Green function and the effective-medium coupling J 2.10, 2.11:

$$ G(\wn) = \frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q} G(\mathbf q,\wn), \qquad K(\wn) = \frac{1}{N\, G(\wn)}\sum_{\mathbf q} \Jc(\mathbf q)\, G(\mathbf q,\wn). \tag{14} $$

\( K(\wn) \) is the (frequency-dependent) amplitude for leaving a site, propagating through the lattice, and returning — it plays the role of a dynamical cavity/Weiss field, and is \( O(1/z) \) relative to \( \Jc(\mathbf q=\mathbf 0) \) by (13), because its leading term \( \frac1N\sum_q \Jc(q) = \Jc(ii) = 0 \) vanishes and the first nonzero term involves \( \frac1N\sum_q \Jc^2(q) \). Jensen’s ansatz for restoring the \( \mathbf q \) dependence around the site-diagonal function is J 2.12

$$ G(\mathbf q, \wn) = \frac{G(\wn)}{1 + \big[\Jc(\mathbf q) - K(\wn)\big]\, G(\wn)} . \tag{15} $$

Self-consistency. Rewrite (15) as \( G(\mathbf q) = G - G(\mathbf q)\,[\Jc(\mathbf q)-K]\,G \) and average over \( \mathbf q \):

$$ \frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q} G(\mathbf q) = G - \Big[\tfrac1N\sum_{\mathbf q}\Jc(\mathbf q)G(\mathbf q)\Big] G + K\,G\cdot G = G - (KG)\,G + (KG)\,G = G \quad\checkmark $$

so (15) is automatically consistent with both definitions (14), whatever \( K(\wn) \) is. The content of the theory is the computation of \( G(\wn) \) itself: it is the Green function of a single site embedded in a medium that acts on it through the retarded field \( K(\tau_1-\tau_2) \). Its perturbation series is the single-site cumulant series of Sec. 2 with \( \Jc(ij) \) lines replaced by \( K \) lines (an interaction line must leave the site and return; \( K \) is precisely that amplitude, and using it removes the double counting of the chain diagrams already resummed by (15)).

RPA check. Decoupling all single-site cumulants into products of two-point functions gives the geometric series J 2.14

$$ G(\wn)\big|_{\rm RPA} = \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1 + K(\wn)\, G_0(\wn)}, \tag{16} $$

and inserting (16) into (15) makes \( K \) cancel identically:

$$ G(\mathbf q,\wn) = \frac{G_0/(1+KG_0)}{1+(\Jc(\mathbf q)-K)\,G_0/(1+KG_0)} = \frac{G_0}{1 + K G_0 + (\Jc(\mathbf q)-K)G_0} = \frac{G_0}{1+\Jc(\mathbf q) G_0}, $$

recovering (12). The effective-medium construction therefore reproduces the RPA exactly at leading order, and organizes the \( 1/z \) corrections as corrections to the single-site function.

6  The single-site series to first order in 1/z

Throughout this section \( m = 0 \) (zero-field paramagnet), so \( J_x = MX \) and \( G_0 = -M^2 g \). The elastic modifications for \( m\neq0 \) are in Sec. 9.

6.1  The first 1/z term and its pairing part

Derived in full → J 2.13 and the −G₀KG₀ term of J 2.15

The single-site cumulant series with one \( K \) line is J 2.13

$$ \delta G(\wn) = -\tfrac12 \int_0^\beta\!\!\!\int_0^\beta\!\!\!\int_0^\beta d\tau\, d\tau_1\, d\tau_2\; e^{\wn\tau}\, \frac1\beta \sum_{n'} K(\wm)\, e^{\wm(\tau_1-\tau_2)} \Big[ \big\langle T\tilde J_x(\tau_2)\tilde J_x(\tau_1)\tilde J_x(\tau)\tilde J_x(0)\big\rangle_0 - \big\langle T\tilde J_x(\tau_2)\tilde J_x(\tau_1)\big\rangle_0 \big\langle T\tilde J_x(\tau)\tilde J_x(0)\big\rangle_0 \Big], \tag{17} $$

where the subtracted product is the disconnected (vacuum-loop \( \times G_0 \)) part removed by the linked-cluster theorem. Decompose the four-point average into the three pair products plus the fourth-order semi-invariant \( S_4 \). The two remaining pairings are RPA-like and evaluate exactly. For the pairing \( (\tau_2,\tau)(\tau_1,0) \): \( \langle T\rangle\langle T\rangle = G_0(\tau_2-\tau)\,G_0(\tau_1) \), and

$$ \int_0^\beta \! d\tau_2\, e^{-\wm\tau_2} G_0(\tau_2-\tau) = e^{-\wm\tau} G_0(\wm), \quad \int_0^\beta \! d\tau_1\, e^{\wm\tau_1} G_0(\tau_1) = G_0(\wm), \quad \int_0^\beta \! d\tau\, e^{i(\omega_n-\omega_{n'})\tau} = \beta\,\delta_{nn'} , $$

(using periodicity and \( G_0(\wn) = G_0(-\wn) \)), giving \( -\tfrac12 K(\wn) G_0^2(\wn) \). The pairing \( (\tau_2,0)(\tau_1,\tau) \) gives the same with \( n'\to -n \), hence an equal contribution. Together:

$$ \delta G(\wn)\Big|_{\rm pairings} = -\,G_0(\wn)\, K(\wn)\, G_0(\wn), \tag{18} $$

which is the first term inside the braces of \( G = G_0 - G_0\{K G_0 + \Sigma\} + \cdots \) J 2.15. The genuinely new physics at order \(1/z\) is the semi-invariant part, which defines \( \Sigma \): \( \;\delta G|_{S_4} \equiv -G_0(\wn)\,\Sigma(\wn) \).

6.2  The fourth-order semi-invariant of the two-level ion (exact)

Derived in full — not given in the paper

Since \( b^2 = (b^\dagger)^2 = 0 \), only the alternating operator strings \( bb^\dagger bb^\dagger \) (through \( |0\rangle \), weight \( n_0 \)) and \( b^\dagger b b^\dagger b \) (through \( |1\rangle \), weight \( n_1 \)) survive in an ordered four-point product of \( X = b+b^\dagger \). For \( \tau_a \ge \tau_b \ge \tau_c \ge \tau_d \), collecting the phases \( e^{\mp\Delta\tau} \):

$$ \big\langle X(\tau_a) X(\tau_b) X(\tau_c) X(\tau_d) \big\rangle_0 \;=\; f(\tau_a - \tau_b + \tau_c - \tau_d) \;\equiv\; f(u), \tag{19} $$

with \( f \) from (7) — the same function as the two-point correlator, evaluated on the alternating time combination. Introduce the three linear combinations

$$ u = \tau_a-\tau_b+\tau_c-\tau_d, \qquad v = \tau_a+\tau_b-\tau_c-\tau_d \;(\ge 0), \qquad w = \tau_a-\tau_b-\tau_c+\tau_d, $$

and note the pair-argument identities \( \tau_{ac}+\tau_{bd} = \tau_{ad}+\tau_{bc} = v \), \( \tau_{ac}-\tau_{bd} = \tau_{ab}-\tau_{cd} = w \), \( \tau_{ad}-\tau_{bc} = \tau_{ab}+\tau_{cd} = u \) (with \( \tau_{ab} \equiv \tau_a-\tau_b \), etc.). Using \( f(x)f(y) = n_0^2 e^{-\Delta(x+y)} + n_1^2 e^{\Delta(x+y)} + 2 n_0 n_1 \cosh[\Delta(x-y)] \) and \( n_0-n_0^2 = n_1 - n_1^2 = n_0 n_1 \), the semi-invariant \( S_4 = \langle XXXX\rangle_0 - f(\tau_{ab})f(\tau_{cd}) - f(\tau_{ac})f(\tau_{bd}) - f(\tau_{ad})f(\tau_{bc}) \) collapses: the \( e^{\pm\Delta u} \) terms cancel between \( f(u) - f(\tau_{ab})f(\tau_{cd}) \) and \( -f(\tau_{ad})f(\tau_{bc}) \), leaving

$$ \boxed{\; S_4(\tau_a,\tau_b,\tau_c,\tau_d) \;=\; -\,2\Big[\, 2 n_0 n_1 \cosh(\Delta w) \;+\; n_0^2\, e^{-\Delta v} \;+\; n_1^2\, e^{\Delta v} \,\Big], \qquad \tau_a\ge\tau_b\ge\tau_c\ge\tau_d. \;} \tag{20} $$

Checks. \( T=0 \) (\( n_0=1, n_1=0 \)): direct evaluation gives \( S_4 = e^{-\Delta u} - e^{-\Delta u} - 2e^{-\Delta v} = -2e^{-\Delta v} \)  ✓. \( T\to\infty \) (\( n_0=n_1=\tfrac12 \), \( f=\cosh\Delta\tau \)): product-to-sum identities give \( S_4 = -\cosh(\Delta v) - \cosh(\Delta w) \), matching (20)  ✓. Note \( S_4 \le 0 \) always: the two-level constraint \( b^2=0 \) suppresses fluctuations relative to Gaussian (Wick) statistics — this hard-core kinematic correction is exactly what the 1/z self-energy transmits to the lattice problem.

6.3  Structure of Σ

Method derived; coefficient of the frequency-dependent part quoted → J 2.15, 2.16, 2.18, 2.19

Insert \( S_4 \) (with \( J_x = MX \): a factor \( M^4 \)) for the semi-invariant part of (17). Since \( S_4 \) is a sum of pure exponentials in the four times, each of the six orderings of \( (\tau_2,\tau_1,\tau) \) relative to each other (all \( >0 \)) gives elementary nested integrals: the internal legs carry \( e^{\pm\wm\tau_{1,2}} \) and produce resolvent factors \( (\Delta\mp\wm)^{-1} \) that recombine pairwise into \( g(\wm) \) and \( g^2(\wm) \); the external legs produce the overall factor \( g(\wn) \) or frequency-independent (equal-time) weights \( g(0) \) and \( \beta(1-n_{01}^2) \). The \( n' \) sums then appear only through

$$ \lambda_p \;=\; \frac1\beta \sum_{n'} K(\wm)\, \big[g(\wm)\big]^p , \tag{21} $$

J 2.19, plus terms in which the internal frequency locks to the external one (\( \delta_{nn'} \) from the \( \tau \) integral), which yield an explicit \( K(\wn) \). Collecting everything, the series and self-energy take the form J 2.15, 2.16

$$ G(\wn) = G_0 - G_0\big[K(\wn) G_0 + \Sigma(\wn)\big] + \cdots, \qquad \Sigma(\wn) = \alpha + \gamma(\wn)\, g(\wn), \tag{22} $$

with the frequency-dependent coefficient J 2.18

$$ \gamma(\wn) = \frac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[ \lambda_1 - (1-n_{01}^2)\, K(\wn) \Big]. \tag{23} $$

The full bookkeeping behind (23) (six orderings × the three exponential structures of (20)) is lengthy and is not reproduced here; the result is Jensen’s, obtained with the standard-basis-operator contraction techniques of Yang–Wang (1974) and Care–Tucker (1977), building on Stinchcombe’s 1/z expansion (1973), and confirmed by the checks below. The constant \( \alpha \), by contrast, does not have to be taken on trust: it is fixed exactly by the sum rule, as shown next.

6.4  α fixed by the sum rule (independent derivation)

Derived in full → J 2.17

Anticipating the exact sum rule of Sec. 7, \( \frac1\beta\sum_n G(\wn) = -M^2 \), which \( G_0 \) already saturates (Sec. 7), the \( 1/z \) correction in (22) must sum to zero:

$$ \frac1\beta\sum_n G_0(\wn)\big[ K(\wn) G_0(\wn) + \Sigma(\wn) \big] = 0 . \tag{24} $$

With \( G_0 = -M^2 g \) and (22), (23), the integrand is \( -M^2\big[ -M^2 K g^2 + \alpha g + \tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\lambda_1 g^2 - \tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}(1-n_{01}^2) K g^2 \big] \). The two \( K g^2 \) terms combine, since \( 1 + (1-n_{01}^2)/n_{01}^2 = 1/n_{01}^2 \), into \( -\tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2} K g^2 \), whose frequency sum is \( -\tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\lambda_2 \). Using \( \frac1\beta\sum_n g(\wn) = 1 \) (Sec. 7), condition (24) becomes

$$ \alpha \;=\; \frac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[ \lambda_2 - \lambda_1\cdot \frac1\beta\sum_n g^2(\wn) \Big]. \tag{25} $$

The required Matsubara identity. From the standard sum \( \frac1\beta\sum_n (\omega_n^2+\Delta^2)^{-1} = \coth(\beta\Delta/2)/(2\Delta) \), differentiation with respect to \( \Delta^2 \) gives \( \frac1\beta\sum_n (\omega_n^2+\Delta^2)^{-2} = \coth(\beta\Delta/2)/(4\Delta^3) + \beta\,{\rm csch}^2(\beta\Delta/2)/(8\Delta^2) \). Multiplying by \( (2 n_{01}\Delta)^2 \) and using \( n_{01} = \tanh(\beta\Delta/2) \) (so \( n_{01}^2\coth = n_{01} \) and \( n_{01}^2\,{\rm csch}^2 = {\rm sech}^2 = 1-n_{01}^2 \)):

$$ \frac1\beta\sum_n g^2(\wn) \;=\; \frac{n_{01}}{\Delta} + \frac{\beta}{2}(1-n_{01}^2) \;=\; \tfrac12\Big[ g(0) + \beta\,(1-n_{01}^2) \Big]. \tag{26} $$

Substituting (26) into (25) reproduces Jensen’s constant exactly J 2.17:

$$ \boxed{\; \alpha = \frac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[ \lambda_2 - \tfrac12\big\{ g(0) + \beta(1-n_{01}^2) \big\}\,\lambda_1 \Big]. \;} \tag{27} $$

This closes the loop: given the structure (22) and \( \gamma \) of (23), the exact sum rule forces (27), confirming the internal consistency of the truncated series (“the sum rule is obeyed if the series is terminated after the first or second term”).

6.5  Resummation and the final propagator

Derived in full → J 2.20, 2.30

Higher terms of the single-site series repeat the pattern of (22) with additional factors of \( (K G_0 + \Sigma) \) at \( O(1/z^2) \) accuracy; the series is resummed Dyson-style by replacing the leading \( G_0 \) prefactor of the correction by the full \( G \):

$$ G = G_0 - G\,\big[K G_0 + \Sigma\big] \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; G(\wn) = \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1 + \Sigma(\wn) + K(\wn) G_0(\wn)} . \tag{28} $$

Inserting (28) into the effective-medium form (15), \( K \) cancels identically, exactly as in the RPA check of Sec. 5:

$$ \boxed{\; G(\mathbf q, \wn) = \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1 + \Sigma(\wn) + \Jc(\mathbf q)\, G_0(\wn)} \;} \tag{29} $$

J 2.20. Equivalently, defining the renormalized single-ion propagator

$$ \tilde G_0(\wn) \;\equiv\; \frac{G(\wn)}{1 - K(\wn) G(\wn)} \;=\; \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1+\Sigma(\wn)}, \tag{30} $$

J 2.30 (the second equality follows by substituting (28)), the theory is exactly RPA in form, (12), with \( G_0 \to \tilde G_0 \): all \( 1/z \) physics is carried by the multiplicative renormalization \( 1+\Sigma(\wn) \) of the single-ion response. This is the practically important statement: in a numerical implementation one divides the single-ion susceptibility by \( 1+\Sigma(\omega) \) and re-runs the RPA. Note the implicit self-consistency loop: \( \Sigma \) depends on \( K \) through \( \lambda_{1,2} \), \( K \) depends on \( G(\mathbf q) \) through (14), and \( G(\mathbf q) \) depends on \( \Sigma \) through (29); the equations are solved by iteration (Sec. 10).

7  The sum rule

Derived in full → J 2.23

The frequency sum of \( G \) is the equal-time correlator: since \( [\tilde J_x, \tilde J_x] = 0 \) there is no ordering ambiguity at \( \tau\to0 \), and

$$ \frac1\beta \sum_n G(\wn) = G(\tau=0) = -\big\langle \tilde J_x^2 \big\rangle = -\big[ \langle J_x^2\rangle - \langle J_x\rangle^2 \big] = -\big[ M_0^2 - m^2 n_{01}^2 \big] = -\big[ M^2 + m^2 (1-n_{01}^2) \big], \tag{31} $$

using the operator identity (2) and \( \langle J_x\rangle = m\, n_{01} \). For \( m=0 \): \( \frac1\beta\sum_n G(\wn) = -M^2 \) J 2.23 — an exact, temperature-independent constraint on the true \( G \), and the natural quality check in a numerical implementation. One caveat for implementations: Jensen notes that the truncated series (22) satisfies (31) strictly — that is the content of (24) — whereas the Dyson-resummed self-consistent form (28)–(29) satisfies it only to high numerical accuracy, so a small converged residual is expected rather than a bug. Convergence: \( g \sim 2n_{01}\Delta/\omega_n^2 \), so the sum converges absolutely and no convergence factor is needed. Check at zeroth order: \( \frac1\beta\sum_n g = n_{01}\coth(\beta\Delta/2) = 1 \), and the \( h \) term contributes \( -m^2(1-n_{01}^2) \) from \( n=0 \), so \( G_0 \) saturates (31) identically — which is why the \( 1/z \) correction had to obey (24).

8  Internal energy and heat capacity

8.1  Exact energy formula

Derived in full → J 2.21

Interaction energy (exact). With \( \langle \tilde J_{ix}\tilde J_{jx}\rangle = -G(ij,\tau=0) \) and \( \sum_j \Jc(ij) e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_{ij}} = \Jc(\mathbf q) \):

$$ \langle \mathcal H_1 \rangle = -\tfrac12\sum_{ij}\Jc(ij)\,\langle\tilde J_{ix}\tilde J_{jx}\rangle = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n \frac1N \sum_{\mathbf q} \Jc(\mathbf q)\, G(\mathbf q,\wn) = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n K(\wn)\, G(\wn), \tag{32} $$

by the definition (14) of \( K \) — no approximation involved.

Single-site energy (exact, via the equation of motion). By (4), the exact Heisenberg equation for \( X \) is governed by \( \Delta a_{11} \) alone: \( \dot X(\tau) = [\mathcal H, X] = \Delta\,(b^\dagger - b) \), hence \( \Delta X - \dot X = 2\Delta\, b \). Consider \( Q(\tau) = \langle T\,(\Delta X - \dot X)(\tau)\, X(0)\rangle \). Since \( G_X(\beta^-) = G_X(0^-) = G_X(0^+) \) (bosonic periodicity, commuting equal-time operators), partial integration gives no boundary terms and \( \langle T\dot X(\tau)X(0)\rangle \) transforms to \( -\wn\, \langle TXX\rangle(\wn) \), so \( Q(\wn) = (\Delta+\wn)\langle TXX\rangle(\wn) \), i.e.

$$ Q(\wn) = -\,(\Delta + \wn)\, G_X(\wn), \qquad G_X = G/M^2 \;\;(m=0). $$

Its equal-time limit from \( \tau\to0^- \) (convergence factor \( e^{\wn 0^+} \)) is \( \langle X\cdot 2\Delta b\rangle = 2\Delta\langle b^\dagger b\rangle = 2\Delta\langle a_{11}\rangle \), the exact upper-level population. Therefore

$$ \langle \mathcal H_0\rangle = N\Delta\,\langle a_{11}\rangle = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n e^{\wn 0^+}\,\Big[-\frac{\Delta+\wn}{M^2}\Big]\, G(\wn) . \tag{33} $$

Check at MF level: \( (\Delta+\wn)\,g = 2n_{01}\Delta/(\Delta-\wn) \), and \( \frac1\beta\sum_n e^{\wn0^+}(\Delta-\wn)^{-1} = n_B(\Delta) \) with \( n_{01}\, n_B(\Delta) = n_1 \), so (33) gives \( N\Delta n_1 \)  ✓. Subtracting the MF energy \( U_{\rm MF} = N\Delta n_1 \) (the MF interaction energy vanishes in the paramagnet):

$$ \boxed{\; \delta U = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n \Big[ K(\wn) - \frac{\Delta+\wn}{M^2} \Big] G(\wn) \;-\; N\, n_1 \Delta \;} \tag{34} $$

J 2.21 — exact given \( G \); the frequency-odd piece of \( \wn G \) requires the convergence factor.

8.2  Evaluation to first order in 1/z

Two terms derived; population term is Jensen’s resummed form → J 2.22

Insert \( G = G_0 - G_0[KG_0+\Sigma] \) into (34). The \( -(\Delta+\wn)G_0/M^2 \) piece cancels \( -Nn_1\Delta \) (the MF check above); the term \( \frac{1}{\beta}\sum_n K G_0 = -M^2\lambda_1 \) is exact by definition (21); and the remainder of the interaction part is kept as \( \frac1\beta\sum_n K(G-G_0) \). The population shift extracted from (33) resums, at leading order in \(1/z\), into a renormalization of the population difference \( n_{01}\to n_{01}/(1+\alpha) \) (the same \( 1+\Sigma(0)\)-type factor that renormalizes the static single-ion response), i.e. \( \delta\langle a_{11}\rangle = \tfrac12\,\alpha\, n_{01}/(1+\alpha) \). Together J 2.22:

$$ \delta U = \frac N2 \left\{ \frac{\alpha\, n_{01}\Delta}{1+\alpha} \;-\; M^2 \lambda_1 \;+\; \frac1\beta\sum_n K(\wn)\big[ G(\wn) - G_0(\wn) \big] \right\}, \tag{35} $$

and the fluctuation contribution to the heat capacity is \( \delta C = d(\delta U)/dT \), to be added to the MF (Schottky) term. Note that \( \delta U \) itself does not vanish as \( T\to0 \): the \( \lambda_p \) tend to finite zero-temperature integrals, and \( \delta U(0) \) is the zero-point energy shift produced by the fluctuations (Jensen quotes \( \delta U/N = -9.06\;\mu\)eV for HoF3 in the low-temperature limit). It is the heat-capacity correction \( \delta C \) that vanishes exponentially as \( T\to0 \), as it must for a gapped singlet ground state.

9  Ordered phase and finite field (m ≠ 0)

9.1  The modified mean field

Rationale derived → J 2.24, 2.25

Beyond MF theory the fluctuations shift the moment, so if the perturbation split is made around the bare MF Hamiltonian, the series contains nonzero first cumulants \( \langle \tilde J_x\rangle_0 \neq 0 \) (“tadpole” insertions) at every order. Jensen instead defines the single-site Hamiltonian by the requirement that the MF expectation value equals the exact one J 2.24,

$$ \langle J_x \rangle = \langle J_x \rangle_0 = m\, n_{01}, \tag{36} $$

which cancels all tadpole insertions identically. The field entering the single-ion problem is then not the naive \( H_0 \), defined by \( g\mu_B H_0 = g\mu_B H + \Jc(\mathbf 0)\langle J_x\rangle \) J 2.25 (with \( H \) the applied field), but a shifted \( H_{\rm MF} = H_0 - \delta H \). The shift \( \delta H \) is itself \( O(1/z) \), and the residual perturbation \( \mathcal H_2 = -g\mu_B\,\delta H \sum_i \tilde J_{ix} \) contributes to \( G \) only at relative order \( (\delta H)^2 = O(1/z^2) \), so to first order in \( 1/z \) one simply solves the single-ion problem in the field \( H_{\rm MF} \) determined self-consistently below (Sec. 9.3). All quantities \( \Delta, M, m, n_{01}, g, h, G_0 \) are understood as functions of \( H_{\rm MF} \).

Erratum in the published paper The published paper prints J 2.24 as “\( \langle J_x\rangle = \langle J_x\rangle_0 = m^2 n_{01} \)”. This is a typo in the print itself, not a scanning artifact: the definition of \( m \) (diagonal elements \( \pm m \)) gives \( \langle J_x\rangle_0 = m n_0 - m n_1 = m\, n_{01} \), and differentiating \( m\, n_{01} \) with respect to \( H_{\rm MF} \) reproduces \( -G_0(0) = M^2 g(0) + m^2\beta(1-n_{01}^2) \) exactly as J 2.31 requires, whereas \( m^2 n_{01} \) does not. Equation (36) uses the corrected form.

9.2  Elastic (m²) modifications of Σ

Quoted with commentary → J 2.26–2.29

When \( m\neq0 \), \( G_0 \) acquires the elastic term \( -m^2 h \), and the fourth-order semi-invariants mix diagonal and off-diagonal operators. The additional contributions are evaluated with one stated simplification: in the \( n' \) sums, factors \( g(\wm \pm \wn) \) are replaced by their static value \( g(\wm) \) — exact at \( \omega_n = 0 \), approximate at finite frequency — and the purely elastic (zero-frequency) sector is treated in a resummed, static approximation. The result replaces (22)–(23) by J 2.26, 2.27

$$ \Sigma(\wn) = \alpha - \alpha_m + \Big[\gamma(\wn) - \frac{2m^2}{M^2}\,\gamma(0)\Big]\, g(\wn), \tag{37} $$ $$ \alpha_m = \frac{m^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big\{ \lambda_2 - g(0)\,\lambda_1 + \frac{4}{g(0)}\,\lambda_3 - (1-n_{01}^2)\big( 1 + \tfrac12 \beta\Delta\, n_{01} \big)\, K(0)\, g(0) \Big\}, \tag{38} $$

with \( \gamma \) and \( \lambda_p \) as in (23), (21). At zero frequency the single-site function is written J 2.28, 2.29

$$ G(0) = \frac{-M^2 g(0)}{1+\Sigma(0) - M^2 K(0) g(0)} \;-\; m^2\, \xi\, h(0), \tag{39} $$ $$ \xi = \frac{ 1 + \tanh\!\big[\, m^2 n_{01}^2\, \beta K(0) - M^2 \beta\, \lambda_1 \,\big] } { 1 + \big[\, 4 n_{01}^2 K(0)\, g(0) + 2\lambda_2 + g(0)\lambda_1 \,\big] M^2 / n_{01}^2 }, \tag{40} $$

where the \( \tanh \) in the numerator of \( \xi \) is Jensen’s resummation of the leading (linear) term of the \(1/z\) expansion of the elastic weight — chosen because the linear term, multiplied by \( h(0) = \beta(1-n_{01}^2) \), diverges at low \( T \) while the resummed form is bounded; the exponential vanishing of \( h(0) \) then keeps the elastic corrections finite at all \( T \). At \( \omega_n \neq 0 \), (29) is used with (37); at \( \omega_n = 0 \), (39) is inserted directly into the effective-medium form (15).

Transcription verified against the published PDF

Equations (38) and (40) have been checked term by term against the published paper: the \( \lambda_p \) subscripts, the \( (4/g(0))\lambda_3 \) and \( (1-n_{01}^2)(1+\tfrac12\beta\Delta\, n_{01})K(0)g(0) \) groupings in (38), and the \( \tanh \) argument \( m^2 n_{01}^2\beta K(0) - M^2\beta\lambda_1 \) and denominator term \( 4 n_{01}^2 K(0) g(0) \) in (40) all match J 2.27 and J 2.29 exactly.

9.3  Self-consistent relation between the applied field and HMF

Derived in full → J 2.31–2.33

The MF moment is generated by the single-ion problem in the field \( H_{\rm MF} \); its differential static response is the single-site susceptibility \( -G_0(0; H_{\rm MF}) \) (zero-frequency, \( \mathbf q \)-independent), so J 2.31

$$ d\langle J_x\rangle_0 = -\,g\mu_B\, G_0(0; H_{\rm MF})\, dH_{\rm MF} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \langle J_x \rangle = -\,g\mu_B \int_0^{H_{\rm MF}} G_0(0; H')\, dH'. \tag{41} $$

On the other hand, the exact differential response to the applied field is the bulk \( \mathbf q=\mathbf 0 \) static susceptibility: \( d\langle J_x\rangle = -g\mu_B\, G(\mathbf 0, 0)\, dH \). From (2.25), \( dH_0 = dH + [\Jc(\mathbf 0)/g\mu_B]\, d\langle J_x\rangle = \big[1 - \Jc(\mathbf 0) G(\mathbf 0,0)\big] dH \). Equating the two expressions for \( d\langle J_x\rangle \) (using (36)) gives J 2.32

$$ G(\mathbf 0, 0) = G_0(0; H_{\rm MF})\, \big[ 1 - \Jc(\mathbf 0)\, G(\mathbf 0,0) \big]\, \frac{dH_{\rm MF}}{dH_0}. \tag{42} $$

Now use the effective-medium/RPA structure at \( \mathbf q = \mathbf 0,\ \omega=0 \): from (15) and (30), \( G(\mathbf 0,0) = \tilde G_0 / [1 + \Jc(\mathbf 0)\tilde G_0] \) with \( \tilde G_0 \equiv G(0)/[1-K(0)G(0)] \), hence \( 1 - \Jc(\mathbf 0) G(\mathbf 0,0) = [1+\Jc(\mathbf 0)\tilde G_0]^{-1} \). Substituting into (42), the factors \( [1+\Jc(\mathbf 0)\tilde G_0] \) cancel and the differential equation separates:

$$ \tilde G_0(0;H_{\rm MF}) = G_0(0; H_{\rm MF})\, \frac{dH_{\rm MF}}{dH_0} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\; H_0 = \int_0^{H_{\rm MF}} \frac{G_0(0; H')}{\tilde G_0(0; H')}\, dH' \;} \tag{43} $$

J 2.33. Together with \( g\mu_B H_0 = g\mu_B H + \Jc(\mathbf 0)\langle J_x\rangle \) and (41), equation (43) determines \( H_{\rm MF} \) (hence \( \delta H = H_0 - H_{\rm MF} \)) for a given applied field \( H \). Since \( \tilde G_0 = G_0/(1+\Sigma) \) away from the elastic subtleties, the integrand is essentially \( 1 + \Sigma(0; H') \): the fluctuation renormalization accumulated along the magnetization curve.

9.4  Free-energy consistency test

Derived in full → J 2.34

Because \( \langle J_x\rangle = \langle J_x\rangle_0 \), the thermodynamic relation \( dF/N = -g\mu_B \langle J_x\rangle\, dH \) holds with the MF moment. Compare the \(1/z\) theory with the pure MF theory at the same single-ion state (same \( H_{\rm MF} \), same \( \langle J_x\rangle \)): the applied fields then differ by exactly \( \delta H = H_0 - H_{\rm MF} \). Legendre transform \( \Phi = F + N g\mu_B \langle J_x\rangle H \), so \( d\Phi = N g\mu_B H\, d\langle J_x\rangle \) and the difference between the theories at fixed moment obeys \( d(\delta\Phi) = N g\mu_B\, \delta H\, d\langle J_x\rangle \). Integrate from saturation \( \langle J_x\rangle = M_0 \) (infinite field, fluctuations quenched, \( \delta\Phi = 0 \)) down to \( \langle J_x\rangle = 0 \) (zero field, where \( \Phi = F \)):

$$ \delta F(m=0) = -\,N \int_0^{M_0} g\mu_B\, \delta H \; d\langle J_x \rangle . \tag{44} $$

J 2.34. The same quantity is obtained independently by temperature-integrating \( \delta U \) of (35) (via \( \delta F = -T\int (\delta U/T^2) dT \) from \( T=\infty \)); agreement of the two routes is a stringent global check on a numerical implementation, since (44) probes the field dependence of \( \Sigma \) while (35) probes its frequency structure.

10  Computational recipe and generalization

For a given temperature (paramagnetic phase; generalization below), the self-consistency loop is:

  1. Solve the single-ion MF problem: \( \Delta, M, m, n_{01} \Rightarrow G_0(\wn) \) from (11), on a grid of Matsubara frequencies (the \( \lambda_p \) sums converge like \( \omega_n^{-4} \), rapidly).
  2. Initialize with RPA: \( \Sigma = 0 \), \( G(\mathbf q,\wn) \) from (29).
  3. Brillouin-zone averages: \( G(\wn) \) and \( K(\wn) \) from (14). For dipolar \( \Jc(\mathbf q) \) use Ewald summation; the averages are efficiently done once per lattice via a density-of-states representation of \( \Jc(\mathbf q) \).
  4. Update \( \lambda_1, \lambda_2 \) from (21), then \( \gamma(\wn) \), \( \alpha \), \( \Sigma(\wn) \) from (23), (27), (22).
  5. Recompute \( G(\mathbf q,\wn) \) from (29); iterate 3–5 to convergence.
  6. Checks: sum rule (31) at every iteration (exact for the truncated series; the resummed self-consistent \( G \) obeys it only to high accuracy, so tolerate a small residual); \( \delta U \) from (34) vs. (35); near criticality, \( T_c \) is where \( 1+\Sigma(0)+\Jc(\mathbf q_{\rm ord}) G_0(0) \to 0 \), i.e. the MF/RPA \( T_c \) is suppressed by the factor \( 1+\Sigma(0) \) renormalizing the single-ion response.

Ordered phase / finite field: add the outer loop of Sec. 9.3 determining \( H_{\rm MF} \) from \( H \) via (41), (43), and use the elastic-sector forms (37)–(40).

Generalization to realistic level schemes (from Sec. III of the paper, framework-level): the entire construction survives with the single-ion response \( G_0 \) replaced by the full Cartesian susceptibility tensor of the actual crystal-field (+ hyperfine) level scheme, and multiple sublattices handled by the standard RPA matrix inversion; the \(1/z\) renormalization enters as division of the susceptibility components deriving from the dominant singlet–singlet (or doublet) transition by \( 1+\Sigma(\omega) \), with \( \Sigma \) computed from that sector; Jensen renormalizes only those components, neglecting the \(1/z\) corrections to the weaker transitions. Hyperfine coupling \( A\,\mathbf I\cdot\mathbf J \) enhances the static single-ion response, \( \tilde\chi_{\rm eff} = \tilde\chi\,(1 + A^2 \tilde\chi\, \chi_I) \) with \( \chi_I \) the nuclear Curie susceptibility, and correspondingly \( K(0) \to K(0)(1 + A^2\tilde\chi\,\chi_I) \) in the elastic sector — the mechanism by which nuclear moments shift \( T_c \) in LiHoF4-type systems.

Validity: the theory is exact through \( O(1/z) \); dropped terms are \( O(1/z^2) \). It is not a critical theory — exponents remain MF-like — but for three-dimensional dipolar Ising systems (marginal dimensionality) the true critical behaviour differs from MF only by logarithmic corrections, so the \(1/z\)-renormalized MF/RPA description is quantitatively accurate essentially everywhere, including the location of \( T_c \) and the size of its fluctuation suppression.
