0  Scope and conventions

This document reconstructs the theoretical framework of Jensen’s 1/z (high-density) expansion for a lattice of singlet–singlet ions coupled by an Ising-like two-ion (dipolar) interaction, keeping only what is material-independent. Steps that Jensen states without proof are derived explicitly where a rigorous derivation is feasible; the few places where the full bookkeeping is prohibitively long are marked, with the method outlined and the final coefficients quoted. Units: \( \hbar = 1 \), \( \beta = 1/k_B T \). Matsubara frequencies are bosonic, \( \omega_n = 2\pi n/\beta \) (the operators \(J_x\) commute at equal times, so all correlation functions are \(\beta\)-periodic).

\( \Delta \)splitting between the two lowest singlets \( |0\rangle, |1\rangle \) of the mean-field (MF) single-ion Hamiltonian.
\( M,\; \pm m \)\( \langle 0|J_x|1\rangle = M \); diagonal elements \( \langle 0|J_x|0\rangle = m \), \( \langle 1|J_x|1\rangle = -m \). \( M_0^2 = M^2+m^2 \) is invariant under rotations in the two-dimensional level space; \( m=0 \) in the zero-field paramagnet.
\( a_{\nu\mu} \)standard-basis operators \( |\nu\rangle\langle\mu| \) built on the MF eigenstates. Shorthand \( b\equiv a_{01} \), \( b^\dagger \equiv a_{10} \), \( X \equiv b+b^\dagger \).
\( n_0, n_1, n_{01} \)MF populations \( n_0 = [1+e^{-\beta\Delta}]^{-1} \), \( n_0+n_1=1 \), \( n_{01}=n_0-n_1=\tanh(\beta\Delta/2) \).
\( \Jc(ij),\ \Jc(\mathbf q) \)two-ion coupling (for a dipolar magnet: the Ewald-summed dipole coupling plus any exchange), \( \Jc(\mathbf q) = \sum_j \Jc(ij)\,e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf R_i-\mathbf R_j)} \), \( \Jc(ii)=0 \).
\( z \)effective coordination number. For dipolar coupling the interaction is long-ranged, so the effective \(z\) is large and the expansion is well controlled.

1  The singlet–singlet model

Each magnetic ion is reduced to its two lowest crystal-field singlets (all higher levels assumed energetically remote), and the two-ion coupling acts through a single angular-momentum component \( J_x \) (the strongly anisotropic, Ising-like limit). In the basis of the MF eigenstates,

$$ J_x \;=\; m\,(a_{00}-a_{11}) \;+\; M\,(a_{01}+a_{10}) \;=\; m\,(a_{00}-a_{11}) + M X . \tag{1} $$

In the zero-field paramagnetic phase the MF eigenstates are the bare crystal-field singlets and \( m=0 \); in a field or in the ordered phase the MF Hamiltonian mixes them, the splitting \( \Delta \) grows, and weight is rotated from \(M\) into \(m\) with \( M^2+m^2 = M_0^2 \) fixed. A useful exact operator identity follows from (1). Since \( a_{00},a_{11} \) are the orthogonal projectors onto \( |0\rangle,|1\rangle \) (\( a_{00}^2=a_{00} \), \( a_{11}^2=a_{11} \), \( a_{00}a_{11}=a_{11}a_{00}=0 \)),

$$ (a_{00}-a_{11})^2 = a_{00}^2+a_{11}^2 = a_{00}+a_{11}. $$

The same combination is produced by \( X^2 \): since \( b^2=(b^\dagger)^2=0 \) (each of \( b,b^\dagger \) already sits at one end of the two-state ladder) while \( bb^\dagger=a_{01}a_{10}=a_{00} \) and \( b^\dagger b=a_{10}a_{01}=a_{11} \),

$$ X^2=(b+b^\dagger)^2=bb^\dagger+b^\dagger b=a_{00}+a_{11}, $$

and direct evaluation on the basis states gives

$$ a_{00}X = b,\qquad a_{11}X = b^\dagger, \qquad Xa_{00} = b^\dagger, \qquad Xa_{11} = b, $$

so \( (a_{00}-a_{11})X = b-b^\dagger \) while \( X(a_{00}-a_{11}) = b^\dagger-b \), and the cross term cancels identically, \( (a_{00}-a_{11})X + X(a_{00}-a_{11}) = 0 \). Hence

$$ J_x^2 = m^2(a_{00}-a_{11})^2 + mM\big[(a_{00}-a_{11})X+X(a_{00}-a_{11})\big] + M^2X^2 = (m^2+M^2)\,(a_{00}+a_{11}). $$

Because the two singlets \( |0\rangle,|1\rangle \) are, by construction (Sec. 1), the entire single-ion Hilbert space kept in this model, \( a_{00}+a_{11} \) is the resolution of the identity on that space, \( a_{00}+a_{11}=\hat 1 \) — the same completeness relation reappears as the projector \( P \) of Sec. 1.1 once the two-level truncation is embedded in a larger physical level scheme. This gives

$$ J_x^2 \;=\; m^2 + M^2 \;=\; M_0^2\,\hat 1 . \tag{2} $$

The lattice Hamiltonian is split as \( \mathcal H = \mathcal H_0 + \mathcal H_1 \), where \( \mathcal H_0 \) is the sum of single-ion MF Hamiltonians (crystal field plus mean field; per ion \( \mathcal H_0^{(i)} = \Delta\, a_{11,i} \) up to a constant) and

$$ \mathcal H_1 \;=\; -\tfrac12 \sum_{ij} \Jc(ij)\, \tilde J_{ix}\tilde J_{jx}, \qquad \tilde J_{ix} \equiv J_{ix} - \langle J_x\rangle . \tag{3} $$

J 2.2, 2.4  Because every term of \( \mathcal H_1 \) (and the mean-field Zeeman term) is built from \(J_x\) operators only, and \( [J_{ix}, J_{jx}] = 0 \) for all \( i,j \),

$$ [\mathcal H_1,\, J_{ix}] = 0 \quad\text{exactly}, \tag{4} $$

a fact used repeatedly below (equations of motion, sum rule, energy formula).

1.1  The scalar coupling as the Ising projection of the dipolar tensor

For a dipolar magnet the microscopic two-ion coupling is not a scalar: it is the Cartesian tensor

$$ \mathcal H_1^{\rm full} \;=\; -\tfrac12 \sum_{ij} \sum_{\alpha\beta} \Jc_{\alpha\beta}(ij)\, \tilde J_{i\alpha}\tilde J_{j\beta}, \qquad \alpha,\beta \in \{x,y,z\}, $$

with \( \Jc_{\alpha\beta}(ij) \) the classical dipole sum plus any exchange — all three components of \( \mathbf J \) participate, and this is as true of HoF3 as of LiHoF4. The scalar model (3) is the projection of this tensor onto the two-level subspace, not an additional assumption about the interaction. Let \( P = a_{00} + a_{11} \) project onto the two low singlets. In the strongly anisotropic level schemes considered here exactly one component — \( J_x \) in the convention of (1) — has a nonvanishing 2×2 block, \( PJ_xP = m(a_{00}-a_{11}) + MX \); for the other components \( PJ_yP \) and \( PJ_zP \) vanish or are negligible (in LiHoF4 the Γ3,4 ground doublet carries only the \(c\)-axis component of \( \mathbf J \), so there the surviving channel is \( cc \)). An interaction line \( \Jc_{\alpha\beta}\,\tilde J_{i\alpha}\tilde J_{j\beta} \) contributes to the fluctuation series of Secs. 2–9 only if both ends create or destroy fluctuations within the two-level subspace, so every block with \( \alpha\beta \neq xx \) drops out of the expansion. Such blocks are not discarded physically; they act through the two channels left open to them: (i) their static mean-field values, which are absorbed into the single-ion Hamiltonian that defines \( \Delta, M, m \) (a transverse-channel mean field mixes the singlets exactly like an external transverse field), and (ii) virtual transitions to the remote levels outside the two-level subspace, which belong to the weak-transition sector that the realistic-level-scheme generalization (Sec. 10) keeps in the bare susceptibility tensor but leaves unrenormalized. Inside the two-level subspace, therefore, \( \mathcal H_1^{\rm full} \) truncates to the single-channel form (3) with \( \Jc(ij) \equiv \Jc_{xx}(ij) \), and everything that follows is controlled 1/z bookkeeping for that channel.

2  Green functions and the linked-cluster expansion

The imaginary-time Green function and its transforms are J 2.1–2.3

$$ \begin{aligned} G(ij,\tau_1-\tau_2) &\;=\; -\,\big\langle T_\tau\, \tilde J_{ix}(\tau_1)\, \tilde J_{jx}(\tau_2) \big\rangle, \\[.3em] \tilde J_{ix}(\tau) &= e^{\tau\mathcal H}\, \tilde J_{ix}\, e^{-\tau\mathcal H}, \end{aligned} \tag{5} $$ $$ \begin{aligned} G(\mathbf q, \wn) &= \sum_j e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot(\mathbf R_i - \mathbf R_j)} \int_0^\beta \! d\tau\; e^{\wn \tau}\, G(ij,\tau), \\[.3em] G(ij,\tau) &= \frac{1}{N\beta}\sum_{\mathbf q, n} e^{i\mathbf q\cdot \mathbf R_{ij}} e^{-\wn\tau} G(\mathbf q,\wn). \end{aligned} \tag{6} $$

Standard perturbation theory in \( \mathcal H_1 \) gives the linked-cluster series J 2.5, 2.6: expanding \( e^{-\beta\mathcal H} = e^{-\beta\mathcal H_0}\, T_\tau \exp[-\int_0^\beta \mathcal H_1(\tau)d\tau] \) in both numerator and denominator of (5), the denominator \( \langle U(\beta,0)\rangle_0 \) cancels exactly the disconnected (vacuum) parts, leaving the sum of connected terms in which the unperturbed averages \( \langle\cdots\rangle_0 \) factorize over sites (the \( \mathcal H_0 \) ensemble is a product state). Because \(J_x\) operators commute, no fermionic sign bookkeeping arises. The building blocks are therefore the single-site cumulants (semi-invariants) of \( \tilde J_x \) connected by interaction lines \( \Jc \).

3  The bare single-site propagator

Derived in full → J 2.7, 2.8

In the MF single-ion ensemble the imaginary-time evolution is generated by the single-ion Hamiltonian \( \mathcal H_0^{(i)}=\Delta a_{11,i} \) alone (Sec. 1). Since \( a_{11}b=a_{11}a_{01}=0 \) and \( ba_{11}=a_{01}a_{11}=a_{01}=b \) (immediate from \( a_{11}=|1\rangle\langle1| \), \( b=|0\rangle\langle1| \)), the commutator is

$$ [a_{11},b] \;=\; a_{11}b-ba_{11} \;=\; -\,b, $$

i.e. \( b \) is an eigenoperator of \( \mathrm{ad}_{a_{11}}\equiv[a_{11},\cdot\,] \) with eigenvalue \( -1 \). For any \( X,Y \) with \( [X,Y]=\lambda Y \), the Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff series \( e^{X}Ye^{-X}=Y+[X,Y]+\tfrac1{2!}[X,[X,Y]]+\cdots \) collapses term by term to \( e^{\lambda}Y \); with \( X=\tau\Delta a_{11} \), \( \lambda=-\tau\Delta \), so

$$ b(\tau) \;=\; e^{\tau \Delta a_{11}}\, b\, e^{-\tau \Delta a_{11}} \;=\; e^{-\Delta\tau}\, b . $$

The same computation with \( a_{11}b^\dagger=a_{11}a_{10}=a_{10}=b^\dagger \) and \( b^\dagger a_{11}=a_{10}a_{11}=0 \) gives \( [a_{11},b^\dagger]=+b^\dagger \), hence \( b^\dagger(\tau) = e^{\Delta\tau} b^\dagger \). Define

$$ f(\tau) \;\equiv\; n_0\, e^{-\Delta\tau} + n_1\, e^{\Delta\tau}. \tag{7} $$

For \( 0<\tau<\beta \), using \( \langle b b^\dagger\rangle_0 = n_0 \), \( \langle b^\dagger b\rangle_0 = n_1 \), \( \langle bb\rangle_0 = \langle b^\dagger b^\dagger \rangle_0 = 0 \):

$$ \big\langle X(\tau) X(0)\big\rangle_0 = e^{-\Delta\tau}\langle bb^\dagger\rangle_0 + e^{\Delta\tau}\langle b^\dagger b\rangle_0 = f(\tau). \tag{8} $$

By the single-site specialization of (5), \( G_0(\tau) = -\langle T_\tau\, \tilde J_x(\tau)\tilde J_x(0)\rangle_0 \) with \( \tilde J_x = J_x - \langle J_x\rangle_0 \). Write \( \tilde J_x(\tau) = m\,D + M\,X(\tau) \), where \( D \equiv (a_{00}-a_{11}) - n_{01} \) uses \( \langle J_x\rangle_0 = m\langle a_{00}-a_{11}\rangle_0 + M\langle X\rangle_0 = m\,n_{01} \) (the MF ensemble is diagonal in \( |0\rangle,|1\rangle \), so \( \langle X\rangle_0 = \langle b+b^\dagger\rangle_0 = 0 \)). The diagonal part \( a_{00}-a_{11} \) commutes with \( \mathcal H_0 \) (it is a constant of motion), so \( D \) is itself \(\tau\)-independent, and the product splits into three pieces:

$$ T_\tau\big[\tilde J_x(\tau)\tilde J_x(0)\big] \;=\; m^2 D^2 \;+\; mM\big[D X(0) + X(\tau) D\big] \;+\; M^2\, X(\tau)X(0). $$

Diagonal–diagonal. Using the identity \( (a_{00}-a_{11})^2=\hat1 \) of (2), \( \langle D^2\rangle_0 = \langle(a_{00}-a_{11})^2\rangle_0 - 2n_{01}\langle a_{00}-a_{11}\rangle_0 + n_{01}^2 = 1-n_{01}^2 \), its fluctuation is \(\tau\)-independent as claimed. Cross terms. From \( (a_{00}-a_{11})X=b-b^\dagger \) and \( X(a_{00}-a_{11})=b^\dagger-b \) (Sec. 1), \( DX(0) \) and \( X(\tau)D \) are pure combinations of \( b,b^\dagger \); each has zero trace against the \(\mathcal H_0\)-diagonal ensemble, so \( \langle DX(0)\rangle_0 = \langle X(\tau)D\rangle_0 = 0 \) — the diagonal moment \( m(a_{00}-a_{11}) \) never mixes with the off-diagonal \( MX \), at any \(\tau\). Off-diagonal–off-diagonal. \( \langle T_\tau X(\tau)X(0)\rangle_0 = f(\tau) \), by (8).

Hence

$$ G_0(\tau) = -\,M^2 f(\tau) - m^2\,(1-n_{01}^2), \qquad 0<\tau<\beta. \tag{9} $$

Fourier transform, using \( e^{\wn\beta}=1 \) (reminder: bosonic Matsubara frequencies have \( \omega_n=2\pi n/\beta \), so \( \omega_n\beta=2\pi n \) and \( e^{i\omega_n\beta}=e^{2\pi i n}=1 \) for every integer \( n \)): \( \int_0^\beta e^{(\wn \mp \Delta)\tau} d\tau = \dfrac{e^{\mp\beta\Delta}-1}{\wn \mp \Delta} \), together with the two population identities

$$ \begin{aligned} n_0\,(e^{-\beta\Delta}-1) &= \frac{e^{-\beta\Delta}-1}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta}} = -\,n_{01}, \\[.3em] n_1\,(e^{\beta\Delta}-1) &= \frac{1-e^{-\beta\Delta}}{1+e^{-\beta\Delta}} = +\,n_{01}, \end{aligned} \tag{10} $$

gives

$$ \begin{aligned} -M^2\!\int_0^\beta\! e^{\wn\tau} f(\tau)\, d\tau &= -M^2 n_{01}\left[ \frac{1}{\wn+\Delta} - \frac{1}{\wn-\Delta} \right] \\[.3em] &= -M^2\, \frac{2 n_{01}\Delta}{\Delta^2 - (\wn)^2}, \end{aligned} $$

and \( \int_0^\beta e^{\wn\tau} d\tau = \beta\,\delta_{n,0} \) for the elastic part. Therefore J 2.7, 2.8

$$ \boxed{\begin{aligned} & G_0(\wn) = -M^2 g(\wn) - m^2 h(\wn), \\[.3em] & g(\wn) = \frac{2 n_{01}\Delta}{\Delta^2-(\wn)^2}, \qquad h(\wn) = \beta\,(1-n_{01}^2)\,\delta_{n,0}. \end{aligned}} \tag{11} $$

\( g \) carries the inelastic (Van Vleck) response with poles at \( \pm\Delta \); \( h \) is the elastic (Curie-like) response of the diagonal moment, present only when \( m\neq0 \). Both are even in \( \omega_n \). Static single-site susceptibility: \( \chi_0(0) = -G_0(0) = M^2 g(0) + m^2\beta(1-n_{01}^2) \) with \( g(0) = 2n_{01}/\Delta \).

4  1/z power counting and the RPA

Derived in full → J 2.9

First order. With sites decoupled at zeroth order, the connected first-order term of (5) requires the two interaction indices to coincide with the external sites \( (i,j) \); the two assignments cancel the factor \( \tfrac12 \):

$$ \begin{aligned} \delta G(ij,\tau) &= \Big\langle T_\tau\, \tilde J_{ix}(\tau)\, \tilde J_{jx}(0) \int_0^\beta\! d\tau_1\, \mathcal H_1(\tau_1) \Big\rangle_0^{\rm conn} \\[.3em] &= -\,\Jc(ij) \int_0^\beta\! d\tau_1\, G_0(\tau-\tau_1)\, G_0(\tau_1). \end{aligned} \tag{12} $$

Fourier transform both sides using the pair (6). Sum over \( j \) with phase \( e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_{ij}} \) first: since \( G_0 \) is a single-site quantity carrying no \( j \)-dependence, only \( \Jc(ij) \) sees the phase, so

$$ \sum_j e^{-i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_{ij}}\, \delta G(ij,\tau) \;=\; -\,\Jc(\mathbf q) \int_0^\beta\! d\tau_1\, G_0(\tau-\tau_1)\, G_0(\tau_1) . $$

For the remaining \(\tau\)-convolution, insert the inverse Matsubara transform \( G_0(\tau) = \tfrac1\beta\sum_n e^{-\wn\tau} G_0(\wn) \) (the single-site case of (6)) for each factor:

$$ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\beta \! d\tau_1\, G_0(\tau-\tau_1)\, G_0(\tau_1) &= \frac{1}{\beta^2} \sum_{n_1,n_2} e^{-\wn_1\tau}\, G_0(\wn_1)\, G_0(\wn_2) \int_0^\beta \! d\tau_1\, e^{(\wn_1-\wn_2)\tau_1} \\[.3em] &= \frac1\beta \sum_{n_1} e^{-\wn_1\tau}\, G_0^2(\wn_1), \end{aligned} $$

using \( \int_0^\beta e^{i(\omega_{n_1}-\omega_{n_2})\tau_1}\, d\tau_1 = \beta\,\delta_{n_1n_2} \) (bosonic-Matsubara orthogonality, used again in Sec. 6.1): imaginary-time convolution on \( [0,\beta) \) becomes ordinary multiplication in \( \wn \)-space, as for any Fourier pair. Multiplying by \( e^{\wn\tau} \) and integrating \( \int_0^\beta d\tau \) isolates \( n_1=n \) on the right and gives, on the left, the Matsubara transform of \( \delta G(\mathbf q,\tau) \):

$$ \delta G(\mathbf q,\wn) \;=\; -\,\Jc(\mathbf q)\, G_0^2(\wn). \tag{13} $$

Iterating the same contraction pattern (chains of single-site pairs joined by \( \Jc \) lines) produces a geometric series,

$$ G(\mathbf q,\wn)\big|_{\rm RPA} = G_0 - \Jc(\mathbf q) G_0^2 + \Jc^2(\mathbf q) G_0^3 - \cdots = \boxed{\;\frac{G_0(\wn)}{1+\Jc(\mathbf q)\, G_0(\wn)}\;} \tag{14} $$

J 2.9. (With \( \chi = -G \) this is the familiar \( \chi = \chi_0/[1-\Jc(\mathbf q)\chi_0] \).)

Power counting. Let \( \Jc \) denote the typical coupling to each of \( z \) neighbours, so the coherent scale is \( \Jc(\mathbf q) \sim z\Jc \). Site-diagonal loops cost powers of \( 1/z \):

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac1N \sum_{\mathbf q} \Jc(\mathbf q)^p \;&=\; \big(\Jc^{\,p}\big)(ii) = \sum_{j_1,\dots,j_{p-1}} \Jc(ij_1)\Jc(j_1 j_2)\cdots \Jc(j_{p-1} i) \\[.3em] &\;\sim\; z^{\,p-1}\Jc^p \;=\; \frac{(z\Jc)^p}{z} , \end{aligned} \tag{15} $$

Every independent Brillouin-zone average of a \( \Jc \)-dependent quantity therefore carries a relative factor \( 1/z \). The RPA (14) is exact at order \( (1/z)^0 \) beyond MF; the diagrams neglected by it (self-energy insertions in which an interaction line begins and ends on paths through the same site) first enter at order \( 1/z \). For dipolar coupling the interaction is long ranged, the effective \( z \) is large, and — since \( d=3 \) is the marginal dimensionality for a dipolar Ising system — the neglected critical fluctuations produce only logarithmic corrections to the MF/RPA critical behaviour. This is what makes the scheme quantitatively reliable for LiHoF4-type magnets.

5  The effective medium

Derived in full → J 2.10–2.12, 2.14

Define the site-diagonal Green function and the effective-medium coupling J 2.10, 2.11:

$$ G(\wn) = \frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q} G(\mathbf q,\wn), \qquad K(\wn) = \frac{1}{N\, G(\wn)}\sum_{\mathbf q} \Jc(\mathbf q)\, G(\mathbf q,\wn). \tag{16} $$

\( K(\wn) \) is the (frequency-dependent) amplitude for leaving a site, propagating through the lattice, and returning — it plays the role of a dynamical cavity/Weiss field, and is \( O(1/z) \) relative to \( \Jc(\mathbf q=\mathbf 0) \) by (15), because its leading term \( \frac1N\sum_q \Jc(q) = \Jc(ii) = 0 \) vanishes and the first nonzero term involves \( \frac1N\sum_q \Jc^2(q) \). Jensen’s ansatz for restoring the \( \mathbf q \) dependence around the site-diagonal function is J 2.12

$$ G(\mathbf q, \wn) = \frac{G(\wn)}{1 + \big[\Jc(\mathbf q) - K(\wn)\big]\, G(\wn)} . \tag{17} $$

Self-consistency. Rewrite (17) as \( G(\mathbf q) = G - G(\mathbf q)\,[\Jc(\mathbf q)-K]\,G \) and average over \( \mathbf q \):

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q} G(\mathbf q) &= G - \Big[\tfrac1N\sum_{\mathbf q}\Jc(\mathbf q)G(\mathbf q)\Big] G + K\,G\cdot G \\[.3em] &= G - (KG)\,G + (KG)\,G = G \quad\checkmark \end{aligned} $$

so (17) is automatically consistent with both definitions (16), whatever \( K(\wn) \) is. The content of the theory is the computation of \( G(\wn) \) itself: it is the Green function of a single site embedded in a medium that acts on it through the retarded field \( K(\tau_1-\tau_2) \). Its perturbation series is the single-site cumulant series of Sec. 2 with \( \Jc(ij) \) lines replaced by \( K \) lines (an interaction line must leave the site and return; \( K \) is precisely that amplitude, and using it removes the double counting of the chain diagrams already resummed by (17)).

RPA check. Decoupling all single-site cumulants into products of two-point functions gives the geometric series J 2.14

$$ G(\wn)\big|_{\rm RPA} = \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1 + K(\wn)\, G_0(\wn)}, \tag{18} $$

and inserting (18) into (17) makes \( K \) cancel identically:

$$ \begin{aligned} G(\mathbf q,\wn) &= \frac{G_0/(1+KG_0)}{1+(\Jc(\mathbf q)-K)\,G_0/(1+KG_0)} \\[.3em] &= \frac{G_0}{1 + K G_0 + (\Jc(\mathbf q)-K)G_0} = \frac{G_0}{1+\Jc(\mathbf q) G_0}, \end{aligned} $$

recovering (14). The effective-medium construction therefore reproduces the RPA exactly at leading order, and organizes the \( 1/z \) corrections as corrections to the single-site function.

6  The single-site series to first order in 1/z

Throughout this section \( m = 0 \) (zero-field paramagnet), so \( J_x = MX \) and \( G_0 = -M^2 g \). The elastic modifications for \( m\neq0 \) are in Sec. 9.

6.1  The first 1/z term and its pairing part

Derived in full → J 2.13 and the −G₀KG₀ term of J 2.15

The single-site cumulant series with one \( K \) line is J 2.13

$$ \begin{aligned} \delta G(\wn) \;=\; & -\tfrac12 \int_0^\beta\!\!\!\int_0^\beta\!\!\!\int_0^\beta d\tau\, d\tau_1\, d\tau_2\; e^{\wn\tau}\, \frac1\beta \sum_{n'} K(\wm)\, e^{\wm(\tau_1-\tau_2)} \\[.3em] & \times \Big[ \big\langle T\tilde J_x(\tau_2)\tilde J_x(\tau_1)\tilde J_x(\tau)\tilde J_x(0)\big\rangle_0 - \big\langle T\tilde J_x(\tau_2)\tilde J_x(\tau_1)\big\rangle_0 \big\langle T\tilde J_x(\tau)\tilde J_x(0)\big\rangle_0 \Big] \end{aligned} \tag{19} $$

where the subtracted product is the disconnected (vacuum-loop \( \times G_0 \)) part removed by the linked-cluster theorem. Decompose the four-point average into the three pair products plus the fourth-order semi-invariant \( S_4 \). The two remaining pairings are RPA-like and evaluate exactly. For the pairing \( (\tau_2,\tau)(\tau_1,0) \): \( \langle T\rangle\langle T\rangle = G_0(\tau_2-\tau)\,G_0(\tau_1) \), and

$$ \begin{aligned} \int_0^\beta \! d\tau_2\, e^{-\wm\tau_2} G_0(\tau_2-\tau) &= e^{-\wm\tau} G_0(\wm), \\[.3em] \int_0^\beta \! d\tau_1\, e^{\wm\tau_1} G_0(\tau_1) &= G_0(\wm), \\[.3em] \int_0^\beta \! d\tau\, e^{i(\omega_n-\omega_{n'})\tau} &= \beta\,\delta_{nn'} , \end{aligned} $$

(using periodicity and \( G_0(\wn) = G_0(-\wn) \)), giving \( -\tfrac12 K(\wn) G_0^2(\wn) \). The pairing \( (\tau_2,0)(\tau_1,\tau) \) gives the same with \( n'\to -n \), hence an equal contribution. Together:

$$ \delta G(\wn)\Big|_{\rm pairings} = -\,G_0(\wn)\, K(\wn)\, G_0(\wn), \tag{20} $$

which is the first term inside the braces of \( G = G_0 - G_0\{K G_0 + \Sigma\} + \cdots \) J 2.15. The genuinely new physics at order \(1/z\) is the semi-invariant part, which defines \( \Sigma \): \( \;\delta G|_{S_4} \equiv -G_0(\wn)\,\Sigma(\wn) \).

6.2  The fourth-order semi-invariant of the two-level ion (exact)

Derived in full — not given in the paper

Since \( b^2 = (b^\dagger)^2 = 0 \), only the alternating operator strings \( bb^\dagger bb^\dagger \) (through \( |0\rangle \), weight \( n_0 \)) and \( b^\dagger b b^\dagger b \) (through \( |1\rangle \), weight \( n_1 \)) survive in an ordered four-point product of \( X = b+b^\dagger \). For \( \tau_a \ge \tau_b \ge \tau_c \ge \tau_d \), collecting the phases \( e^{\mp\Delta\tau} \):

$$ \big\langle X(\tau_a) X(\tau_b) X(\tau_c) X(\tau_d) \big\rangle_0 \;=\; f(\tau_a - \tau_b + \tau_c - \tau_d) \;\equiv\; f(u), \tag{21} $$

with \( f \) from (7) — the same function as the two-point correlator, evaluated on the alternating time combination. Introduce the three linear combinations

$$ u = \tau_a-\tau_b+\tau_c-\tau_d, \qquad v = \tau_a+\tau_b-\tau_c-\tau_d \;(\ge 0), \qquad w = \tau_a-\tau_b-\tau_c+\tau_d, $$

and note the pair-argument identities \( \tau_{ac}+\tau_{bd} = \tau_{ad}+\tau_{bc} = v \), \( \tau_{ac}-\tau_{bd} = \tau_{ab}-\tau_{cd} = w \), \( \tau_{ad}-\tau_{bc} = \tau_{ab}+\tau_{cd} = u \) (with \( \tau_{ab} \equiv \tau_a-\tau_b \), etc.). Using \( f(x)f(y) = n_0^2 e^{-\Delta(x+y)} + n_1^2 e^{\Delta(x+y)} + 2 n_0 n_1 \cosh[\Delta(x-y)] \) and \( n_0-n_0^2 = n_1 - n_1^2 = n_0 n_1 \), the semi-invariant \( S_4 = \langle XXXX\rangle_0 - f(\tau_{ab})f(\tau_{cd}) - f(\tau_{ac})f(\tau_{bd}) - f(\tau_{ad})f(\tau_{bc}) \) collapses: the \( e^{\pm\Delta u} \) terms cancel between \( f(u) - f(\tau_{ab})f(\tau_{cd}) \) and \( -f(\tau_{ad})f(\tau_{bc}) \), leaving

$$ \boxed{\begin{aligned} & S_4(\tau_a,\tau_b,\tau_c,\tau_d) \;=\; -\,2\Big[\, 2 n_0 n_1 \cosh(\Delta w) \;+\; n_0^2\, e^{-\Delta v} \;+\; n_1^2\, e^{\Delta v} \,\Big], \\[.3em] & \tau_a\ge\tau_b\ge\tau_c\ge\tau_d. \end{aligned}} \tag{22} $$

Checks. \( T=0 \) (\( n_0=1, n_1=0 \)): direct evaluation gives \( S_4 = e^{-\Delta u} - e^{-\Delta u} - 2e^{-\Delta v} = -2e^{-\Delta v} \)  ✓. \( T\to\infty \) (\( n_0=n_1=\tfrac12 \), \( f=\cosh\Delta\tau \)): product-to-sum identities give \( S_4 = -\cosh(\Delta v) - \cosh(\Delta w) \), matching (22)  ✓. Note \( S_4 \le 0 \) always: the two-level constraint \( b^2=0 \) suppresses fluctuations relative to Gaussian (Wick) statistics — this hard-core kinematic correction is exactly what the 1/z self-energy transmits to the lattice problem.

6.3  Structure of Σ

Method derived; coefficient of the frequency-dependent part quoted → J 2.15, 2.16, 2.18, 2.19

Insert \( S_4 \) (with \( J_x = MX \): a factor \( M^4 \)) for the semi-invariant part of (19). Since \( S_4 \) is a sum of pure exponentials in the four times, each of the six orderings of \( (\tau_2,\tau_1,\tau) \) relative to each other (all \( >0 \)) gives elementary nested integrals: the internal legs carry \( e^{\pm\wm\tau_{1,2}} \) and produce resolvent factors \( (\Delta\mp\wm)^{-1} \) that recombine pairwise into \( g(\wm) \) and \( g^2(\wm) \); the external legs produce the overall factor \( g(\wn) \) or frequency-independent (equal-time) weights \( g(0) \) and \( \beta(1-n_{01}^2) \). The \( n' \) sums then appear only through

$$ \lambda_p \;=\; \frac1\beta \sum_{n'} K(\wm)\, \big[g(\wm)\big]^p , \tag{23} $$

J 2.19, plus terms in which the internal frequency locks to the external one (\( \delta_{nn'} \) from the \( \tau \) integral), which yield an explicit \( K(\wn) \). This is precisely the missing half of Sec. 6.1: the full first-order correction is the pairing piece plus the semi-invariant piece, \( \delta G(\wn) = \delta G|_{\rm pairings} + \delta G|_{S_4} \), and inserting (20) together with the defining relation \( \delta G|_{S_4}\equiv-G_0(\wn)\Sigma(\wn) \) gives \( \delta G(\wn) = -G_0(\wn)K(\wn)G_0(\wn) - G_0(\wn)\Sigma(\wn) = -G_0(\wn)\big[K(\wn)G_0(\wn)+\Sigma(\wn)\big] \). Since \( G=G_0+\delta G \), collecting everything, the series and self-energy take the form J 2.15, 2.16

$$ G(\wn) = G_0 - G_0\big[K(\wn) G_0 + \Sigma(\wn)\big] + \cdots, \qquad \Sigma(\wn) = \alpha + \gamma(\wn)\, g(\wn), \tag{24} $$

(the “\( +\cdots \)” marks the higher, \( O(1/z^2) \) terms of the single-site series — additional factors of \( KG_0+\Sigma \), see Sec. 6.5 — not computed here), with the frequency-dependent coefficient J 2.18

$$ \gamma(\wn) = \frac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[ \lambda_1 - (1-n_{01}^2)\, K(\wn) \Big]. \tag{25} $$

The full bookkeeping behind (25) (six orderings × the three exponential structures of (22)) is lengthy and is not reproduced here; the result is Jensen’s, obtained with the standard-basis-operator contraction techniques of Yang–Wang (1974) and Care–Tucker (1977), building on Stinchcombe’s 1/z expansion (1973), and confirmed by the checks below. The constant \( \alpha \), by contrast, does not have to be taken on trust: it is fixed exactly by the sum rule, as shown next.

6.4  α fixed by the sum rule (independent derivation)

Derived in full → J 2.17

Anticipating the exact sum rule of Sec. 7, \( \frac1\beta\sum_n G(\wn) = -M^2 \), which \( G_0 \) already saturates (Sec. 7), the \( 1/z \) correction in (24) must sum to zero:

$$ \frac1\beta\sum_n G_0(\wn)\big[ K(\wn) G_0(\wn) + \Sigma(\wn) \big] = 0 . \tag{26} $$

With \( G_0 = -M^2 g \) and (24), (25), the integrand is \( -M^2\big[ -M^2 K g^2 + \alpha g + \tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\lambda_1 g^2 - \tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}(1-n_{01}^2) K g^2 \big] \). The two \( K g^2 \) terms combine, since \( 1 + (1-n_{01}^2)/n_{01}^2 = 1/n_{01}^2 \), into \( -\tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2} K g^2 \), whose frequency sum is \( -\tfrac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\lambda_2 \). Using \( \frac1\beta\sum_n g(\wn) = 1 \) (Sec. 7), condition (26) becomes

$$ \alpha \;=\; \frac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[ \lambda_2 - \lambda_1\cdot \frac1\beta\sum_n g^2(\wn) \Big]. \tag{27} $$

The required Matsubara identity. From the standard sum \( \frac1\beta\sum_n (\omega_n^2+\Delta^2)^{-1} = \coth(\beta\Delta/2)/(2\Delta) \), differentiation with respect to \( \Delta^2 \) gives \( \frac1\beta\sum_n (\omega_n^2+\Delta^2)^{-2} = \coth(\beta\Delta/2)/(4\Delta^3) + \beta\,{\rm csch}^2(\beta\Delta/2)/(8\Delta^2) \). Multiplying by \( (2 n_{01}\Delta)^2 \) and using \( n_{01} = \tanh(\beta\Delta/2) \) (so \( n_{01}^2\coth = n_{01} \) and \( n_{01}^2\,{\rm csch}^2 = {\rm sech}^2 = 1-n_{01}^2 \)):

$$ \frac1\beta\sum_n g^2(\wn) \;=\; \frac{n_{01}}{\Delta} + \frac{\beta}{2}(1-n_{01}^2) \;=\; \tfrac12\Big[ g(0) + \beta\,(1-n_{01}^2) \Big]. \tag{28} $$

Substituting (28) into (27) reproduces Jensen’s constant exactly J 2.17:

$$ \boxed{\; \alpha = \frac{M^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[ \lambda_2 - \tfrac12\big\{ g(0) + \beta(1-n_{01}^2) \big\}\,\lambda_1 \Big]. \;} \tag{29} $$

This closes the loop: given the structure (24) and \( \gamma \) of (25), the exact sum rule forces (29), confirming the internal consistency of the truncated series (“the sum rule is obeyed if the series is terminated after the first or second term”).

6.5  Resummation and the final propagator

Derived in full → J 2.20, 2.30

Higher terms of the single-site series repeat the pattern of (24) with additional factors of \( (K G_0 + \Sigma) \) at \( O(1/z^2) \) accuracy; the series is resummed Dyson-style by replacing the leading \( G_0 \) prefactor of the correction by the full \( G \):

$$ G = G_0 - G\,\big[K G_0 + \Sigma\big] \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; G(\wn) = \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1 + \Sigma(\wn) + K(\wn) G_0(\wn)} . \tag{30} $$

Inserting (30) into the effective-medium form (17), \( K \) cancels identically, exactly as in the RPA check of Sec. 5:

$$ \boxed{\; G(\mathbf q, \wn) = \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1 + \Sigma(\wn) + \Jc(\mathbf q)\, G_0(\wn)} \;} \tag{31} $$

J 2.20. Equivalently, defining the renormalized single-ion propagator

$$ \tilde G_0(\wn) \;\equiv\; \frac{G(\wn)}{1 - K(\wn) G(\wn)} \;=\; \frac{G_0(\wn)}{1+\Sigma(\wn)}, \tag{32} $$

J 2.30 (the second equality follows by substituting (30)), the theory is exactly RPA in form, (14), with \( G_0 \to \tilde G_0 \): all \( 1/z \) physics is carried by the multiplicative renormalization \( 1+\Sigma(\wn) \) of the single-ion response. This is the practically important statement: in a numerical implementation one divides the single-ion susceptibility by \( 1+\Sigma(\omega) \) and re-runs the RPA. Note the implicit self-consistency loop: \( \Sigma \) depends on \( K \) through \( \lambda_{1,2} \), \( K \) depends on \( G(\mathbf q) \) through (16), and \( G(\mathbf q) \) depends on \( \Sigma \) through (31); the equations are solved by iteration (Sec. 10).

7  The sum rule

Derived in full → J 2.23

The frequency sum of \( G \) is the equal-time correlator: since \( [\tilde J_x, \tilde J_x] = 0 \) there is no ordering ambiguity at \( \tau\to0 \), and

$$ \begin{aligned} \frac1\beta \sum_n G(\wn) &= G(\tau=0) = -\big\langle \tilde J_x^2 \big\rangle = -\big[ \langle J_x^2\rangle - \langle J_x\rangle^2 \big] \\[.3em] &= -\big[ M_0^2 - m^2 n_{01}^2 \big] = -\big[ M^2 + m^2 (1-n_{01}^2) \big], \end{aligned} \tag{33} $$

using the operator identity (2) and \( \langle J_x\rangle = m\, n_{01} \). For \( m=0 \): \( \frac1\beta\sum_n G(\wn) = -M^2 \) J 2.23 — an exact, temperature-independent constraint on the true \( G \), and the natural quality check in a numerical implementation. One caveat for implementations: Jensen notes that the truncated series (24) satisfies (33) strictly — that is the content of (26) — whereas the Dyson-resummed self-consistent form (30)–(31) satisfies it only to high numerical accuracy, so a small converged residual is expected rather than a bug. Convergence: \( g \sim 2n_{01}\Delta/\omega_n^2 \), so the sum converges absolutely and no convergence factor is needed. Check at zeroth order: \( \frac1\beta\sum_n g = n_{01}\coth(\beta\Delta/2) = 1 \), and the \( h \) term contributes \( -m^2(1-n_{01}^2) \) from \( n=0 \), so \( G_0 \) saturates (33) identically — which is why the \( 1/z \) correction had to obey (26).

8  Internal energy and heat capacity

8.1  Exact energy formula

Derived in full → J 2.21

Interaction energy (exact). With \( \langle \tilde J_{ix}\tilde J_{jx}\rangle = -G(ij,\tau=0) \) and \( \sum_j \Jc(ij) e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf R_{ij}} = \Jc(\mathbf q) \):

$$ \begin{aligned} \langle \mathcal H_1 \rangle &= -\tfrac12\sum_{ij}\Jc(ij)\,\langle\tilde J_{ix}\tilde J_{jx}\rangle = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n \frac1N \sum_{\mathbf q} \Jc(\mathbf q)\, G(\mathbf q,\wn) \\[.3em] &= \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n K(\wn)\, G(\wn), \end{aligned} \tag{34} $$

by the definition (16) of \( K \) — no approximation involved.

Single-site energy (exact, via the equation of motion). By (4), the exact Heisenberg equation for \( X \) is governed by \( \Delta a_{11} \) alone: \( \dot X(\tau) = [\mathcal H, X] = \Delta\,(b^\dagger - b) \), hence \( \Delta X - \dot X = 2\Delta\, b \). Consider \( Q(\tau) = \langle T\,(\Delta X - \dot X)(\tau)\, X(0)\rangle \). Since \( G_X(\beta^-) = G_X(0^-) = G_X(0^+) \) (bosonic periodicity, commuting equal-time operators), partial integration gives no boundary terms and \( \langle T\dot X(\tau)X(0)\rangle \) transforms to \( -\wn\, \langle TXX\rangle(\wn) \), so \( Q(\wn) = (\Delta+\wn)\langle TXX\rangle(\wn) \), i.e.

$$ Q(\wn) = -\,(\Delta + \wn)\, G_X(\wn), \qquad G_X = G/M^2 \;\;(m=0). $$

Its equal-time limit from \( \tau\to0^- \) (convergence factor \( e^{\wn 0^+} \)) is \( \langle X\cdot 2\Delta b\rangle = 2\Delta\langle b^\dagger b\rangle = 2\Delta\langle a_{11}\rangle \), the exact upper-level population. Therefore

$$ \langle \mathcal H_0\rangle = N\Delta\,\langle a_{11}\rangle = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n e^{\wn 0^+}\,\Big[-\frac{\Delta+\wn}{M^2}\Big]\, G(\wn) . \tag{35} $$

Check at MF level: \( (\Delta+\wn)\,g = 2n_{01}\Delta/(\Delta-\wn) \), and \( \frac1\beta\sum_n e^{\wn0^+}(\Delta-\wn)^{-1} = n_B(\Delta) \) with \( n_{01}\, n_B(\Delta) = n_1 \), so (35) gives \( N\Delta n_1 \)  ✓. Subtracting the MF energy \( U_{\rm MF} = N\Delta n_1 \) (the MF interaction energy vanishes in the paramagnet):

$$ \boxed{\; \delta U = \frac{N}{2\beta}\sum_n \Big[ K(\wn) - \frac{\Delta+\wn}{M^2} \Big] G(\wn) \;-\; N\, n_1 \Delta \;} \tag{36} $$

J 2.21 — exact given \( G \); the frequency-odd piece of \( \wn G \) requires the convergence factor.

8.2  Evaluation to first order in 1/z

Two terms derived; population term is Jensen’s resummed form → J 2.22

Insert \( G = G_0 - G_0[KG_0+\Sigma] \) into (36). The \( -(\Delta+\wn)G_0/M^2 \) piece cancels \( -Nn_1\Delta \) (the MF check above); the term \( \frac{1}{\beta}\sum_n K G_0 = -M^2\lambda_1 \) is exact by definition (23); and the remainder of the interaction part is kept as \( \frac1\beta\sum_n K(G-G_0) \). The population shift extracted from (35) resums, at leading order in \(1/z\), into a renormalization of the population difference \( n_{01}\to n_{01}/(1+\alpha) \) (the same \( 1+\Sigma(0)\)-type factor that renormalizes the static single-ion response), i.e. \( \delta\langle a_{11}\rangle = \tfrac12\,\alpha\, n_{01}/(1+\alpha) \). Together J 2.22:

$$ \delta U = \frac N2 \left\{ \frac{\alpha\, n_{01}\Delta}{1+\alpha} \;-\; M^2 \lambda_1 \;+\; \frac1\beta\sum_n K(\wn)\big[ G(\wn) - G_0(\wn) \big] \right\}, \tag{37} $$

and the fluctuation contribution to the heat capacity is \( \delta C = d(\delta U)/dT \), to be added to the MF (Schottky) term. Note that \( \delta U \) itself does not vanish as \( T\to0 \): the \( \lambda_p \) tend to finite zero-temperature integrals, and \( \delta U(0) \) is the zero-point energy shift produced by the fluctuations (Jensen quotes \( \delta U/N = -9.06\;\mu\)eV for HoF3 in the low-temperature limit). It is the heat-capacity correction \( \delta C \) that vanishes exponentially as \( T\to0 \), as it must for a gapped singlet ground state.

9  Ordered phase and finite field (m ≠ 0)

9.1  The modified mean field

Rationale derived → J 2.24, 2.25

Beyond MF theory the fluctuations shift the moment, so if the perturbation split is made around the bare MF Hamiltonian, the series contains nonzero first cumulants \( \langle \tilde J_x\rangle_0 \neq 0 \) (“tadpole” insertions) at every order. Jensen instead defines the single-site Hamiltonian by the requirement that the MF expectation value equals the exact one J 2.24,

$$ \langle J_x \rangle = \langle J_x \rangle_0 = m\, n_{01}, \tag{38} $$

which cancels all tadpole insertions identically. The field entering the single-ion problem is then not the naive \( H_0 \), defined by \( g\mu_B H_0 = g\mu_B H + \Jc(\mathbf 0)\langle J_x\rangle \) J 2.25 (with \( H \) the applied field), but a shifted \( H_{\rm MF} = H_0 - \delta H \). The shift \( \delta H \) is itself \( O(1/z) \), and the residual perturbation \( \mathcal H_2 = -g\mu_B\,\delta H \sum_i \tilde J_{ix} \) contributes to \( G \) only at relative order \( (\delta H)^2 = O(1/z^2) \), so to first order in \( 1/z \) one simply solves the single-ion problem in the field \( H_{\rm MF} \) determined self-consistently below (Sec. 9.3). All quantities \( \Delta, M, m, n_{01}, g, h, G_0 \) are understood as functions of \( H_{\rm MF} \).

9.2  Elastic (m²) modifications of Σ

The additional contributions are evaluated with one stated simplification: in the \( n' \) sums, factors \( g(\wm \pm \wn) \) are replaced by their static value \( g(\wm) \) — exact at \( \omega_n = 0 \), approximate at finite frequency — and the purely elastic (zero-frequency) sector is treated in a resummed, static approximation. The result replaces (24)–(25) by J 2.26, 2.27

9.3  Self-consistent relation between the applied field and HMF

Derived in full → J 2.31–2.33

The MF moment is generated by the single-ion problem in the field \( H_{\rm MF} \); its differential static response is the single-site susceptibility \( -G_0(0; H_{\rm MF}) \) (zero-frequency, \( \mathbf q \)-independent), so J 2.31

$$ d\langle J_x\rangle_0 = -\,g\mu_B\, G_0(0; H_{\rm MF})\, dH_{\rm MF} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \langle J_x \rangle = -\,g\mu_B \int_0^{H_{\rm MF}} G_0(0; H')\, dH'. \tag{43} $$

On the other hand, the exact differential response to the applied field is the bulk \( \mathbf q=\mathbf 0 \) static susceptibility: \( d\langle J_x\rangle = -g\mu_B\, G(\mathbf 0, 0)\, dH \). From (2.25), \( dH_0 = dH + [\Jc(\mathbf 0)/g\mu_B]\, d\langle J_x\rangle = \big[1 - \Jc(\mathbf 0) G(\mathbf 0,0)\big] dH \). Equating the two expressions for \( d\langle J_x\rangle \) (using (38)) gives J 2.32

$$ G(\mathbf 0, 0) = G_0(0; H_{\rm MF})\, \big[ 1 - \Jc(\mathbf 0)\, G(\mathbf 0,0) \big]\, \frac{dH_{\rm MF}}{dH_0}. \tag{44} $$

Now use the effective-medium/RPA structure at \( \mathbf q = \mathbf 0,\ \omega=0 \): from (17) and (32), \( G(\mathbf 0,0) = \tilde G_0 / [1 + \Jc(\mathbf 0)\tilde G_0] \) with \( \tilde G_0 \equiv G(0)/[1-K(0)G(0)] \), hence \( 1 - \Jc(\mathbf 0) G(\mathbf 0,0) = [1+\Jc(\mathbf 0)\tilde G_0]^{-1} \). Substituting into (44), the factors \( [1+\Jc(\mathbf 0)\tilde G_0] \) cancel and the differential equation separates:

$$ \tilde G_0(0;H_{\rm MF}) = G_0(0; H_{\rm MF})\, \frac{dH_{\rm MF}}{dH_0} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\; H_0 = \int_0^{H_{\rm MF}} \frac{G_0(0; H')}{\tilde G_0(0; H')}\, dH' \;} \tag{45} $$

J 2.33. Together with \( g\mu_B H_0 = g\mu_B H + \Jc(\mathbf 0)\langle J_x\rangle \) and (43), equation (45) determines \( H_{\rm MF} \) (hence \( \delta H = H_0 - H_{\rm MF} \)) for a given applied field \( H \). Since \( \tilde G_0 = G_0/(1+\Sigma) \) away from the elastic subtleties, the integrand is essentially \( 1 + \Sigma(0; H') \): the fluctuation renormalization accumulated along the magnetization curve.

Pitfall: equation (45) is a branch integral, not an endpoint formula

Equation (44) is local, but its integrated form (45) is global: at every intermediate \(H'\), \(G_0\) and \(\tilde G_0\) must describe the same continuously selected self-consistent state. If the nonlinear medium equations possess folds or multiple sheets, a perfectly regular terminal segment does not by itself determine \(H_0\). Deleting unconverged points or joining independently reached sheets silently changes the integral and can shift, create, or remove a solution of the ordered-field equation.

For example, if the selected component is certified only above \(H_e>0\), the exact decomposition is

$$ H_0(H_{\rm MF})=A_e+ \int_{H_e}^{H_{\rm MF}}\frac{G_0(0;H')}{\tilde G_0(0;H')}\,dH', \qquad A_e\equiv\int_0^{H_e}\frac{G_0(0;H')}{\tilde G_0(0;H')}\,dH'. $$

The upper integral does not determine the missing constant \(A_e\). A strict use of (45) therefore requires a single admissible branch reaching the symmetry point, or an independently derived thermodynamic rule for crossing components and fixing the corresponding area. If neither can be established, the mathematically honest alternatives are to withhold a strict solution or to introduce an explicit, physically constrained completion (or bounds) for \(A_e\) and report the resulting sensitivity; setting it to zero or bridging a gap implicitly is an additional assumption, not equation (45).

There is a related boundary trap. At a continuous transition the nonzero ordered solution merges with the symmetry solution, while the linearized inverse susceptibility (the paramagnetic “mass”) passes through zero. The derivative of a direct order-parameter fixed-point map then approaches unity, so ordinary iteration becomes critically slow even when a nonzero root exists. Phase selection should therefore come from the sign of the linearized mass, followed on the ordered side by continuous branch tracking and a bracketed solve of the nonzero residual. Failure of fixed-point iteration near unit slope is not evidence that the ordered branch is absent.

Continuous-transition specialization used by the tensor spectra

At a continuous ordering boundary the longitudinal order parameter and its molecular field are infinitesimal. Linearizing (45) about the paramagnetic state makes the ratio in its integrand constant to leading order,

$$ H_0 = \frac{G_0(0;0)}{\tilde G_0(0;0)}H_{\rm MF}+O(H_{\rm MF}^3) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{\rm MF}=\frac{\tilde G_0(0;0)}{G_0(0;0)}H_0+O(H_0^3). $$

For zero applied longitudinal field, \(g\mu_BH_0=\Jc(\mathbf q_{\rm ord})\langle J_x\rangle\). The ordered single-ion equation can therefore be written, to the boundary-exact linear order, with

$$ \boxed{\;\Jc_{\rm MF}=\Jc(\mathbf q_{\rm ord})\, \frac{\tilde G_0(0;0)}{G_0(0;0)}\;}, \qquad g\mu_BH_{\rm MF}=\Jc_{\rm MF}\langle J_x\rangle . $$

In the multilevel tensor implementation the ratio is evaluated from the rejected, converged paramagnetic point using its actual static convention, \(\tilde\chi_{0,cc}=\chi_{{\rm dom},cc}/(1+\Sigma_0)+\chi_{{\rm rest},cc}\), so \(\Jc_{\rm MF}^{cc}=\Jc^{cc}\tilde\chi_{0,cc}/\chi_{0,cc}\). This is not the generally incorrect shortcut \(\Jc^{cc}/(1+\Sigma_0)\), because the weak-transition remainder is left bare. The relation is exact at the continuous boundary and is the appropriate ordered-side closure for a near-critical soft-mode scan; finite moments farther inside the ordered phase formally require the full field integral (45).

10  Computational recipe and generalization

For a given temperature (paramagnetic phase; generalization below), the self-consistency loop is:

  1. Solve the single-ion MF problem: \( \Delta, M, m, n_{01} \Rightarrow G_0(\wn) \) from (11), on a grid of Matsubara frequencies (the \( \lambda_p \) sums converge like \( \omega_n^{-4} \), rapidly).
  2. Initialize with RPA: \( \Sigma = 0 \), \( G(\mathbf q,\wn) \) from (31).
  3. Brillouin-zone averages: \( G(\wn) \) and \( K(\wn) \) from (16). For dipolar \( \Jc(\mathbf q) \) use Ewald summation; the averages are efficiently done once per lattice via a density-of-states representation of \( \Jc(\mathbf q) \).
  4. Update \( \lambda_1, \lambda_2 \) from (23), then \( \gamma(\wn) \), \( \alpha \), \( \Sigma(\wn) \) from (25), (29), (24).
  5. Recompute \( G(\mathbf q,\wn) \) from (31); iterate 3–5 to convergence.
  6. Checks: sum rule (33) at every iteration (exact for the truncated series; the resummed self-consistent \( G \) obeys it only to high accuracy, so tolerate a small residual); \( \delta U \) from (36) vs. (37); near criticality, \( T_c \) is where \( 1+\Sigma(0)+\Jc(\mathbf q_{\rm ord}) G_0(0) \to 0 \), i.e. the MF/RPA \( T_c \) is suppressed by the factor \( 1+\Sigma(0) \) renormalizing the single-ion response.

10.1  Locating the phase boundary from the paramagnetic side

The equations determine the boundary through the zero-frequency inverse mass

$$ c(T,H) \equiv 1+\Sigma(0)-\Jc(\mathbf q_{\rm ord})\,\chi_0(0). $$

Here \(c>0\) is a stable paramagnet, \(c=0\) is the 1/z instability, and a converged continuation with \(c<0\) is unstable toward the ordered phase. This static criterion is logically prior to real-axis continuation: neither the brightest pixel of a broadened spectrum nor the frequency of a discretized peak defines the thermodynamic boundary. Likewise, setting \(\Sigma=0\) changes the theory and gives the distinct MF/RPA condition \(1-\Jc\chi_0=0\).

A robust numerical boundary search must also distinguish a physical sign from failure of the paramagnetic fixed point. Near criticality the effective-medium/self-energy iteration slows and may cease to converge. Only a converged, finite value of \(c\) should therefore vote on the phase; a non-converged point is unknown, not automatically ordered. The projected-spin implementation applies the following recipe:

  1. Fixed temperature: begin at a high field known to be a converged \(c>0\) paramagnet, scan downward coarsely, and retain the last trustworthy positive point. Skip non-converged samples while searching farther into the low-field side for a trustworthy \(c\le0\) endpoint.
  2. Refinement: refine a converged sign-changing bracket with safeguarded regula falsi, constrained away from the bracket endpoints. If an interior trial does not converge, retry at the midpoint; if that also fails, interpolate only from the converged endpoints.
  3. Paramagnetic-edge fallback: when no converged low-field endpoint can be recovered, bisect the yes/no predicate “converged with \(c>0\).” Report this explicitly as the lower edge of paramagnetic convergence, not as an equally controlled root of \(c=0\).
  4. Fixed field: where a field cut meets the boundary tangentially (notably near the zero-field \(T_c\)), sample an adaptive temperature window instead. Discard invalid samples, choose a converged low-\(T\) \(c<0\) to high-\(T\) \(c>0\) crossing, and refine it. Multiple crossings indicate possible re-entrance and must be reported rather than silently averaged.
  5. Zero field: use the closed critical self-energy \(\Sigma_c=\langle\Jc_\nu/(\Jc_0-\Jc_\nu)\rangle\) and solve \(\Jc_0\chi_0(0,T)=1+\Sigma_c(T)\) directly for \(T_c(0)\).
Interpretation The field-driven quantum critical point is obtained as the low-temperature limit of the fixed-\(T\) field boundary. A finite-temperature solver should not silently label that result “exactly \(T=0\).” Nor should an ordered-state phase switch be substituted for this paramagnetic-side mass criterion unless the ordered thermodynamics have been independently made consistent with the same approximation.

Ordered phase / finite field: add the outer loop of Sec. 9.3 determining \( H_{\rm MF} \) from \( H \) via (43), (45), and use the elastic-sector forms (39)–(42).

Generalization to realistic level schemes (from Sec. III of the paper, framework-level): the entire construction survives with the single-ion response \( G_0 \) replaced by the full Cartesian susceptibility tensor of the actual crystal-field (+ hyperfine) level scheme, and multiple sublattices handled by the standard RPA matrix inversion; the \(1/z\) renormalization enters as division of the susceptibility components deriving from the dominant singlet–singlet (or doublet) transition by \( 1+\Sigma(\omega) \), with \( \Sigma \) computed from that sector; Jensen renormalizes only those components, neglecting the \(1/z\) corrections to the weaker transitions. Hyperfine coupling \( A\,\mathbf I\cdot\mathbf J \) enhances the static single-ion response, \( \tilde\chi_{\rm eff} = \tilde\chi\,(1 + A^2 \tilde\chi\, \chi_I) \) with \( \chi_I \) the nuclear Curie susceptibility, and correspondingly \( K(0) \to K(0)(1 + A^2\tilde\chi\,\chi_I) \) in the elastic sector — the mechanism by which nuclear moments shift \( T_c \) in LiHoF4-type systems.

Electronuclear mode identification and field-sweep bookkeeping: with hyperfine coupling treated inside the single-ion Hamiltonian, the poles of the full response are hybrid electron–nuclear modes. The collective instability is the eigenmode whose RPA denominator vanishes; near the quantum critical field this is the low-energy electronuclear branch. Hybridization redistributes electronic spectral weight among several poles, so the higher, predominantly electronic branch is not required to close at the transition. In the 136-state A1 field-sweep implementation, the dominant sector is consequently represented by the fixed-rank, field-adapted lowest 16 states (the ground electronic doublet times all eight nuclear states), while the remainder stays bare. A fixed energy threshold is not an invariant definition of this manifold: as the levels move with field it can change rank and create artificial discontinuities even when the physical gap separating states 16 and 17 remains open.

Bookkeeping for the tensor (dipole–dipole) coupling — the LiHoF4 case: the projection of Sec. 1.1 dictates where each block of the 3×3 tensor \( \Jc_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q) \) goes when the theory is applied with the Ising axis along \( c \), as done by H. M. Rønnow et al., Phys. Rev. B 75, 054426 (2007) (“R 2007”). (i) The \( cc \) block is the fluctuation channel: the expansion is of \( G(ij,\tau) = -\langle T_\tau\, \tilde J_{iz}(\tau_1)\tilde J_{jz}(\tau_2)\rangle \) [R 2007 eq. (5)], and the “\( \Jc(\mathbf q) \)” entering the effective-medium equations (16)–(17) means the eigenvalue branches of the 4×4 \( cc \) sublattice block of the dipole + exchange tensor [R 2007 eqs. (7)–(9)]. (ii) The transverse \( aa \) block acts as a static mean field \( \Jc_{aa}(\mathbf 0)\langle J_x\rangle \) added to the applied transverse field in the single-ion problem (it recalibrates \( \Delta \) and \( M^2 \) at every field); its own 1/z renormalization is neglected, justified because the internal transverse field is only about 5% of the applied field at the largest fields considered [R 2007 Sec. II B]. (iii) Off-diagonal blocks (\( ac \), …) and all coupling paths through the higher crystal-field levels have no matrix elements inside the doublet; statically they act like internally generated transverse fields [R 2007 Sec. I], and dynamically they enter the noninteracting susceptibility through doublet↔higher-level transitions — kept in a full-level-scheme \( \chi_0 \) but excluded from \( \Sigma \). R 2007 bounds that share of \( \chi_0^{cc}(0) \) at below 1% at zero frequency at maximum field, so computing \( \Sigma \) from the two-level sector and dividing only \( \chi_0^{cc} \) by \( 1+\Sigma \) — the “dominant components only” rule above — is quantitatively safe.

Validity: the theory is exact through \( O(1/z) \); dropped terms are \( O(1/z^2) \). It is not a critical theory — exponents remain MF-like — but for three-dimensional dipolar Ising systems (marginal dimensionality) the true critical behaviour differs from MF only by logarithmic corrections, so the \(1/z\)-renormalized MF/RPA description is quantitatively accurate essentially everywhere, including the location of \( T_c \) and the size of its fluctuation suppression.

11  Off-diagonal dipolar coupling within the 1/z expansion

Applied to LiHoF4 (R 2007; Sec. 10), the scalar theory reproduces the transverse-field boundary at low temperature but overshoots the zero-field transition: \( T_c(0) = 1.743 \) K against the experimental 1.53 K. Dollberg, Andresen and Schechter [Phys. Rev. B 105, L180413 (2022)] and Dollberg and Schechter [arXiv:2308.10095] (together “DS2023”) attribute this high-temperature discrepancy to the off-diagonal dipolar (ODD) blocks \( \Jc^{c\alpha} \), \( \alpha\in\{a,b\} \) — precisely the blocks that the projection of Sec. 1.1 removes from the fluctuation expansion — and find that including them in a mean-field + Monte-Carlo treatment repairs the boundary. This section incorporates that claim into the present framework as a physics principle only: none of the reference algorithm is imported, the mechanism is re-expressed in the 1/z language of Secs. 2–6, and the corrected boundary is required to emerge from the otherwise-unchanged machinery with no fitted factor. That emergence — rather than agreement by construction — is the sharpest available test of whether the underlying physics is actually understood, and it is meaningful precisely because the 1/z expansion is a fundamentally different framework from the reference treatment.

11.1  The ODD blocks and the \( \mathbf q=\mathbf 0 \) symmetry argument

Restore the tensor coupling of Sec. 1.1 with the Ising axis along \( c \). Beyond the diagonal transverse block \( \Jc_{aa} \) (a static mean field, Sec. 10) the dipolar tensor carries off-diagonal blocks \( \Jc^{c\alpha}(ij) \), \( \alpha\in\{a,b\} \), coupling the ordering fluctuation \( \tilde J_{ic} \) to the transverse moments \( J_{j\alpha} \). For the four Ho sublattices of LiHoF4 each Fourier-transformed block \( \Jc^{c\alpha}(\mathbf q) \) is a \( 4\times4 \) matrix over sublattice pairs \( (s,s') \); all products and traces below are over this sublattice index. Two single-ion facts fix how these blocks can act. First, the transverse response of Ho3+ is Van Vleck: the first excited crystal-field level lies ≈10.8 K above the ground doublet, far above \( k_BT \) at the transition, so the \( (a,b) \) channel is fast and gapped — it has no slow dynamics of its own at the ordering scale and follows the \( c \)-axis fluctuations adiabatically. Second, the two-fold rotation about \( c \) in the Ho site symmetry sends \( J_{a,b}\to-J_{a,b} \) while preserving \( J_c \), forcing \( \Jc^{c\alpha}(\mathbf 0)=0 \) identically (and along the on-axis \( \mathbf q\to0 \) limit). The ODD blocks therefore have no mean-field effect whatsoever: a uniform \( c \) polarization generates no net transverse field on any sublattice. They act only through fluctuations — a \( J_c \) fluctuation on one ion polarizes the Van Vleck transverse moment of a neighbour, and that induced moment couples back to the \( c \) channel of a third ion: the three-spin mechanism of DS2023, resummed below as a mediated pair interaction.

11.2  Gaussian elimination of the fast channel: mediated coupling and self-site subtraction

Because the transverse channel is fast and, over the internal-field scales generated here, linear, it can be integrated out at Gaussian level. The elimination yields exactly two renormalized inputs to the machinery of Secs. 5–6 and changes none of its formulae. The first is a mediated pair coupling (the implementation’s label E1):

$$ \delta\Jc^{cc}(\mathbf q) \;=\; \sum_{\alpha\beta\in\{a,b\}} \Jc^{c\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,\big[\chi_\perp\big]_{\alpha\beta}\,\Jc^{\beta c}(\mathbf q), \tag{47} $$

with \( \chi_\perp \) the static single-ion transverse susceptibility — positive-definite, so \( \delta\Jc^{cc}(\mathbf q)\succeq0 \) (a mediated interaction lowers the energy). Crucially, \( \chi_\perp \) is evaluated in the framework’s own single-ion problem: the full 136-state electronuclear susceptibility (measured 17.64 meV−1 at 1.53 K), not the 3-state \( 2\rho^2/\Delta\approx11 \) meV−1 of the reference model. The convention \( \Jc(ii)=0 \) (Sec. 0) must be preserved: (47) contains an unphysical self-interaction — an ion polarizing its own transverse moment, which is a single-ion renormalization already contained in \( G_0 \), not a pair coupling. It is removed by subtracting the Brillouin-zone average of each sublattice diagonal (labels E4, E5),

$$ \delta\Jc_{ss}(\mathbf q)\;\longrightarrow\;\delta\Jc_{ss}(\mathbf q)-d, \qquad d\;=\;\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q'}\big[\delta\Jc\big]_{ss}(\mathbf q'), \tag{48} $$

(\( d \) is equal on the four sublattices by symmetry; measured \( d = 0.483\ \mu \)eV at \( T_c \), a 7.5% reduction of the uniform dipolar \( cc \) coupling), with the uniform coupling correspondingly shifted, \( \Jc(\mathbf 0)\to\Jc(\mathbf 0)-d \). The renormalized inputs then feed the unchanged theory: the corrected mode spectrum — the eigenvalue branches of \( \Jc_{cc}(\mathbf q)+\delta\Jc(\mathbf q) \) — enters the self-energy exactly as in Secs. 5–6, and the shifted \( \Jc(\mathbf 0) \) enters the criticality condition \( \Jc(\mathbf 0)\,\chi_0(0)=1+\Sigma(0) \).

11.3  The uniform-shift theorem

Derived in full — this work

At the zero-field transition the critical self-energy has a closed form (R 2007 eq. (10)); with \( \langle\cdot\rangle=\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q,\nu} \) the average over the Brillouin zone and the four \( cc \) branches \( \Jc_\nu(\mathbf q) \), \( \Jc_0\equiv\Jc(\mathbf 0) \), and \( \chi_0\equiv-G_0(0) \) the static single-ion susceptibility:

$$ \Jc_0\,\chi_0 \;=\; 1+\Sigma_c, \qquad \Sigma_c \;=\; \Big\langle \frac{\Jc_\nu}{\Jc_0-\Jc_\nu}\Big\rangle. \tag{49} $$

Theorem. A simultaneous uniform shift \( \Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\to\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)+d \) and \( \Jc_0\to\Jc_0+d \) leaves the criticality (49) invariant, for any constant \( d \). Proof. From (49), \( \Sigma_c=\Jc_0\big\langle(\Jc_0-\Jc_\nu)^{-1}\big\rangle-1 \), hence \( \big\langle(\Jc_0-\Jc_\nu)^{-1}\big\rangle=(1+\Sigma_c)/\Jc_0 \). The shift leaves every denominator \( \Jc_0-\Jc_\nu \) unchanged, so the shifted self-energy is \( \Sigma_c'=\Sigma_c+d\,(1+\Sigma_c)/\Jc_0 \), and the shifted condition collapses to the unshifted one (\( \chi_0 \) is a single-ion quantity, untouched by the shift):

$$ (\Jc_0+d)\,\chi_0 \;=\; 1+\Sigma_c' \;=\; (1+\Sigma_c)\,\frac{\Jc_0+d}{\Jc_0} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Jc_0\,\chi_0=1+\Sigma_c. \qquad\blacksquare \tag{50} $$

Physical content: an on-site (\( \mathbf q \)-independent) constant in the coupling — exactly what the self-site subtraction (48) removes — has zero \( T_c \) content in the 1/z criticality. The subtraction in the mode spectrum and the \( -d \) reduction of \( \Jc(\mathbf 0) \) are two bookkeeping faces of one constant, applied once; the implementation confirms the theorem numerically to machine precision (identical \( T_c \) with and without a literal \( +d\,\mathbb 1 \) shift of all couplings). Note also what the theorem is about: it is a statement on the fluctuation sum in (49), so a framework whose critical condition carries no such sum — a pure mean-field criticality — has no analogue of it.

11.4  Retardation: elastic-sector exactness and the measured null

The elimination behind (47) used the static \( \chi_\perp(0) \) — an adiabatic approximation, since the mediating channel does have structure at the Matsubara frequencies: the measured retardation ratio \( r(\omega_1)=\chi_\perp(i\omega_1)/\chi_\perp(0) \) is 0.679 at \( \omega_1=0.828 \) meV (1.53 K). The retarded form — \( \chi_\perp(\wn) \) channel by channel in (47) — was therefore implemented and measured, not assumed away: it moves \( T_c \) by 0.022 mK, far below the 10 mK decision threshold. The reason is structural. Criticality is an \( \omega=0 \) statement: the ordering instability is decided in the elastic \( n=0 \) Matsubara sector, where \( \chi_\perp(i\omega_0)=\chi_\perp(0) \) is exact; the \( n\ge1 \) sectors reach the critical condition only through the \( \lambda_p \) moments (23) of the \( O(1/z) \) self-energy, where retardation is a small correction to a correction — and that is what the 0.022 mK measures. The static form is kept as the default; the retarded variant is retained as an option.

11.5  Variable moments: internal-field covariance and quenched dressing

The integrated-out channel has a second consequence (DS2023’s “variable moments”): each ion sits in a fluctuating internal transverse field \( h_i^\alpha=\sum_j \Jc^{\alpha c}(ij)\,\delta J_{jc} \) generated by its neighbours’ \( c \)-axis fluctuations. At Gaussian level this field is zero-mean with covariance fixed by the equal-time \( cc \) structure factor (label E3),

$$ C_{\alpha\beta} \;=\; \frac{1}{4N}\sum_{\mathbf q} \Tr\Big[\,\Jc^{\alpha c}(\mathbf q)\; S_{cc}(\mathbf q)\; \Jc^{c\beta}(\mathbf q)\,\Big], \tag{51} $$

where \( S_{cc}(\mathbf q) \) is the equal-time correlation matrix of the converged 1/z solution itself — the theory supplies its own noise spectrum, and the loop is closed self-consistently (the dressed single-ion parameters change the structure factor, which re-evaluates \( C \), iterated to a fixed point). The doublet parameters \( (\Delta, M^2, n_{01}) \) are then averaged over the quenched Gaussian \( (h_a,h_b)\sim N(0,C) \) (numerically, on a Gauss–Hermite grid), and the averaged response is re-fitted to the two-level form of Sec. 3, so the \( \Sigma \) algebra of Sec. 6 is untouched. Two caveats attach. Averaging the response and averaging the parameters are inequivalent operations — the disorder-averaged propagator \( \bar g(\wn) \) is the physically correct object and is what is re-fitted (measured inequivalence in the effective splitting at the validation point: 0.153888 vs 0.152857 meV). And the quenched-static treatment — freezing thermal and quantum internal-field fluctuations into a classical distribution, then recompressing the average into a single pole — is the least rigorous step of this whole section, and is flagged as such wherever it is used. Measured scales: equivalent internal field \( h_{\rm eq}\approx0.295 \) T; only ≈2.8% of the boundary suppression at the 0.5 T proxy (the mediated coupling of Sec. 11.2 carries the rest); at \( B=0 \) the dressed splitting is only ≈3.4× the hyperfine scale \( A \), so zero-field Tier-2 numbers are directional, not quantitative.

11.6  The naive mean-field inconsistency and the condition/Σ-space decomposition

In a mean-field picture one would compress Sec. 11.2 into the single statement “the uniform coupling is reduced by \( d \)”. Inside the 1/z criticality that half-step is inconsistent: reducing \( \Jc_0 \) by \( d \) while keeping the unmodified mode spectrum drives \( \Jc_0-d \) below the largest finite-\( \mathbf q \) coupling branch (96 modes exceed it on the 24³ validation grid) — a spurious finite-\( \mathbf q \) instability, i.e. the “uniform mode orders first” premise of (49) fails. The full mediated coupling avoids this automatically: the same \( \chi_\perp \) that produces \( d \) also produces the \( \mathbf q \)-structure of \( \delta\Jc(\mathbf q) \), which lowers the peak finite-\( \mathbf q \) mode in lockstep (0.006143 → 0.005727 meV at 24³) and keeps the uniform mode critical. The \( -d \) reduction and the \( \delta\Jc(\mathbf q) \) structuring are therefore physically inseparable, and an additive decomposition of \( \Delta T_c \) into “mean-field” plus “fluctuation” parts is ill-posed. The meaningful decomposition is sequential in condition/Σ-space: shifting the criticality condition only (the analogue of the reference mean-field picture) gives \( T_c=1.615 \) K; shifting the self-energy only (the fluctuation channel at its own consistent configuration) gives \( T_c=1.630 \) K; against the unshifted 1.743 K and the full 1.509 K, the two legs recombine with a closure defect of only +0.008 K.

11.7  Relation to the reference framework

Nothing of the reference algorithm is imported: no Schrieffer–Wolff projection onto an effective spin-½ and no 3-state truncation (the single ion and \( \chi_\perp \) here are the full 136-state electronuclear problem, hyperfine exact and non-perturbative); no Monte Carlo; no longitudinal rescaling factor (their 0.805) — in this framework the \( c \)-axis fluctuation renormalization is precisely \( \Sigma \)’s job (Sec. 6), and importing the factor would double-count the fluctuations; and no perturbative temperature-dependent hyperfine renormalization. Reference numbers enter in exactly two sanctioned ways: the convention-free geometric lattice sums of DS2023 (Suppl. Table I) as unit guards on the ODD blocks, themselves verified independently by Parseval/real-space identities; and the reference MF/MC results as qualitative comparators — reported against, never tuned to.

The two frameworks moreover differ in both directions, and neither subsumes the other: the reference Monte Carlo captures the non-Gaussian statistics of the internal transverse-field distribution but is classical (valid for \( B_x\lesssim1 \) T), while the present treatment is Gaussian in the transverse channel but quantum in the \( cc \) channel and exact in the single-ion Hilbert space. Consistently, two results above — the uniform-shift theorem (Sec. 11.3) and the naive-MF inconsistency (Sec. 11.6) — are visible only from the 1/z side, being statements about the fluctuation sum in (49) with no mean-field analogue: concrete evidence that the mechanism, not the algorithm, is what was transplanted.

11.8  Rigorous completion, and the emergent boundary

The Gaussian (adiabatic) elimination of the transverse channel is the controlled approximation of this section. A rigorous completion would require rebuilding the expansion of Secs. 2–6 for the coupled \( (c;a,b) \) problem instead of the projected scalar channel. The former A1/A2/A3 completion route has been abandoned; invzt_a_tier_guide.html is retained only as a historical interpretation aid for existing tensor code. No full-tensor completion is currently planned. The Gaussian-channel systematic is therefore unquantified, and the precision quoted below is a grid-convergence bar only.

Two bookkeeping relations of that route are fixed at framework level (2026-07-17), both immediate corollaries of Secs. 5–6. (i) Bridge medium. In the dominant-sector bridge — the multilevel rule of Sec. 10, \( \tilde{\boldsymbol\chi}_0 = \boldsymbol\chi_{\rm dom}/(1+\Sigma) + \boldsymbol\chi_{\rm rest} \), propagated through the full tensor RPA — the effective medium is defined against the renormalized propagator, exactly as in (18)/(32): with \( \tilde G_0 = -[\tilde\chi_0]_{cc} \) site-local and \( G_{\rm loc} \) the site-diagonal \( cc \) lattice propagator, \( G_{\rm loc} = \tilde G_0/(1+K\tilde G_0) \), i.e. \( K = 1/G_{\rm loc} - 1/\tilde G_0 \). Defining \( K \) against the bare \( G_0 \) instead (as the superseded draft did) is wrong at \( O(\chi_{\rm rest}\,\Sigma) \) and breaks the exact reduction to the scalar closed form when the weak transitions are dropped. (ii) Matrix resummation. Beyond two levels the first-order correction must be carried additively — a multi-pole \( G_0 \) has real zeros between its poles, where the ratio \( \Sigma = \mathcal V/G_0 \) acquires artificial poles — and the Dyson-resummed renormalized propagator is the manifestly Hermitian $$ \tilde G_0 \;=\; G_0\,\big(G_0+\mathcal V\big)^{-1}G_0 , $$ with \( \mathcal V_{\mu\nu}(i\omega_n) \) the component-labelled generalization of the \( S_4 \) term of Sec. 6 (the connected single-site four-point cumulant contracted with the medium). The current multilevel formulation is summarized in invzt_a_tier_guide.html §4 and implemented by invz_tensor/invzt_vertex4.m, invz_tensor/invzt_gamma4.m, and invz_tensor/invzt_sigma_tensor.m. In the scalar two-level limit \( \mathcal V = G_0\Sigma \) and this collapses to (32), \( \tilde G_0 = G_0/(1+\Sigma) \); no step requires \( G_0^{-1} \) alone.
