The invz module 1/z effective-medium expansion for the dipolar Ising ferromagnet LiHoF4 — user manual & mathematical reference

This module computes the magnetic susceptibility of LiHoF4 one order beyond mean-field/RPA, by resumming the leading 1/z single-ion self-energy self-consistently with a scalar effective-medium lattice sum. It covers both phases — the paramagnetic transverse-field boundary Bc(T) and the real-frequency response χ″cc(q,ω), including the soft mode that dips at the quantum phase transition and hardens into the ferromagnet below Bc — following Rønnow et al., PRB 75, 054426 (2007) ("R 2007"), which applies Jensen's high-density expansion (J. Jensen, PRB 49, 11833 (1994)) to this material.

ScopeParamagnetic and ferromagnetic phases; scalar (cc-channel) self-energy — §1.7 explains how the full 3×3 dipolar tensor reduces to this single channel; transverse field B∥a by default, or rotated within the transverse plane via the validated phi_ab knob (§1.8). The ordered phase uses the bare mean-field order parameter with the elastic-sector self-energy (framework SS9.2, J 2.26–2.27); the applied-field magnetization curve (framework SS9.3, J 2.31–2.33) and free-energy consistency (framework SS9.4, J 2.34) are deferred. The full symbolic derivation lives in jensen_1z_framework.html at the repository root (equations there are cited in the code as "HTML eq N"). This page is the working manual: what to run, what it returns, and the equations each function evaluates. See Scope & limitations for what is deliberately left out.

1 · Physical picture & mathematical foundations

LiHoF4 is an Ising dipolar ferromagnet. Each Ho3+ ion has a crystal-field ground doublet (an effective two-level system along the c axis) that is split by a transverse field into a gap \(\Delta\), and a strongly hybridized 165Ho nuclear spin (\(I=7/2\)). At the temperatures of interest the electronic and nuclear states are treated together as one larger single-ion Hilbert space (17 electronic × 8 nuclear = 136 states). The magnetic transition is driven by the cc (longitudinal) component of the dipolar + exchange coupling \(\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\).

The theory has four moving parts, evaluated in the order below.

1.1 Single-ion two-level response

The dominant electronic doublet contributes a two-level response \(g\), with occupation difference \(n_{01}\) and static value \(g_0\):

$$g(z) = \frac{2\,n_{01}\,\Delta}{\Delta^{2}-z^{2}}, \qquad n_{01}=\tanh\frac{\Delta}{2k_BT}, \qquad g_0\equiv g(0)=\frac{2n_{01}}{\Delta}.$$

The bare local (cc) Green function is \(G_0=-\chi_0^{cc}\). In the pure two-level limit \(G_0=-M^{2}g\), where \(M^2=|\langle0|J_z|1\rangle|^{2}\) is the transition matrix element; in the code, \(G_0\) is the full electronuclear \(-\chi_0^{cc}\) (so hyperfine enhancement enters non-perturbatively), while the doublet parameters \((\Delta,M^{2},n_{01},g_0)\) set the self-energy.

1.2 Effective medium — the lattice sum

The self-consistent effective-medium coupling \(K(\wn)\) is the fixed point of (R 2007 eqs 7–9; \(\langle\cdot\rangle=\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q,\nu}\) over the Brillouin zone and the four cc sublattice branches):

$$G=\frac{G_0}{1+\Sigma+K\,G_0}, \qquad G_{\mathbf q}=\frac{G}{1+(\Jc_\nu-K)\,G}, \qquad K=\Big\langle\frac{\Jc_\nu\,G_{\mathbf q}}{G}\Big\rangle.$$

At fixed \(\Sigma\) this is not iterated: substituting \(G\) into \(G_{\mathbf q}\) cancels \(K\) (\(G_{\mathbf q}=G_0/(1+\Sigma+\Jc_\nu G_0)\)), leaving a linear equation for \(K\) that invz_emt_scalar solves in closed form,

$$K=\frac{A\,(1+\Sigma)}{1-A\,G_0}, \qquad A=\Big\langle\frac{\Jc_\nu}{1+\Sigma+\Jc_\nu\,G_0}\Big\rangle, \qquad G=\frac{G_0}{1+\Sigma+K\,G_0}.$$

So each medium solve is one direct evaluation per \(\Sigma\) (no inner fixed-point loop; frequencies may be blocked via opts.freq_block to bound peak memory), and at the true fixed point \(\langle G_{\mathbf q}\rangle=G\) exactly (the med.closure diagnostic is then machine-zero). A vanishing denominator \(1-A\,G_0\to0\) is the ordered-side instability where the paramagnetic medium ceases to exist; \(K\)/\(G\) go non-finite and med.converged=false reports it. Only the outer \(K\!\leftrightarrow\!\Sigma\) coupling (below) is iterated.

1.3 The 1/z self-energy

The Matsubara moments of \(K\) weighted by powers of \(g\),

$$\lambda_p=\frac1\beta\sum_n K(\wn)\,g(\wn)^{p},$$

fix the self-energy \(\Sigma=\alpha+\gamma\,g\) (Jensen; HTML eqs 21–27 / J 2.17–2.19):

$$\alpha=\frac{M^{2}}{n_{01}^{2}}\Big[\lambda_2-\half\big(g_0+\beta(1-n_{01}^{2})\big)\lambda_1\Big], \qquad \gamma=\frac{M^{2}}{n_{01}^{2}}\Big[\lambda_1-(1-n_{01}^{2})\,K\Big].$$

Equations 1.2 and 1.3 are coupled (\(K\) depends on \(\Sigma\), \(\Sigma\) depends on \(K\)); invz_solve_point iterates them to a fixed point at each \((T,B_x)\).

1.4 Renormalized susceptibility & criticality

On the real axis the self-energy divides the whole cc component, and the observable is a mode RPA over the coupling branches (HTML eqs 22–23, 29–30 / J 2.15–2.16, 2.18, 2.20, 2.30):

$$\Sigma(\omega)=\alpha+\frac{M^{2}}{n_{01}^{2}}\big[\lambda_1-(1-n_{01}^{2})K(\omega)\big]\,g(\omega+i\eta),\qquad \tilde\chi_0=\frac{\chi_0^{cc}}{1+\Sigma},\qquad \chi(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{\tilde\chi_0}{1-\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\,\tilde\chi_0}.$$

The paramagnetic instability (phase boundary) is where the uniform mode diverges,

$$\Jc(0)\,\chi_0^{cc}(0)=1+\Sigma(0)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \texttt{crit}\equiv 1+\Sigma(0)-\Jc(0)\,\chi_0^{cc}(0)=0,$$

and exactly at zero field the critical self-energy has the closed form (R 2007 eq 10)

$$\Sigma_c(0)=\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac{\Jc_{\mathbf q}}{\Jc(0)-\Jc_{\mathbf q}}.$$

1.5  Ferromagnetic phase (m ≠ 0)

Below \(B_c\) a spontaneous longitudinal moment \(m_0=\langle J_z\rangle\) develops: invz_single_ion (opts.order) solves the extra ordering mean field \(h_z=\Jc_{cc}(0)\langle J_z\rangle\) for its symmetry-broken branch. Spectral weight rotates from the transition matrix element into the diagonal moment, \(M^2+m^2=M_0^2\) fixed, and the self-energy gains an elastic correction (framework SS9.2, J 2.26–2.27):

$$\begin{aligned} \Sigma(\wn)&=(\alpha-\alpha_m)+\Big[\gamma(\wn)-\tfrac{2m^2}{M^2}\gamma(0)\Big]g(\wn), \\[.3em] \alpha_m&=\frac{m^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[\lambda_2-g_0\lambda_1+\tfrac{4}{g_0}\lambda_3-(1-n_{01}^2)\big(1+\tfrac12\beta\Delta\,n_{01}\big)K(0)g_0\Big]. \end{aligned}$$

This reduces to the paramagnetic \(\Sigma=\alpha+\gamma g\) exactly at \(m=0\). The elastic weight \(h(0)=\beta(1-n_{01}^2)\) is exponentially small at low \(T\) (\(n_{01}\to1\)), so the \(\xi\,h\) static correction (framework SS9.2, J 2.28) collapses to the ordinary Dyson form and \(\alpha_m\approx m^2[\lambda_2-g_0\lambda_1+(4/g_0)\lambda_3]\). The mode softens toward \(B_c\) from below (the ordered \(\texttt{crit}\to0\)) and hardens deeper into the ordered phase, tracing the soft-mode "V" of the quantum phase transition.

Option A boundary caveat The moment is the bare mean-field order parameter (the \(H_{\rm MF}\!\leftrightarrow\!H\) self-consistency of framework SS9.3, J 2.33 is deferred), so it onsets at the mean-field boundary, slightly above the 1/z boundary. Near \(B_c\) the FM and PM soft-mode branches therefore meet with a small step (~15 μeV at 0.31 K), not perfect continuity; deep in each phase the result is unaffected.

1.6  Uniform-mode coupling & sample shape

Ordering is driven by the uniform (\(\mathbf q\!=\!0\)) cc coupling \(\Jc(0)\), which carries the intrinsic dipole lattice sum, the exchange \(4\Jc_{12}\), and the Lorentz cavity term — always present, not a shape-dependent toggle:

$$\texttt{info.Jcc0}=\Jc_{\rm dip}^{cc}(0)+4\Jc_{12}+\underbrace{\frac{4\pi}{3V_c}\,g_{\rm fac}}_{\text{Lorentz cavity}}.$$

The sample shape — ion.demag and the spheroid aspect ratio ion.alpha (ellipsoid_demagn) — does not enter this expression at all: per R 2007, the demagnetizing field cancels from the critical condition (ordering occurs at \(\mathbf q\to0^+\), not strict \(\mathbf q=0\)), so info.Jcc0, the branch array Jnu, and the ordering-channel contribution to criticality are demag-invariant; \(T_c(B=0)\) is exactly demag-invariant for the same reason (the transverse moment vanishes at \(B=0\)). invz_jq_modes instead exports the shape dependence separately, in exactly the two places it can physically act:

  • info.Jshape_cc — the strict-\(\mathbf q=0\) observable correction (the shape-dependent share that an earlier version of this code folded directly into \(\Jc(0)\), now split out so the critical coupling stays untouched). Applied only in invz_chi_realaxis, as \(\chi_{\rm meas}=\chi/(1+\texttt{Jshape\_cc}\,\chi)\) — algebraically identical to a shifted-pole form \(\tilde\chi_t/\big(1-(\texttt{Jcc0}-\texttt{Jshape\_cc})\tilde\chi_t\big)\), just written so the demag-invariant pole \(\texttt{Jcc0}\) is never itself shape-dependent. The strict-\(\mathbf q=0\) soft mode saturates at \(1/\texttt{Jshape\_cc}\) instead of diverging; a finite-\(q\) probe (§4, invz_spectra_qpath) never applies this term (the \(q\to0^+\) limit is the intrinsic one).
  • info.Jaa0 — the demag-aware transverse (\(aa\)) coupling, Jaa0_dipole(ion.demag,ion.alpha) \(+\,4\Jc_{12}\) (same lattice-sum machinery as Jcc0_dipole, run on the \(aa\) tensor block; see invz_jq_modes for the exact per-component formula). This is now wired all the way into the transverse mean field — passed as opts.Jxx0 to invz_single_ion, invz_twolevel/invz_twolevel_ordered and invz_chi_realaxis — the one channel through which \(B_c(T)\) vs. applied field can still shift with sample shape (the internal-vs-applied transverse-field relation), even though the ordering criticality itself is shape-blind.

Because invz_jq_modes is the single source for info.Jcc0, info.Jshape_cc and info.Jaa0, and all three are threaded consistently through the mean field, the RPA denominator and the 1/z criticality, setting the shape (or any coupling parameter) propagates everywhere it is physically allowed to act — and nowhere else.

  3. \(B_c(T)\) vs. applied transverse field can shift through the demag-aware info.Jaa0 (internal-vs-applied field relation).

1.7  The 3×3 dipolar tensor vs. the scalar 1/z theory

The dipole–dipole coupling is a full Cartesian tensor \(\Jc_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q)\), \(\alpha,\beta\in\{a,b,c\}\) — in the code a [3,3,4,4] array over sublattice pairs from MF_dipole — so all three spin components participate in the interaction, whereas Jensen's HoF3 theory is written for a scalar coupling in a single channel (\(J_xJ_x\) in his notation). This is not a discrepancy between the two materials that had to be worked around: HoF3 is also dipole-coupled (it is in Jensen's title), and in both cases the scalar \(\Jc(\mathbf q)\) of the 1/z machinery is the projection of the same 3×3 tensor onto the one component that has matrix elements inside the low-energy two-level subspace. The channel is selected by the single-ion level scheme, not by the interaction. In LiHoF4 the Γ3,4 ground doublet carries only the \(c\)-axis component of \(\mathbf J\), so the fluctuation operator in the two-site Green function is \(\tilde J_z\) alone — R 2007 eq. 5 expands \(G(ij,\tau)=-\langle T_\tau\,\tilde J_{iz}(\tau_1)\,\tilde J_{jz}(\tau_2)\rangle\) — and the only tensor block that can attach an interaction line to those fluctuations at both ends is \(\Jc_{cc}(\mathbf q)\). That is the dip(3,3,:,:) extraction in invz_jq_modes, whose 4×4 sublattice block is then diagonalized into the four scalar branches \(\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\), matching R 2007 eqs. 7–9, where \(\Jc(\mathbf q)\) is defined as the eigenvalues of the 4×4 (cc) coupling matrix.

The remaining tensor components are not discarded — each enters through the channel where it can act, and the neglected remainders are bounded by R 2007's own estimates:

  • \(\Jc_{aa}\) → static transverse mean field. The \(aa\) block generates \(h_x=\Jc_{aa}(0)\langle J_x\rangle\), solved self-consistently in invz_single_ion (default ion.Jxx0 \(=\Jc^{aa}_{D\cdot D}(0)+4\Jc_{12}=3.512\) μeV at demag=0; demag-aware calculations pass the shape-corrected info.Jaa0 instead, as opts.Jxx0 — §1.6), which renormalizes the doublet parameters \(\Delta\) and \(M^2\) at every \((T,B_x)\). Only its 1/z fluctuation corrections are dropped: the internal transverse field is ∼5% of the applied field at the largest fields, so renormalizing the transverse moment "should be of no importance" (R 2007 Sec. II B).
  • Off-diagonal blocks & higher crystal-field levels → bare susceptibility only. \(\Jc_{ac},\Jc_{bc},\dots\) multiply operators with no matrix elements inside the doublet (statically they act like internally generated transverse fields, R 2007 Sec. I); dynamically they reach the cc channel only via doublet↔higher-level transitions (first excited CF level at 11 K). Those transitions are included in the module's \(G_0=-\chi_0^{cc}\), which is the full 136-state electronuclear susceptibility (invz_chi0z; the c0(3,3,:) component in invz_solve_point), but they are excluded from \(\Sigma\), which is built from the two-level sector. R 2007 bounds their share of \(\chi_0^{cc}(0)\) at <1% at zero frequency at maximum field — "the singlet–singlet determination of the renormalization effects is acceptable".
  • Renormalize the dominant channel only. Dividing just \(\chi_0^{cc}\) by \(1+\Sigma\) is Jensen's own prescription for realistic level schemes (J 1994 Sec. III; jensen_1z_framework.html §10): the 1/z renormalization applies to the susceptibility components deriving from the dominant two-level transition; the weak transitions stay at their bare/RPA values.

What genuinely stays outside the scalar theory is full-tensor mode mixing — an RPA in which \(\Jc_{ac}(\mathbf q)\) hybridizes the cc soft mode with the gapped transverse excitons at general \(\mathbf q\). That is the "tensor observables" item of §8 (hook: MF_RPA_Yikai.m); for the ordering instability, the soft mode, and \(\chi_{cc}\) it enters at the same <1% weak-transition level quoted above.

Conventions \(\chi=-G\); ferromagnetic \(\Jc>0\); energies in meV; bosonic Matsubara \(\omega_n=2\pi n/\beta\), \(n\ge0\), with weights \([1,2,2,\dots]\). \(\Sigma(0)\) is not monotonic on cooling: it rises from its high-\(T\) decay, peaks near \(k_BT\sim\Delta\), and saturates to a finite zero-point value.

1.8  In-plane field rotation & the vector transverse mean field

§1.7 established \(h_x=\Jc_{aa}(0)\langle J_x\rangle\) as the transverse channel for a field fixed along the crystal \(a\) axis. Rotating the field within the transverse plane (azimuth \(\phi_{ab}\), \(a\to b\)) exposes a second transverse mean field, \(h_y=\Jc_{aa}(0)\langle J_y\rangle\), that the single-axis code path never solved. invz_single_ion's opts.transverse_mf selects how it is handled: 'legacy_x' (default, unchanged) pins \(h_y\equiv0\) regardless of the field direction; 'none' forces both channels to zero (a bare CF+Zeeman control); 'vector_ab' solves \(h_x\) and \(h_y\) self-consistently. 'vector_ab' is not an optional refinement for a rotated field — it is required: the non-axial crystal-field term \(B_{64}^s\) breaks the mirror symmetry that would otherwise force \(\langle J_y\rangle=0\), so \(\langle J_y\rangle\neq0\) even for a field applied purely along \(a\) (\(\phi_{ab}=0\); see the \(\chi_0^{yz}\) group-theory result in docs/SESSION-2026-07-16-field-angle.md, "Round 1"). Forcing \(h_y=0\) for an azimuthally rotated field therefore does not merely approximate a small effect, it breaks the crystal's four-fold (\(C_4\)) symmetry outright: the doublet splitting develops up to a 17 μeV a/b asymmetry at 4 T under legacy_x that is absent under none/vector_ab (the guard in invz_spectra_map/invz_spectra_qpath cites this figure directly). invz_run_ip_scan.m's angular scans make the violation visible quantitatively via invz_c4fit's C4/C8 harmonic decomposition: none and vector_ab both show a stable principal-axis angle (within ∼1.5° of each other at every field) while legacy_x's principal axis swings by tens of degrees, including a sign flip, between field magnitudes (docs/SESSION-2026-07-16-inplane-rotation.md).

Never cross-compare MF models vector_ab shifts even the phi_ab = 0 result relative to legacy_x at the same nominal field (∼0.04 μeV at 4 T, growing at lower field) — a real physical difference between the two mean-field models (whether \(\langle J_y\rangle\) is allowed to respond to \(B_{64}^s\)), not a rotation effect. Do not compare a legacy_x run against a vector_ab run as if only the angle had changed.

The driver-facing knob is phi_ab (§4); it is validated independently of the §1.6/§4 theta_c out-of-plane tilt knob, at theta_c=0, over the full tested angle range — see §8 for the supported range and the combined-tilt exclusion.

1.9  Off-diagonal dipolar (ODD) mediated coupling — Tier 1 & Tier 2

§1.7 dismissed the off-diagonal blocks \(\Jc^{ca},\Jc^{cb}\) as acting only "like internally generated transverse fields" — true at the mean-field level, but at second order they mediate an extra pairwise cc coupling: ion \(i\)'s \(c\)-fluctuation polarizes an intermediate ion's transverse moment (through \(\Jc^{c\alpha}\)), which couples back to ion \(j\)'s \(c\)-fluctuation (through \(\Jc^{\alpha c}\)). This is Dollberg–Schechter's three-spin ODD term (DS2023, arXiv:2308.10095) resummed into a static, \(\chi_\perp\)-mediated coupling \(\delta\Jc^{cc}(\mathbf q)\). It is a strictly additive, opt-in Tier-1 extension: with the flag off it is bit-for-bit the published scalar theory.

The off-diagonal dipolar blocks are the same \([3,3,4,4]\) tensor as §1.7, projected onto the \(c\alpha\) rows (invz_odd_blocks):

$$\Jc^{c\alpha}(\mathbf q)_{ss'}=-g_{\rm fac}\,\mathrm{dip}(3,\alpha,s,s'),\qquad \alpha\in\{a,b\}.\tag{E1a}$$

Contracting a pair of them through the transverse single-ion susceptibility \(\chi_\perp\) — the symmetrized \((a,b)\) block of the full 136-state electronuclear \(\chi_0\) (invz_chiperp; Van Vleck-dominated, \(\chi_{aa}=\chi_{bb}\approx17.6\) meV−1 at 1.53 K, \(B_x=0\)) — gives the mediated coupling per \(\mathbf q\):

$$\delta\Jc^{cc}_{\rm pre}(\mathbf q)=\sum_{\alpha\beta\in\{a,b\}}\Jc^{c\alpha}(\mathbf q)\,[\chi_\perp]_{\alpha\beta}\,\Jc^{\beta c}(\mathbf q)\;\succeq\;0.\tag{E1}$$

Because \(\chi_\perp\succ0\), \(\delta\Jc_{\rm pre}\) is positive-semidefinite (mediated interactions lower energy) and vanishes at \(\mathbf q\to0\) on-axis (\(C_2\)-about-\(c\)). Enforcing the no-self-interaction constraint \(\delta\Jc(ii)=0\) subtracts the BZ-average of each sublattice diagonal (invz_odd_deltaJ):

$$\delta\Jc_{ss}(\mathbf q)\leftarrow\delta\Jc_{ss}(\mathbf q)-\tfrac1{N_q}\sum_{\mathbf q'}\delta\Jc_{ss}(\mathbf q'),\qquad d\equiv\tfrac1{N_q}\sum_{\mathbf q'}\big[\delta\Jc_{\rm pre}\big]_{ss}(\mathbf q')\;(>0,\ \text{equal on all four sublattices}).\tag{E4, E5}$$

The finite-\(q\) grid matrices \(\Jc_{cc}(\mathbf q)+\delta\Jc(\mathbf q)\) carry the post-subtraction coupling (already \(-d\) on the diagonal), and the uniform coupling takes the explicit reduction \(\Jc(0)\to\Jc(0)-d\) — the single line that carries the mean-field DS2023 mechanism into the mean field, the RPA denominator and the 1/z criticality at once (§1.6 single-sourcing). Measured \(d(T_c)=0.48\) μeV, a 7.5% reduction of \(\Jc^{cc}_{D\cdot D}(0)=6.42\) μeV — in the DS2023-consistent 0.3–0.5 μeV band.

The zero-field criticality condition (E2) keeps its form but is now \(T\)-dependent with ODD on, because \(\chi_\perp(T)\) enters \(\delta\Jc\) and hence both \(\Jc(0;T)\) and \(\Sigma_c(0;T)\): \(T_c(0)\) is the root of

$$\Jc(0;T)\,\chi_0^{cc}(0;T)=1+\Sigma_c(0;T),\qquad \Sigma_c(0;T)=\Sigma_c\big(\Jc(0)-d(T),\,\Jc_\nu[\Jc_{cc}+\delta\Jc(T)]\big)\ \text{(R2007 eq.\,10).}\tag{E2}$$

(invz_critical_T0field now accepts \(\Sigma_c\) and \(\Jc(0)\) as function-of-\(T\) handles; \(\chi_\perp(T)\) is nearly flat so the bisection converges unchanged.) The full measurement engine is invz_odd_zero_field.

χ⊥ held fixed (design decision) \(\chi_\perp\) is Van Vleck-dominated and insensitive to the cc order parameter and to \(K\); it is computed once per \((T,B_x)\) before the EMT⇔Σ self-consistency loop, never iterated inside it (invz_chiperp does not route through invz_twolevel — it must stay regular across the ~10 K CF gap, where the two-level object is not). The internal-field parameter renormalization that the on-site \(-d\) would otherwise double-count is Tier 2 (the field covariance (E3), invz_odd_fieldvar — implemented behind opts.odd_tier2, below); full-tensor ODD mode-mixing stays deferred to Tier 3 (§8).

Tier 2 — variable moments (Dollberg), opt-in on top of Tier 1. The same ODD blocks make every ion see a fluctuating internal transverse field \(h_i^\alpha=\sum_j \Jc^{\alpha c}_{ij}\,\delta J^c_j\). Tier 1 keeps the doublet parameters \((\Delta,M^2,n_{01})\) at their applied-field values; Tier 2 lets the internal fields re-dress them (DS2023's "variable moments"). At a converged Tier-1 point the field covariance is the E3 contraction of the equal-time cc structure factor over the BZ (invz_odd_fieldvar, meV2; \(h_{\rm eq}=\sqrt{\max\mathrm{eig}\,C}/(g_L\mu_B)\approx0.3\) T, same order as Dollberg's \(\sim\)0.4 T distribution width), and the doublet is re-dressed by a quenched Gaussian average over \((h_a,h_b)\sim N(0,C)\) on a Gauss–Hermite grid (invz_twolevel_avg): node fields enter as pure field shifts, and the node-averaged response \(\bar g(i\omega_n)\) plus the node-averaged FULL electronuclear \(\bar G_0\) are re-compressed into effective two-level parameters (\(\Delta_{\rm eff}\ge\Delta\), level repulsion) by matching \(\bar g(0)\) and the \(\omega_n^{-2}\) tail exactly. invz_solve_point with opts.odd_tier2=true (requires opts.odd) iterates this to self-consistency: the converged Tier-1 solve seeds \(C\) → dressed \((\Delta_{\rm eff},\dots)\) and \(\bar G_0\) re-enter the inner EMT⇔Σ loop (warm-started) → \(C\) is re-evaluated and mixed (damping 0.5) until \(\max|\delta C|<10^{-3}\) relative (cap opts.max_tier2 = 8; non-convergence masked exactly like the EMT loop; new fields pt.C/tier2_iters/tla/tier2_resid). The criticality condition then carries the dressed local propagator, \(\mathrm{crit}=1+\Sigma_0-\Jc(0)\,\bar\chi_0^{cc}(0)\) — that is how variable moments suppress ordering. The lattice geometric side (the \(\delta\Jc\) rebuild and the \(-d\) shift) stays at the Tier-1 static \(\chi_\perp\) (the χ⊥-held-fixed callout above; Van Vleck, insensitive); combining with opts.odd_retarded errors (invz:oddArgs) — the E3 assembly uses the static mode spectrum and has no validated retarded counterpart (the static T2.2 default makes that combination unused).

Least rigorous step (flagged) The Tier-2 dressing is a quenched-Gaussian, static treatment: thermal/quantum internal-field fluctuations are frozen into a classical distribution and the averaged response is re-compressed into a single pole (invz_twolevel_avg, whose header carries the same flag). Averaging the response and averaging the parameters are inequivalent — the default averages \(g(i\omega_n)\) node-by-node (the disorder-averaged propagator; parameter averaging stays behind opts.tier2.avg='params'), with the measured inequivalence \(\Delta_{\rm eff}=0.153888\) (response) vs \(0.152857\) (params) meV at the T3.2 fixture. Treat Tier-2 numbers accordingly: docs/ODD-LOG.md §T3.2–T3.4.
Measured (T3.3/T3.4) PM gate (1.80 K, 0.05 T): 2 outer iterations, crit 0.09127 → 0.09468 (suppression), \(\Delta\) 1.30×10−4 → 1.18×10−2 meV (the internal field, not the 0.05 T applied field, sets the dressed splitting), \(C_{aa}=4.57\times10^{-4}\) meV2. 1.9.1  Relation to the reference literature — implementation in spirit, not in algorithm

The point of including ODD is that the reference literature (Dollberg, Andresen & Schechter, PRB 105, L180413 (2022); Dollberg & Schechter, arXiv:2308.10095 — “DS2023”) holds that its inclusion fixes the phase boundary at high temperature. This module deliberately does not copy the literature algorithm — only the physics principle: if the authors are right, the correct phase boundary should naturally emerge from the otherwise-unchanged 1/z machinery, which is the best available test of our understanding of the underlying physics — particularly because the 1/z expansion method is fundamentally different from the reference framework. The physics-level treatment, including two analytic results this framing produced (the uniform-shift theorem and the naive-MF inconsistency), is jensen_1z_framework.html §11.

How the principle enters (renormalized inputs, unchanged machinery). The transverse \((a,b)\) response of Ho3+ is Van Vleck (first excited CF level ≈10.8 K, \(\gg k_BT\) at the transition), and the ODD blocks vanish at \(\mathbf q=0\) by the \(C_2\)-about-\(c\) site symmetry — no mean-field effect; ODD acts only through fluctuations. Integrating out that fast transverse channel at Gaussian level yields exactly two renormalized inputs: the mediated pair coupling \(\delta\Jc^{cc}(\mathbf q)\) with its self-site subtraction (E1/E4/E5, Tier 1 — the corrected mode spectrum feeds \(\Sigma_c\), the shifted \(\Jc(0)-d\) feeds \(\Jc(0)\chi_0=1+\Sigma\)) and the internal-field covariance (E3, Tier 2 — quenched-Gaussian re-dressing of the doublet parameters). No formula in the EMT/Σ layer changes. Both mediators are the module's own objects: \(\chi_\perp\) is the full 136-state electronuclear transverse susceptibility (17.64 meV−1 at 1.53 K — not the 3-state \(2\rho^2/\Delta\approx11\) of the reference model), and the Tier-2 covariance is contracted from the module's own converged 1/z equal-time structure factor. Retardation was tested, not assumed (the T2 callout above: 0.022 mK \(\ll\) the 10 mK threshold; criticality lives in the elastic \(n=0\) sector where the static \(\chi_\perp\) is exact).

Deliberately not imported (the algorithm level of DS2023):

  • no Schrieffer–Wolff effective spin-½ projection, no 3-state truncation — the single ion and \(\chi_\perp\) stay the full 136-state electronuclear problem, hyperfine exact, non-perturbative;
  • no Monte Carlo;
  • no 0.805 longitudinal rescaling — in the 1/z framework the \(c\)-axis fluctuation renormalization is \(\Sigma\)'s job; importing the factor would double-count fluctuations;
  • no perturbative temperature-dependent hyperfine renormalization.

DS2023 numbers are used in exactly two ways: (a) their convention-free geometric lattice sums (Suppl. Table I) as unit guards, verified independently by Parseval/real-space identities (ODD-LOG §T1.1); (b) their MF/MC results as qualitative comparators — reported against, never tuned to.

The emergent-boundary test With zero imported fitting factors: \(\Delta T_c(0)=0.2341\pm0.001\) K against the ≈0.21 K high-\(T\) discrepancy — \(T_c(0)\): 1.743 → 1.509 K vs experiment 1.53 K, a 21 mK overshoot, stated plainly; \(\Delta\Sigma_c(0)=+0.091\); and the \(B_c(T)\) suppression attenuates toward high field (\(\chi_\perp\) stiffens as the doublet splits), consistent with the DS2023 crossover ≈3.5 T. Numbers: docs/ODD-LOG.md §T1.5/§V4.
Caveats The Gaussian (adiabatic) elimination of the transverse channel is the controlled approximation; its rigorous completion is the deferred full-tensor route (odd_implementation_plan.html Appendix A; plan docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-16-invz-tensor-odd.md), whose Gaussian truncation must reproduce Tiers 1+2 — the designed future cross-validation. The Tier-2 quenched-Gaussian dressing remains the least rigorous step (flagged above; keep it flagged). And the right claim is that the mechanism naturally closes the gap within the approximation's expected fidelity — not exact agreement: the ±1 mK bar is grid-only, and the Gaussian-channel systematic is unquantified until the tensor route exists.

2 · Data flow & call structure

2.1  Driver call structure — what you run, and what it calls

The two scripts you run are the drivers; everything below them is called to carry out the task. Both fan into one shared solver core, and the only phase-dependent fork is the paramagnet / ferromagnet solve pair — every other function is shared between the phases.

2.2  Data flow through the layers

Each layer consumes only the outputs of the layers above it. The self-consistent point solve (layer 6) is the loop that ties the effective medium (4) to the self-energy (5).

Physical mode, not neutron intensity By default invz_spectra_qpath follows the uniform ferromagnetic-mode coupling \(J(\mathbf q)=v^{\mathsf T}\Jc_{cc}(\mathbf q)v\), \(v=[1\,1\,1\,1]/2\) (invz_jq_path P.Juni) — the same effective coupling MF_RPA_Yikai.m uses, and the physical single mode under the "all four Ho sublattices equivalent" mean-field/RPA approximation both codes make. Do not pass opts.branch = 4 ("max eigenvalue") expecting the same curve: away from Γ the uniform mode stops being an eigenvector of the 4×4 cc block, so the four sorted branches cross, and the largest-eigenvalue branch selects a different physical sublattice combination on each side of the crossing — for this path it mirrors the dispersion about \(h=1.5\). opts.branch = 1..4 remains available as an exploratory sorted-eigenvalue view (branch index, not a mode identity tracked through crossings) but is not the R 2007 Fig. 3 comparison. It is still not a neutron cross-section either way — there is no eigenvector / structure-factor / polarization / magnetic-form-factor weighting — and it inherits three open issues from the rest of the module (§8): closed-grid BZ quadrature bias, possible negative spectral weight from the real-axis continuation, and the bare mean-field FM/PM handoff (invz_solve_auto). 4 · Driver scripts

invz_run_spectra.m — susceptibility spectra

Computes \(\chi''_{cc}(\omega)\) at the uniform mode versus transverse field at fixed \(T\), and chooses its view automatically from the number of fields:

  • Few fields (numel(fields) ≤ sliceMax, default 8) → 1-D line-slice overlay, 1/z solid vs bare-RPA dashed, one colour per field (cf. R 2007 Fig. 2).
  • Many fields → 2-D field-vs-frequency colormap: \(B_x\) on x, \(\omega\) on y, \(\log_{10}\chi''\) in colour, with 1/z and RPA panels side by side (cf. Kovačević 2016 Fig. 3d).

Edit the block at the top of the file: T, fields (e.g. [3.6 4.2 4.8 5.4 6.0] for slices, linspace(0,6,61) for a map), w, eta, sliceMax, and useParallel; set ion.demag/ion.alpha just after invz_ion() for a sample-shape correction (§1.6). eta is the real-axis Lorentzian half-width — a display broadening, not physical damping (the model's intrinsic linewidth is negligible at these temperatures), so keep it \(\gtrsim\) the w spacing or the peaks alias. The heavy result S replots for free via invz_plot_spectra_map(gca, S, S.chiz, '1/z').

Both phases, two theories At each field invz_spectra_map first tries the ferromagnetic (ordered) solve; if the point has a spontaneous moment it uses that, otherwise the paramagnet (S.phase: 1 = FM, 2 = PM, 0 = no solution — the AUTO dispatch). RPA and 1/z are different approximations with different critical fields (invzp_QCP_diagnosis.md). The \(\Sigma=0\) overlay S.chirpa is built from the auto state, which approximates the RPA state only where the ordered 1/z EMT converged to the bare boundary — its boundary is therefore reported as S.Bc_auto, a dispatch diagnostic rather than a certified \(B_c^{\rm RPA}\) (the 1/z-independent RPA dispatcher, diagnosis regression 4, is a scheduled follow-up). S.chiz follows the 1/z theory's own stability (S.phase_1z: the strict-PM 1/z state wherever its mass S.crit_pm \(=1+\Sigma(0)-\Jc(0)\chi_0^{cc}(0)>0\); the bare-MF ordered curve below \(B_c^{1/z}\) as a flagged diagnostic — its ordered-side pole closure at \(B_c^{1/z}\) is a Stage-2 requirement, not a Stage-1 claim). The two boundaries are reported separately and are not forced to agree. Columns with no valid 1/z label are masked (NaN) in S.chiz; columns with no accepted auto state (phase 0), a spuriously converged below-\(B_c\) PM state (S.suspect, crit ≤ 0), or a non-finite PM mass mask both overlays and never reach the peak curves. Genuinely intractable columns, including the degenerate doublet at \(B_x\!\to\!0\) where the two-level treatment breaks down in both phases, are likewise returned as NaN and masked.

theta_c (deg) tilts the swept field out of the transverse ab-plane toward c, modelling experimental field misalignment: the direction is fixed across the sweep and the x-axis stays the field magnitude \(|B|\) (the longitudinal component is \(|B|\sin\theta_c\)). Longitudinal fields route the solve through the moment-form self-energy instead of the strict paramagnet (invz_solve_auto's longitudinal branch, opts.forced_moment, sign-aware branch seeding) — the sharp quantum phase transition becomes a rounded crossover, and S.phase = 1 means "moment-form" for either reason (spontaneous FM below \(B_c\) or field-induced under a tilt). Scalar-stage validity: \(\Sigma\) dresses the cc channel only, exact at \(\theta_c=0\); the Σ=0 scalar-vs-tensor cross-channel reference (invz_chi_tensor_ref / invz_run_tensor_ref) measures the supported range as theta_c ≤ 5° under the driver's own peak observables — mode position agreeing to a few μeV and peak amplitude to ≤10% at every tested field; the raw and tilt-differenced spectral-L2 lineshape metrics are reported diagnostics only, and lineshape fidelity is explicitly not claimed (see docs/SESSION-2026-07-16-field-angle.md). Full-tensor propagation is deferred (design spec §8); azimuthal (in-plane) rotation is covered separately by phi_ab, next.

phi_ab (deg) rotates the swept field within the transverse ab-plane, \(a\to b\), independently of theta_c above: dhat = [cosd(theta_c)*cosd(phi_ab), cosd(theta_c)*sind(phi_ab), sind(theta_c)]. A nonzero phi_ab requires transverse_mf = 'vector_ab' (next paragraph; 'none' also passes, as a bare CF+Zeeman diagnostic) — the driver raises invz:transverseMF rather than silently returning a \(C_4\)-broken result under legacy_x (§1.8). phi_ab = -11° reproduces the experimentally calibrated external-stack geometry (ion.cfRot(Ho) = -11°): the sign is pinned against the external cf.m coefficient-rotation convention by test_invz_cfrot_equiv — coefficient rotation by \(r\) matches field rotation by the same \(+r\), not the naively expected opposite sign. invz_run_ip_scan.m measures the supported range: at theta_c = 0, every tested angle (phi_ab ∈ {0,5,11,15,30,45,60,75,79,90}°, 79° being the C4-equivalent mirror of the production angle -11°) and field ({2, 4.95, 6} T) passes the same peak-observable gate as the theta_c reference above (worst case eps_amp = 2.66%, dE_peak = 7.9 μeV; full table in docs/SESSION-2026-07-16-inplane-rotation.md). Combined theta_c ≠ 0 AND phi_ab ≠ 0 is unvalidated — the two knobs were measured on separate one-dimensional slices, never simultaneously; see §8.

transverse_mf ('legacy_x' default | 'none' | 'vector_ab') selects the transverse mean-field model passed to every single-ion solve in the driver (threaded via opts.solve_opts.transverse_mf); see §1.8 for what each mode does and why legacy_x is \(C_4\)-inconsistent for a rotated field.

Setting qpath (an [nq×3] r.l.u. array) switches the same driver to the ferromagnetic-mode q-path view (invz_spectra_qpath / invz_plot_spectra_qpath) at fixed field(s) Bq: a single field renders 1/z + RPA path-vs-frequency colormaps with a censored peak overlay; several fields (R 2007 Fig. 3 uses Bq = [3.6 4.24 6.0] T) render an \(E_{\rm peak}(q)\) dispersion overlay instead, one colour per field. The dispersion defaults to the uniform FM-mode coupling (opts.branch = 0), the physical mode that reproduces Fig. 3's monotonic \((1,0,0)\to(2,0,0)\) softening; wq sets the frequency grid — widen past its default 0.85 meV if peaks clip at the top edge, since the peak picker censors (never interpolates over) a boundary maximum; dispScale rescales the plotted dispersion only (R 2007 scale their calculated energies by 1.15 to match experiment, Fig. 3). See Quick start for a worked snippet and the physical-vs-exploratory-branch caveat.

invz_run_phase_diagram.m — phase boundary \(B_c(T)\)

Traces the R 2007 Fig. 1 paramagnetic-side boundary in two regimes: the critical field \(B_c(T)\) bisected at each temperature in Ts (invz_critical, vertical cuts), and the critical temperature \(T_c(B)\) bisected at each field in Bs (invz_critical_T, horizontal cuts). The horizontal cuts own the high-\(T\) end, where the boundary runs nearly parallel to the field axis and a fixed-\(T\) cut crosses it at a glancing angle (brackets fail, tiny \(T\) errors give huge \(B_c\) errors). The \(T_c(B)\) bisection window is fixed in the script at [Tlo, Tmax] = [1.0, 2.0] K. Keep Bs entries between 0.5 T and \(B_c(T_{lo}) \approx 2.8\) T: below 0.5 T the paramagnetic solve develops non-convergence patches near the boundary that bias \(T_c\) upward (the 0–0.5 T segment spans only ~4 mK and is represented by the closed-form zero-field \(T_c\) endpoint); above \(B_c(T_{lo})\) the bracket assert fails cleanly to NaN. Full single-core run ≈ 2–3 h; see the next section for the parallel speedup.

5 · Parallelism

Two independent axes can be distributed over a parallel pool; the bisection within a single invz_critical cannot (each bracket halving needs the sign of the previous EMT solve).

invz_run_phase_diagramboundary points (one flat parfor over the Ts and Bs jobs)useParallel = true (default)
invz_spectra_map / invz_run_spectrafield (parfor over fields)opts.parallel = true / useParallel = true

Both use parfor (k = 1:n, nWorkers) with nWorkers = 0 forcing a serial run — so the same code runs with or without the Parallel Computing Toolbox, and falls back to serial automatically when it is absent. A local pool is auto-created on first use (~10–30 s once). The \(\Jc(q)\) lattice sum is computed once up front, so workers do no disk I/O and never contend on invz_projected/cache/. Progress lines interleave across workers — expected. With an 8-worker pool the phase boundary drops from ~2–3 h to roughly 20–30 min.

Boundary speckle Right at the marginal phase boundary a field/temperature point can fail to converge and come back masked, next to converged neighbours. That is the para fixed point going marginal (not a bug). Widen max_outer / soften mixing there, or accept a couple of masked edge points.

8 · Scope & limitations

In scope: both phases — single-ion response, the scalar (cc-channel) 1/z self-energy and effective-medium lattice sum, the paramagnetic-side boundary, the real-axis cc-susceptibility \(\chi(\mathbf q,\nu,\omega)\), and the ferromagnetic phase via the spontaneous-moment mean field + elastic-sector self-energy (\(\alpha_m\), \(\lambda_3\); framework SS9.2, J 2.26–2.27), giving the soft mode on both sides of the transition; the uniform-mode coupling always carries the Lorentz cavity term (demag-invariant), plus an optional sample-shape correction split into the two channels where it can physically act — the strict-uniform observable term info.Jshape_cc and the demag-aware transverse coupling info.Jaa0 (§1.6) — consistently shared by the mean field, RPA and 1/z; and a q-path susceptibility (invz_spectra_qpath) whose default dispersion is the physical uniform ferromagnetic-mode coupling \(v^{\mathsf T}\Jc_{cc}(q)v\), reproducing R 2007 Fig. 3's monotonic softening end to end (not a neutron-intensity calculation; see the Quick-start caveat) — an exploratory sorted-eigenvalue branch (opts.branch=1..4) remains available but is not the Fig. 3 comparison; and, at the scalar stage, two independent field-rotation knobs in invz_run_spectra: an out-of-plane c-axis tilt theta_c that routes a longitudinal field component through the moment-form self-energy (invz_solve_auto's forced_moment branch), turning the sharp transition into a rounded crossover — tensor-referenced valid for theta_c ≤ 5° on peak observables (design spec 2026-07-16-invz-field-angle-design.md; measurement in docs/SESSION-2026-07-16-field-angle.md) — and an in-plane ab-azimuth rotation phi_ab (§1.8), requiring transverse_mf = 'vector_ab' for a \(C_4\)-consistent transverse mean field, tensor-referenced valid at theta_c = 0 across every tested angle (0–90°, in 5° steps plus 11° and 79°) and field (2–6 T) under the same peak-observable gate (invz_run_ip_scan; measurement in docs/SESSION-2026-07-16-inplane-rotation.md); phi_ab = -11° is pinned (test_invz_cfrot_equiv) to reproduce the experimentally calibrated external-stack geometry ion.cfRot(Ho) = -11° (its C4-equivalent mirror point, phi_ab = 79°, is included in the tested set). The two knobs are validated only separately — see the exclusion below. Finally, as an opt-in Tier-1 extension (opts.odd/ion.odd, §1.9): the off-diagonal dipolar (ODD) mediated coupling \(\delta\Jc^{cc}\) — a static, Gaussian (\(\chi_\perp\)-mediated) reduction-plus-\(q\)-structuring of the cc coupling that lowers \(T_c(0)\) by 0.234 K — with the closed-form \(\Sigma_c(0)\) now \(T\)-dependent (through \(\chi_\perp(T)\)) when the flag is on. On top of it, the Tier-2 internal-field single-ion parameter renormalization (field covariance (E3) → Gauss–Hermite-dressed doublet, opts.odd_tier2, §1.9) is in scope as a second opt-in stage — measured to add ~3% of the combined ~0.22 K suppression at the 0.5 T proxy, via a quenched-Gaussian dressing that is the plan's flagged least rigorous step; any full-tensor ODD mode-mixing (Tier 3) is out of scope (below).

Out of scope / future work:

  • Ordered-phase field & thermodynamics (deferred part of the ferromagnetic extension) — the applied-field magnetization curve / \(H_\mathrm{MF}\!\leftrightarrow\!H\) self-consistency (framework SS9.3, J 2.31–2.33), which would make the moment onset exactly at the 1/z boundary (removing the small FM/PM step near \(B_c\)), and the free-energy consistency test (framework SS9.4, J 2.34). The elastic \(\xi\,h\) static correction (framework SS9.2, J 2.28) is included only in its low-\(T\) (ordinary-Dyson) limit.
  • Tensor observables — the full 3×3 / 4-sublattice RPA (neutron cross-sections, transverse components) lives in MF_RPA_Yikai.m; invz_jq_modes's per-sublattice \(\Jc_{cc}(q)\) matrix is the hook point. See §1.7 for why the scalar cc reduction is quantitatively safe for the ordering channel (the neglected tensor mode-mixing enters below the 1% level).
  • ODD Tier 3 (§1.9) — the implemented ODD coupling is Tier 1 (static, Gaussian, \(\chi_\perp\)-mediated) plus the Tier-2 internal-field single-ion parameter re-dressing (field covariance (E3) → \(\Delta,M^2,n_{01}\); opts.odd_tier2). A full-tensor ODD mode-mixing RPA (ODD lines hybridizing the cc soft mode with the transverse excitons) is Tier 3 — deferred. Because \(\chi_\perp\) is held fixed (Gaussian) and the Tier-2 dressing is a quenched-Gaussian single-pole recompression, the zero-field \(T_c\) shift remains a directional/qualitative result vs DS2023, not a rescaled quantitative match.
  • Combined c-tilt + in-plane rotation, and full-tensor propagation — theta_c (out-of-plane, ac-plane tilt) and phi_ab (in-plane, ab-plane rotation, §1.8) are each tensor-referenced valid on their own one-dimensional slice (theta_c ≤ 5° at phi_ab = 0; every tested phi_ab at theta_c = 0) but never together — running the driver with both knobs nonzero is unvalidated and out of scope until a dedicated two-angle measurement is done (design spec §8, amendment 3). Arbitrary-tilt fidelity beyond either validated range would need full-tensor 1/z propagation (rigorous tensor-RPA "A0" against the full 3×3 \(\chi_0\), or the projected "A1" bridge) superseding the scalar cc routing used here; also deferred.
  • Thermodynamics — free energy, \(\delta U\), heat capacity (HTML eqs 34–35 / J 2.21–2.22; eq 44 = framework SS9.4, J 2.34). Note \(\delta U(T\!\to\!0)\neq0\) (zero-point shift).
  • Ewald acceleration of the dipole sums — brute-force + on-disk caching is enough at the 12–24³ grids here.
Small-Bx caveat Near zero field the doublet splitting \(\Delta\) can drop below the 165Ho hyperfine scale (\(A\approx3.36\) μeV). There the two-level determination of \(\Sigma\) over-suppresses \(T_c\) (mirroring R 2007's own comparison with quantum Monte Carlo). Results near the degenerate-doublet limit are directional only — which is why invz_twolevel errors (invz:degenerateDoublet) rather than returning a value for \(\Delta<10^{-4}\) meV.
