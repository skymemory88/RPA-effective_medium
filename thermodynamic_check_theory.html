2. The ordered-field equation

Write the longitudinal molecular field in energy units as \(h=H_{\mathrm{MF}}\). Jensen's field transformation used by the code is

\[ H_0(h)=\int_0^h r(s)\,ds, \qquad r(h)=\frac{G_0(0;h)}{\widetilde G_0(0;h)}. \]

With zero applied longitudinal field, the spontaneous ordered solution satisfies

\[ \boxed{F(h)=H_0(h)-J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}m(h)=0}. \]

Each evaluation of \(r(h)\) itself requires a coupled Matsubara self-energy solve and the bounded static closure. The field equation is consequently the outermost of three nonlinear problems rather than a standalone scalar quadrature.

On a differentiable accepted component, the exact derivative is

\[ F'(h)=r(h) \left[1+J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G_0(h)\right] =r(h)+J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}G_0^{\mathrm{bare}}(h). \]

The former substitution of \(G_{\mathrm{stat}}\) for \(\widetilde G_0\) was wrong and reversed the sign near the high-field soft boundary. Direct finite differences, narrow secants, and both exact forms now agree.

3. What a strict thermodynamic construction requires

A strict equation-(45) result requires all of the following:

  1. a physically admissible coupled node at every integration coordinate from zero to the selected root;
  2. one continuous branch or a derived rule for crossing components;
  3. a justified lower integration constant;
  4. finite positive static and dynamic masses on the required path;
  5. a unique accepted ordered-field root or an independent thermodynamic selector among multiple roots; and
  6. one stationary functional whose derivatives reproduce the response, vertex, moment, and field equation used in the calculation.

The strict solver intentionally masks a field when the exact endpoint or any required profile node fails. Finite values above a gap do not, by themselves, define the missing constant or authorize a component jump.

Important distinction The strict failure is a limitation of the exact path proof. It is no longer an unresolved question about why the production plot contained holes; those numerical root causes are now known and repaired through a separately labeled approximation.

4. Verified numerical root causes

4.1 Missing accepted low-field profile

In the problematic ordered regime, low-\(h\) nodes fail the accepted coupled/static contract while a contiguous high-\(h\) suffix converges. Strict quadrature therefore has neither a complete path nor a justified lower constant. Increasing the node count can locate the component edge more accurately, but cannot create the absent component.

4.2 Rejected-state contamination

Failed nodes formerly returned partially updated state and contaminated subsequent warm starts. The solver now commits \(\Sigma\), \(K_0\), and related continuation data only after every node gate passes. This makes shared-node results deterministic across nested grids.

4.3 Multiple components and finite continuation reach

The coupled equations possess folds and more than one numerical sheet. Cold ascending and ordered descending sweeps can reach different contiguous components. A failed warm transfer does not prove that the root is absent; conversely, one successful warm start is not an equilibrium selector.

4.4 Exact-zero degeneracy

At \(B_x=0\), the electronic doublet basis is arbitrary. The old code formed a zero transition weight and its reciprocal separately, producing a numerical 0*Inf although the combined invariant limit is finite. This is an endpoint representation singularity, not a vanishing physical susceptibility.

4.5 Near-critical long-jump failure

The former 4.68 T mask came from a cold short component whose missing-area support stopped below factor one. A descending solve from accepted ordered fields reaches the longer component. The initial 4.70/4.71 failures came from asking a distant seed to jump too far: two independent histories advanced in smaller field steps agree at 129 and 257 nodes through 4.7188 T.

6. Exact-zero solution

The two-level constructor now returns twolevel_domain_invalid at an unresolved degenerate node instead of throwing or mutating accepted continuation state. Strict mode still fails because it requires that endpoint. The missing-area route selects only the certified positive-\(h\) suffix.

When the transition weight is exactly zero, Matsubara and real-axis code evaluate the cancelled expression

\[ Q_0=\frac{2m^2}{n_{01}^2} \left[\lambda_1-(1-n_{01}^2)K_0\right] \]

rather than separately evaluating a divergent intermediate. Positive transition weights retain their historical arithmetic. Basis-invariant projector diagnostics, an exact-zero/\(10^{-6}\) T ladder, all three diagnostic area members, and a 129/257-node comparison verify the endpoint.

7. Interior and boundary continuation

7.1 Immutable cold pass

All retry eligibility and source states are frozen before any recovery. A recovered point never seeds another recovery. This makes the result independent of retry traversal order and prevents a chain of speculative states from crossing the phase boundary.

7.2 Two-sided interior retry

A cold mask between accepted ordered fields is solved independently from the nearest source on each side. Both results must pass the original physical and residual gates and agree in molecular field, self-energy, and component edge. This recovers the audited 0.36 and 0.45 T holes for every missing-area member.

7.3 Ordered-boundary retry

The last masked ordered-side point has no ordered source above it, so it uses a stricter, distinct contract. It must be a frozen phase==0 point above all accepted ordered cold states and below an accepted PM cold state. Two untouched lower ordered sources must directly recover the same unbridged component. A separately damped PM solve must converge with negative mass, and both ordered results must retain \(D_{\mathrm{uni}},F'\ge10^{-3}\), all original physical masses, and the final residual gate. A cold phase==2 point is never retried.

At 4.68 T the accepted central state has

\[ h_{\mathrm{MF}}=0.0036183\ \mathrm{meV},\qquad D_{\mathrm{uni}}=0.023913,\qquad F'=0.020014, \]

and the independently converged PM mass is \(-0.0178456\). The 129/257 peak displacement is 0.00110 GHz and the integrated spectral-weight change is 0.21%. Only factor one satisfies the complete source and support contract; noncentral members remain incomplete in the separate sensitivity diagnostic and are not enabled in the current full sweep.

9. What remains of the thermodynamic check

9.1 Valid local identities

On a differentiable accepted sheet, the field identities and the corrected \(F'\) relation remain valid numerical checks. Fold-anchored differences can cancel an unknown additive field constant between two sheets that meet at the same fold. These diagnostics helped establish branch topology and reject the idea that Picard failure equals root termination.

9.2 Why they are not a production equilibrium selector

Jensen's free-energy derivation assumes that the moment, bare response, elastic weights, and cumulant vertex arise from one single-ion model. The production hybrid uses the full 136-state electronuclear moment and bare response together with an electronic two-level vertex. No stationary same-ion or explicitly mixed functional has been derived whose derivatives reproduce all of those production equations.

Consequently, a negative fold-anchored potential difference remains a conditional branch-ranking diagnostic, not a rigorous global equilibrium label. Likewise, an uncontrolled saturation extrapolation cannot supply the absent lower integration constant.

Faithful conclusion The thermodynamic-functional limitation is real, but it was not the direct cause of the plotted convergence holes. Those holes came from incomplete low-\(h\) profiles, state-commit semantics, component-dependent continuation, the exact-zero removable singularity, and a near-critical long-jump failure. Production now addresses those mechanisms explicitly without claiming a stronger free-energy theorem.
