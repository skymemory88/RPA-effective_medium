Thermodynamic Check Theory

Definition, derivation, interpretation, and limitations of the fold-anchored potential used in the inverse-\(z\) convergence analysis.

Evidence snapshot through investigation checkpoint 27, with the independent theory review incorporated (31 July 2026). Unless stated otherwise, quoted numerical diagnostics are at \(T=0.1\) K.

Short answer The rigorous route did not fail because the remaining quadrature is merely difficult. It failed because the present production closure does not supply one globally selected, thermodynamically anchored function \(r(h)\) to integrate. The known certified component folds before reaching \(h=0\), \(r\) becomes branch-valued, neither the zero-field nor saturation anchor is controlled, and the full-electronuclear-response / electronic-two-level-vertex hybrid is not the same-ion construction for which Jensen derived a free energy. Integrals over certified pieces remain numerically meaningful diagnostics, but they are not a rigorous equilibrium selector for the production model. A separately labeled missing-area ensemble is now available for practical spectra. It is an opt-in approximation with a strict default, not a repair of the failed thermodynamic proof.

1. Fields, variables, and sign conventions

Let \(H\) denote the applied longitudinal magnetic field and let \(\mu=g\mu_B\). It is convenient to work in the energy-field variable

\[ h_{\mathrm{app}}=\mu H , \]

measured in meV. The magnetic moment variable used below is the dimensionless angular-momentum expectation

\[ m=\langle J_z\rangle . \]

The code uses the Green-function convention \(G=-\chi\). Consequently, a positive static susceptibility corresponds to \(G_0(0)<0\), and the exact bare single-ion response is written

\[ \frac{dm}{dh_{\mathrm{MF}}}=-G_0(0;h_{\mathrm{MF}})>0 . \tag{1} \]
Numerical control For the 136-state electronuclear single-ion producer, a central finite-difference check verifies equation (1) to a maximum relative error of \(2.57\times10^{-7}\) over the sampled 0.5--2.9 T sections. The sign and bare response are therefore not the source of the thermodynamic ambiguity.

2. The constrained Legendre potential

At fixed temperature, define the field-dependent free energy per ion by

\[ f(H,T)=-\frac{1}{\beta N}\log Z(H,T). \tag{2} \]

With a Zeeman term \(-\mu H\sum_iJ_{iz}\), its field differential is

\[ df=-\mu m\,dH=-m\,dh_{\mathrm{app}}. \tag{3} \]

The Legendre transform from applied field to constrained moment is

\[ \phi(m,T)=f(H,T)+h_{\mathrm{app}}m . \tag{4} \]

Using equation (3),

\[ d\phi =df+h_{\mathrm{app}}\,dm+m\,dh_{\mathrm{app}} =h_{\mathrm{app}}\,dm . \tag{5} \]

Thus \(\phi(m,T)\) is the magnetic effective potential at fixed magnetization. Depending on thermodynamic naming conventions it may be called a constrained Helmholtz potential, magnetic Legendre potential, or effective action for the uniform moment. The unambiguous definition is equation (4).

Important distinction The quantity analyzed here is a correction to \(\phi(m,T)\), not directly to \(f(H,T)\). A fixed-moment comparison and a fixed-applied- field equilibrium comparison are different operations.

3. Jensen's modified mean field and the fluctuation shift

Jensen introduces three related longitudinal fields. In energy units, write

\[ h_0=h_{\mathrm{app}}+J_0m , \qquad h_{\mathrm{MF}}=h_0-\delta h , \qquad \delta h=h_0-h_{\mathrm{MF}} . \tag{6} \]

Here \(h_0\) is the usual molecular field, \(h_{\mathrm{MF}}\) is the field used to define the reference single-ion Hamiltonian, and \(\delta h\) is the fluctuation-induced field correction. Jensen chooses \(h_{\mathrm{MF}}\) so that the moment of the reference ion equals the interacting moment. In the original two-level construction,

\[ \langle J_z\rangle =\langle J_z\rangle_0 =m_{\mathrm{diag}}\,n_{01}. \tag{7} \]

This equality cancels one-point cumulants, or tadpoles, in the perturbation expansion.

Compare the corrected theory and mean-field theory at the same single-ion state \(h_{\mathrm{MF}}\), and therefore at the same moment. The applied fields needed to produce that state differ by \(\delta h\). If

\[ \delta\phi(m) =\phi_{1/z}(m)-\phi_{\mathrm{MF}}(m), \tag{8} \]

then equation (5) gives Jensen's fixed-moment relation

\[ d(\delta\phi)=\delta h\,dm . \tag{9} \]

Equation (9) is the thermodynamic identity underlying the potential check. It is stronger than simply having a converged Green-function fixed point: it assumes that the approximation supplies a consistent free energy and response.

4. Derivation of the field equation

Let \(\widetilde G_0(0;h_{\mathrm{MF}})\) be the effective local static propagator after the fluctuation self-energy has been incorporated but before the final uniform lattice denominator. Jensen's response argument yields

\[ \frac{dh_0}{dh_{\mathrm{MF}}} = \frac{G_0(0;h_{\mathrm{MF}})} {\widetilde G_0(0;h_{\mathrm{MF}})} \equiv r(h_{\mathrm{MF}}). \tag{10} \]

Integration gives the familiar field mapping

\[ h_0(h_{\mathrm{MF}}) = \int_0^{h_{\mathrm{MF}}}r(u)\,du , \tag{11} \]

provided the path from zero is defined and the chosen integration constant is legitimate.

The spectra calculation applies a transverse field \(B_x\) but no external longitudinal field. Then \(h_0=J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}m(h)\), so the ordered longitudinal state must solve

\[ F(h) \equiv \int_0^h r(u)\,du-J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}m(h)=0 . \]

This is why missing or branch-ambiguous values of \(r\) affect the single-ion field used by the final susceptibility and spectra even when the final real-frequency solver itself is healthy.

From the definition \(\delta h=h_0-h_{\mathrm{MF}}\), equation (10) implies

\[ \frac{d\delta h}{dh_{\mathrm{MF}}}=r(h_{\mathrm{MF}})-1 . \tag{12} \]

Equations (9) and (12) are the two differential identities combined by the fold-anchored calculation.

5. What the rigorous construction actually requires

Equation (11) looks like an ordinary one-dimensional quadrature, but its integrand is the output of a nonlinear many-body closure. At each value of \(h\), production must first solve three logically distinct problems:

Layer Question What “converged” means
Static medium Does the bounded static closure have an admissible root? Its residual closes and the supremum, uniform, and static-lattice masses remain in the physical domain.
Coupled node Do \(\Sigma\), \(K\), the moments \(\lambda_p\), and the static response solve their simultaneous equations? The original or exactly reduced coupled residual vanishes and the dynamic masses are admissible. A Picard iteration merely reaching or missing that root is a separate question.
Field path Which coupled root is followed as \(h\) varies, and does that component connect the integration endpoints? A continuous, certified branch and its integration constant have been specified.
Thermodynamics Is the selected response the derivative of a state function, and does the chosen branch minimize the correct fixed-field potential? A stationary functional or an equivalent integrability derivation supplies path independence, an anchor, and a stability/selection rule.

Therefore a finite list of values of \(r\), or even a highly accurate integral over those values, is not sufficient. For equation (11) to define a thermodynamic field map, the following four objects must exist together:

  1. a coupled solution at every required integration coordinate;
  2. a rule selecting one branch when several roots coexist;
  3. a justified integration constant; and
  4. a path on which \(r(h)\) is a sufficiently regular, single-valued response of one consistent approximation.
Terminology “The \(r(h)\) integral does not converge” has been used informally for several different failures. The decisive production failure is better stated as the required thermodynamic path is incomplete and unselected. On every certified smooth piece tested so far, ordinary numerical integration itself behaves normally.

6. Absolute fluctuation potential and its anchor

In Jensen's original closed two-level setting, fluctuations are assumed to vanish at infinite-field saturation \(m=M_0\). The normalization is

\[ \delta\phi(M_0)=0 . \tag{13} \]

Integrating equation (9) from saturation to a finite moment gives

\[ \delta\phi(m) = \int_{M_0}^{m}\delta h(m')\,dm' = -\int_m^{M_0}\delta h(m')\,dm' . \tag{14} \]

At \(m=0\), equation (14) is Jensen's free-energy consistency test: the field-integrated result should agree with the free-energy correction obtained independently by temperature-integrating the interaction energy.

Why the absolute route is unavailable here The production hybrid does not display a controlled integrable high-field tail within the valid truncated manifold. Setting \(\delta\phi=0\) at a finite cutoff would insert an arbitrary additive constant. The absolute form (14) was therefore not used.

6.1 Why neither endpoint currently supplies the constant

The lower anchor in equation (11) is unavailable because the strict \(h=0\) ordered predictor has no accepted coupled static closure over the tested field range. At 1 T, independent reduced and unreduced constructions agree on a smooth moment-closed branch at \(h=0\), but its pole-cancelled static residual remains positive by \(1158.79\)--\(2210.78\ \mathrm{meV}^{-1}\). A raw residual sign change at a removable local pole is therefore not a missing physical root. These completed branch and multistart checks are strong local/profile evidence; they do not constitute an interval proof against every disconnected root.

Structural endpoint obstruction This failure is stronger than an unsuccessful predictor. With no longitudinal applied field, symmetry gives \(m(0)=0\), hence \(F(0)=0\). If a differentiable certified path also reaches a nonzero ordered root \(F(h_*)=0\), Rolle's theorem requires some \(c\in(0,h_*)\) with \(F'(c)=0\). Provided \(r(c)\) is finite and nonzero, the derivative identity in section 7.5 below then gives \(1+J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G_0(c)=0\): the path must touch the uniform stability boundary. If \(r(0)>0\) and its initial slope is already negative, the zero-field endpoint itself lies on the unstable side. Thus no smooth path from the symmetric root to a distinct ordered root can remain strictly inside the positive-uniform-mass domain throughout. This conditional theorem does not enumerate disconnected roots, but it explains why enforcing the production stability gate cannot merely reveal a missing stable bridge to \(h=0\).

The upper anchor is a valid idea in a closed saturating model, but the present hybrid does not reach it in a controlled regime. On certified nodes spanning 8 to 128 times the ordinary profile ceiling,

\[ |r(h)-1|\sim h^{-p},\qquad p=0.593\ \text{at 1 T},\quad p=0.811\ \text{at 4 T}. \]

Both fitted exponents are below the \(p>1\) condition needed for \(\int^\infty|r-1|\,dh\) to converge. The matched closed two-level control gives \(p=1.733\) and \(1.568\), showing that saturation anchoring is coherent in that closed theory. The finite 136-state electronuclear calculation eventually saturates only at longitudinal fields large enough to probe omitted higher multiplets, outside the controlled validity of the retained crystal-field model. Extending to that numerical saturation would therefore manufacture, rather than derive, the integration constant.

7. Numerical anatomy of the non-convergence

7.1 The original mask and what it did—and did not—prove

The strict production profile requires a certified \(h=0\) predictor and a contiguous set of positive-\(h\) nodes. Instead, the controlled 0.5--2.2 T census found rejected low-\(h\) nodes followed by a certified high-\(h\) block. This makes the integral in equation (11) undefined under the strict contract. It does not imply that the susceptibility is zero, that every failed node has no exact root, or that equation (10) is algebraically false.

Several genuine numerical issues were eliminated without filling the gap. Transactional rollback stopped failed iterates from contaminating later warm starts, and exact reciprocal arithmetic removed a spurious nonfinite evaluation at a removable local pole. Nested 33/65/129-node grids then gave deterministic shared-node verdicts, but the independent \(h=0\) failure and the missing lower component remained. This is why grid density, arithmetic rearrangement, or more iterations cannot be the underlying resolution.

7.2 Exact reduction separates root existence from solver attraction

The dynamic equations were reduced exactly to four unknowns, \((\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3,x)\), with every nonzero Matsubara \(K_n\) eliminated by a bounded scalar equation. The reduced residual reproduces a certified 1 T node to \(7.11\times10^{-15}\), and an independent evaluation of all 740 original self-energy components confirms the same solution geometry. The conclusions below are therefore not artifacts of the reduced coordinates.

At \(B_x=1\) T, pseudo-arclength continuation finds a regular saddle-node at

\[ (h_f,s_f) =(0.0054789314231\ \mathrm{meV},\,0.6565777265), \qquad s=-J_{\mathrm{sup}}x . \]

All measured physical margins remain positive at the turn. The branch then returns toward larger \(h\) and reaches the explicit \(s=1\) static-stability boundary near \(h=0.0097188640\) meV. Thus the known component has a genuine minimum in \(h\); decreasing the fixed field in smaller steps cannot carry it to zero.

Mathematically, if the coupled equations are \(\mathcal R(\mathbf y,h)=0\), the implicit-function theorem supplies a local graph \(\mathbf y(h)\) only while \(\partial_{\mathbf y}\mathcal R\) is invertible. At the observed fold,

\[ \det\!\left(\partial_{\mathbf y}\mathcal R\right)=0, \qquad \frac{dh}{ds}=0, \qquad \frac{d^2h}{ds^2}>0 , \]

while the augmented pseudo-arclength Jacobian remains full rank. The solution curve is smooth in its arclength \(s\), but it is no longer a single-valued graph over \(h\). This is precisely why a fixed-\(h\) iterator can stop even though pseudo-arclength continuation proceeds. Because all propagator masses remain positive at this turn, calling it a thermodynamic spinodal would require a derived functional; what is established is a saddle-node of the self-consistency equations. A separate disconnected low-\(h\) component has not been excluded.

At the same \(h=0.006\) meV, the original equations have two admissible roots,

\[ s_L=0.412077554837,\qquad s_H=0.809166172289. \]

Their outer-map eigenvalues are approximately \(0.4173\) and \(2.6434\), while the fold has eigenvalue \(1\). Picard iteration can attract the low-\(s\) root, loses contraction at the fold, and repels from the high-\(s\) root. No positive scalar damping can make a real mode \(\lambda>1\) contractive because its damped eigenvalue is

\[ \lambda_{\mathrm{damped}}=1-a+a\lambda>1 \qquad (a>0,\ \lambda>1). \]

Negative \(a\) can act as a numerical branch selector when all relevant multipliers of the target root lie on the same side of one. For example, \(a=-0.6\) maps \(\lambda=2.6434\) to approximately \(0.014\), while it makes the low-sheet multiplier approximately \(1.35\). This does not regularize the fold: \(\lambda=1\) is fixed by the entire affine family \(\lambda\mapsto1+a(\lambda-1)\). A scalar choice also fails whenever the target spectrum straddles one. Negative damping may therefore find a known repelling root, but it cannot repair the saddle-node or supply a thermodynamic branch rule.

Key distinction Picard non-convergence is an iteration-stability statement, not a root-existence or thermodynamic-stability statement. Exact continuation later found Picard-repelling high roots at 0.8, 0.9, and 1.1 T with residuals below \(3.25\times10^{-10}\).

7.3 The field dependence is not described by one universal failure

Exact cross-field continuation finds two roots on diagnostic sections from 0.5 through 1.2 T. Both persist to 0.355 T on an explicitly extrapolated diagnostic path, but correctors fail at 0.350 T; that brackets a merger or termination without yet certifying its local type. The high branch reaches the uniform boundary near 1.2167 T.

In contrast, at 2.5, 2.7, and 2.9 T the known branch was extended to \(s=0.999999\), and fifteen constrained full-residual starts found no second root. This is strong finite evidence for a one-root region near the sampled branch, not a theorem excluding every disconnected component. The low-field two-sheet topology is therefore important but not universal across the entire magnetic-field axis.

7.4 Why filtering failed as a rigorous construction

Discarding failed or nonfinite nodes can leave a smooth-looking retained integral and can even produce plausible final spectra. It cannot infer the omitted contribution, select between \(L\) and \(H\), or determine the additive field constant. A two-endpoint trapezoid similarly bridged negative- and positive-mass states across an unresolved component boundary. Those experiments demonstrate that final observables may be insensitive to some integral detail; they do not turn the omitted area into a thermodynamic zero.

7.5 A second obstruction: the physical uniform boundary

On any differentiable certified component, the ordered field residual

\[ F(h)=\int_0^h r(u)\,du-J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}m(h) \]

has the exact derivative

\[ F'(h) =r(h)\left[1+J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G_0(h)\right]. \]

A zero of the uniform mass \(1+J_{0,\mathrm{eff}}\widetilde G_0\) is therefore a stationary point of \(F\) on that component. At 4 T, adaptive continuation reaches such a uniform/supremum boundary near \(h=0.0080428632\) meV while the outer map remains locally contractive and the other dynamic margins remain positive. This differs from the 1 T regular fold, where all masses are positive but the fixed-\(h\) coupled Jacobian loses rank. The failure set contains at least these two mechanisms; no universal damping or step-size change can repair both.

The distinction is also visible in the augmented equations \(\mathcal G=(\mathcal R,F)\). Wherever \(\partial_{\mathbf y}\mathcal R\) is invertible, their block determinant has the Schur-complement form

\[ \det \partial_{(\mathbf y,h)}\mathcal G =\det(\partial_{\mathbf y}\mathcal R) \left[ \partial_hF-\partial_{\mathbf y}F (\partial_{\mathbf y}\mathcal R)^{-1} \partial_h\mathcal R \right]. \]

The first factor loses rank at the regular 1 T fluctuation-sector fold; the bracket is the derivative of \(F\) along a regular coupled branch and vanishes at the uniform boundary. Joint pseudo-arclength continuation of \((\mathbf y,h)\) can expose both geometries without confusing them with nested-iterator failure. It still cannot decide which sheet is physical or manufacture a free-energy functional.

8. Fold-anchored relative potential of two sheets

At \(B_x=1\) T, the exact reduced equations have a low-\(s\) sheet and a high-\(s\) return sheet. They merge at the certified regular fold

\[ h_f=0.0054789314231\ \mathrm{meV}, \qquad s_f=0.6565777265 . \tag{15} \]

Denote the sheets by \(L\) and \(H\), and define

\[ \Delta r(h)=r_H(h)-r_L(h). \tag{16} \]

8.1 Cancellation of the unknown field constant

If both sheets are branches of one continuous constrained functional, then at the common fold they have the same state and the same \(\delta h_f\). Integrating equation (12) separately along each sheet therefore gives

\[ \delta h_H(t)-\delta h_L(t) = \int_{h_f}^{t}\Delta r(u)\,du . \tag{17} \]

The unknown absolute value \(\delta h_f\) cancels. This is the central advantage of the fold-anchored comparison: it does not fill the missing interval between \(h=0\) and the fold.

General fold gauge Conditional on two sheets belonging to one continuous stationary construction, their common regular fold is a canonical relative normalization: the sheets are the same state at the branch point, so an unknown additive constant cancels in their difference. This is a local relative statement. It neither supplies an absolute anchor nor proves that the assumed stationary construction exists.

8.2 Potential difference at the same constrained moment

Both sheets at a given \(h_{\mathrm{MF}}\) use the same bare single-ion state, so they share the same moment \(m(h_{\mathrm{MF}})\). Applying equation (9) to their difference gives

\[ \Delta\phi(h) \equiv \phi_H(h)-\phi_L(h) = \int_{m_f}^{m(h)} \big[\delta h_H(m')-\delta h_L(m')\big]\,dm' . \tag{18} \]

Parameterizing the moment by \(t=h_{\mathrm{MF}}\) and using equation (17),

\[ \Delta\phi(h) = \int_{h_f}^{h} m'(t) \left[ \int_{h_f}^{t}\Delta r(u)\,du \right]dt . \tag{19} \]

Reversing the order of integration produces the numerically more direct single-integral form

\[ \boxed{ \Delta\phi(h) = \int_{h_f}^{h} \Delta r(u)\,[m(h)-m(u)]\,du } . \tag{20} \]

Since \(h\) has units of meV and \(m\) is dimensionless, \(\Delta\phi\) is in meV per ion.

Independent algebra review Equations (2)--(12) and the Fubini transformation from (19) to (20) were independently re-derived with the same sign and orientation. The \(1.72\times10^{-9}\) meV/ion difference between the two numerical evaluations is \(1.2\times10^{-3}\) relative and is smaller than the alternate-node coarsening change; it is consistent with quadrature, not an error in equation (20). This validates the conditional calculation, not its unproved thermodynamic premise.

9. What the negative result means

Under the assumptions behind equations (9), (12), and (17),

\[ \Delta\phi(h)<0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \phi_H(m(h))<\phi_L(m(h)). \tag{21} \]

In words: at the same constrained single-ion moment, the high-\(s\) sheet has the lower inferred fluctuation Legendre potential.

This result is not contradicted by the coupling-constant audit. The low sheet connects continuously to zero interaction, while the high sheet terminates at a uniform boundary at finite coupling. That makes the high sheet interaction-born, but it does not by itself make the sheet unphysical: a legitimate broken-symmetry branch can also terminate at a finite critical coupling while the zero-coupling branch is unstable. Conversely, connectivity to zero coupling does not prove equilibrium. The coupling path and the fold integral therefore remain two non-selecting diagnostics until a branch-specific physical signature or a derived stationary functional breaks the tie.

10. Why this is not yet an equilibrium comparison

A physical calculation at a specified applied energy field \(h_{\mathrm{app}}\) requires the fixed-field potential

\[ f_j(h_{\mathrm{app}}) = \phi_j(m)-h_{\mathrm{app}}m . \tag{22} \]

Each candidate state must first satisfy the stationarity condition

\[ \frac{\partial\phi_j}{\partial m}=h_{\mathrm{app}} . \tag{23} \]

The equilibrium state is then the stationary candidate with the lowest value of equation (22). Two states compared at the same \(h_{\mathrm{MF}}\) share the same bare moment, but their inferred \(\delta h\) values—and hence their applied fields—can differ. Therefore equation (20) alone does not rank equilibrium phases at one common applied field.

Interpretation boundary The calculation supplies a same-moment constrained ranking. It does not by itself supply the complete fixed-applied-field phase selection.

11. Why the production hybrid blocks a rigorous free-energy label

11.1 The same-ion premise in Jensen's derivation

In the published two-level construction, all of the following are generated by the same reference single-ion Hamiltonian:

  • the moment \(m_{\mathrm{diag}}n_{01}\);
  • the bare propagator \(G_0\);
  • the elastic and inelastic static weights;
  • the connected four-point cumulant producing \(\Sigma\); and
  • the resummed elastic factor \(\xi\).

This correspondence is what connects the diagrammatic response, tadpole cancellation, and the thermodynamic differential (9).

11.2 What production currently combines

Object Production source
Moment \(m(h)\) 136-state electronuclear single-ion Hamiltonian
Bare response \(G_0\) 136-state electronuclear susceptibility
\(m,M,\Delta,n_{01}\) entering the vertex Separate 17-state electronic ion, reduced to two levels
Self-energy \(\Sigma\) and elastic factor \(\xi\) Jensen electronic two-level formulas

Consequently, the vertex used to dress the 136-state propagator is not the connected four-point cumulant of the same reference system.

11.3 What Hilbert-space truncation does—and does not—explain

A finite basis is not, by itself, incompatible with thermodynamics. Any finite-dimensional Hamiltonian has a well-defined partition function, free energy, moment, and response. A truncated approximation can therefore be internally thermodynamic if its moment, two-point propagator, self-energy/vertex, and free-energy correction are all derived within the same retained space and the observables of interest are converged as that space is enlarged.

Three different representation issues must not be conflated:

Representation Role in this investigation Conclusion
136-state electronuclear ion Production moment and bare \(G_0\) Internally satisfies \(dm/dh=-G_0\) on the tested fields. It is not the source of the sign or derivative problem.
Electronic two-level sector from a separate 17-state ion Production \(\Sigma\), \(\xi\), and vertex parameters Not the same statistical producer as the 136-state response; Jensen's same-ion thermodynamic proof no longer closes.
Field-adapted lowest 16 of the 136 electronuclear states Tested as a possible internally closed reduced vertex Spectrally smooth and thermally complete, but not converged for connected four-point fluctuation content.

The lowest-16 test is particularly instructive. Its 16/17 energy gap is 0.618--1.235 meV, its retained thermal population is essentially one, and adjacent subspace overlaps exceed 0.999996. Those facts would make it look excellent to an energy- or population-based truncation test. Nevertheless, at 0.5 T it contains only 9.86% of the connected variance at the first accepted node and 0.521% at the high-\(h\) endpoint. On the tested rank ladder \(16,24,32,48,64,96,136\), all 136 states are needed to exceed 90% connected-variance coverage at every non-predictor sample. Connected-variance coverage is an appropriate warning for a four-point construction, but it is not itself a norm or rigorous error bound on the complete connected vertex. A constructive convergence test should be stated in moment/Krylov terms appropriate to the correlation order: a four-point vertex probes multiparticle states generated from operator- acted vectors, not only the one-particle energy subspace. The present coverage census has not yet established that stronger rank criterion.

Physical lesson Thermally unoccupied states can contribute virtually to a susceptibility and even more strongly to a connected four-point vertex. Energy convergence, retained Boltzmann weight, and two-point static-weight convergence do not certify a fluctuation vertex. The relevant truncation criterion is tied to the correlation order entering the approximation.

Conversely, replacing production by a closed electronic two-level model does restore same-ion consistency but still does not create the missing low-\(h\) component under the tested bounded solver. It fails every tested \(h=0\) predictor and, at 1 T, encounters a dynamic-domain/moment mismatch before static closure. Representation inconsistency is therefore the decisive reason that the thermodynamic proof fails, but it is not by itself a complete explanation of the root topology; disconnected roots in that control remain an open completeness question.

11.4 Quantitative same-ion audit

Across the sampled 0.5--2.9 T sections,

\[ \frac{G_{0,\mathrm{full}}}{G_{0,\mathrm{twolevel}}} =0.146938\text{--}0.889455, \tag{24} \] \[ m_{\mathrm{full}}-m_{\mathrm{twolevel}} =0.0953704\text{--}1.32617. \tag{25} \]

At exact hybrid roots, retaining the converged \((K,\lambda,\Sigma)\) but replacing the hybrid static weights with the Jensen-consistent two-level weights shifts the signed local inelastic denominator

\[ D_{\mathrm{loc}} =1+\Sigma(0)+K(0)G_{0,\mathrm{inel}}(0), \]

in absolute amount by \(0.004228\)--\(0.432507\) across the retained exact roots. At the point that previously produced the headline factor-178 change in \(G_{\mathrm{stat}}\), the underlying replacement is \(D_{\mathrm{loc}}:0.285577\to0.00905581\). At the 1.2 T high root it changes sign, \(0.408513\to-0.0239942\). The response ratio is strongly amplified when this denominator approaches zero, so factor 178 is a poor measure of representation error and is not used as one here. The unamplified bare-response and moment differences in equations (24)--(25), together with these signed denominator shifts, are the relevant same-producer audit.

Disposition The numerical ratio \(r=G_0/\widetilde G_0\) is well defined for the hybrid equations, and equation (20) is a reproducible integral of that ratio. What is not established is the stronger statement that this integral is the variation of a thermodynamic free-energy functional. Jensen's same-ion proof cannot be transferred unchanged.

This does not prove that no alternative functional can exist. It proves that such a functional is not the published closed two-level functional and must be separately derived.

11.5 The mathematical issue: integrability and stationarity

The deeper mathematical requirement is that the closure be \(\Phi\)-derivable, or otherwise be shown to arise from a stationary state function. Schematically, let \(y_i\) denote internal variables such as self-energy, medium, or moment parameters. A candidate constrained functional would have a differential of the form

\[ d\mathcal P =\delta h\,dm+\sum_i R_i(m,\mathbf y)\,dy_i , \tag{26} \]

where its stationary equations are \(R_i=0\). On a stationary branch, equation (26) reduces to \(d\mathcal P=\delta h\,dm\), which is the structure used in equation (9). In canonical conjugate coordinates, a twice-differentiable \(\mathcal P\) also imposes the reciprocity conditions

\[ \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y_j} =\frac{\partial R_j}{\partial y_i}, \qquad \frac{\partial\delta h}{\partial y_i} =\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial m}. \tag{27} \]

Jensen's common cumulant construction is what supplies this stationary correspondence in the closed two-level theory. Production instead combines derivatives and vertices generated by different reference systems. The ratio \(r\) can still be evaluated algebraically, but there is no derivation showing that the resulting one-form is exact or that its integral is path independent.

A tested attempt to rescue the existing residual with a fitted diagonal metric failed because the residual Jacobian contains a one-way cross derivative: one member of a proposed reciprocal pair is structurally zero while the other is nonzero. On shell, a nonsingular diagonal multiplier would require \(d_iJ_{ij}=d_jJ_{ji}\); the one-way pair therefore rules out the whole nonsingular diagonal family, not only the fitted coefficients. It does not rule out a full matrix multiplier, nonlinear coordinates, or a more general mixed functional. Pointwise symmetrization with an indefinite full matrix is not the hard part; the unresolved requirement is a smooth, coherent multiplier field whose differential is integrable over the solution manifold. For the fixed residual, a symmetric positive-definite multiplier additionally requires a real, diagonalizable Jacobian. These are scoped necessary conditions, not a proof that no stationary functional exists.

The same distinction applies to stability. Positivity of the propagator masses is a necessary physical-domain gate for the implemented closure, but it is not automatically the Hessian test for a thermodynamic minimum. The latter would require the Hessian of \(\mathcal P\), including relaxation of the internal variables through the appropriate Schur complement. This is why neither the outer Picard eigenvalue nor an endpoint mass alone can select equilibrium.

12.1 Why the two branch diagnostics are not contradictory

The coupling path gives the low sheet adiabatic connectivity to the zero-interaction hybrid state. The fold-anchored field integral conditionally gives the high sheet the lower same-moment proxy. These statements answer different questions and use different anchors. Neither is a selector: an interaction-born branch can be the legitimate ordered branch, while a non-\(\Phi\)-derivable closure need not give path-independent potential differences. Their disagreement is evidence of missing thermodynamic structure, not a statistically valid error bar and not a license to average the two branches.

12.2 Why completing the root search would still not finish the proof

A mathematically complete enumeration of all coupled roots would settle the topology, but it would not by itself choose the equilibrium root, supply the field-integration constant, or prove that \(r\,dh\) is the differential of a state function. Conversely, deriving a stationary functional would clarify selection and admissibility, but its stationary manifold might still contain folds, spinodals, and multiple phases that require continuation and free-energy comparison. The numerical and thermodynamic tasks are coupled, but neither substitutes for the other.

Present conclusion The rigorous route is not proven impossible in principle. It is incomplete for the current production hybrid and would require a substantial new many-body derivation—not another damping choice, quadrature grid, root-polishing method, or silent interpolation across rejected nodes.

  • The full 136-state bare producer is internally differentiated correctly: \(dm/dh=-G_0\) is verified numerically.
  • The exact reduced and original equations agree on certified roots, the regular 1 T fold, and the two-sheet geometry.
  • The ratio \(r=G_0/\widetilde G_0\) is a well-defined hybrid observable at each certified root. Integrals over a declared sheet are reproducible numerical diagnostics.
  • The fold-anchored integral legitimately cancels a common unknown constant conditional on both sheets belonging to one stationary constrained functional. Its sign can guide, but cannot certify, a later approximation.
  • The strict mask correctly reports that the required ordered field profile is incomplete under the current acceptance contract; it should not be reinterpreted as zero susceptibility.
  • The initial throw-away-NaN experiment has been replaced by an explicit positive missing-area ensemble propagated through the final real-axis susceptibility. Its spread measures sensitivity to three declared completions on one numerical branch; it is not evidence that the omitted area is zero, a probability distribution, or an equilibrium uncertainty interval.
  • A frozen two-sided adjacent-field retry reproducibly recovers the interior 0.36 and 0.45 T masks at 129 nodes for all three area members. It does not authorize a one-sided retry at the high-field boundary or a copied value at exact zero field.

These surviving results provide a clean boundary for future work: retain certified component integrals and exact-root data; represent the unknown area, branch choice, and anchor as explicit assumptions; and propagate their spread into fields, susceptibility, and spectra. That is an approximation program informed by the rigorous investigation, rather than an unlabelled continuation of the failed proof.

15. What would be required to promote the diagnostic

Calling \(\Delta\phi\) a rigorous thermodynamic potential difference would require at least one of the following internally consistent constructions:

  1. Derive the connected multilevel electronuclear four-point vertex and use it with the same electronuclear moment and \(G_0\). Its convergence must be tested by a four-point criterion, not only by energy levels or static spectral weight.
  2. Derive an explicit mixed functional whose stationary variations reproduce the current full-\(G_0\)/two-level-vertex closure, including the elastic resummation and effective-medium equations.
  3. Replace the hybrid by a closed response/vertex representation and demonstrate convergence of its relevant observables and of the required field path. The tested closed electronic two-level control does not yet meet this condition.

Each route carries a different burden. A full multilevel construction is closest to the physical single-ion problem but requires a feasible connected four-point vertex and, for an infinite-field anchor, a controlled completion of omitted higher multiplets. A mixed-functional construction could preserve the current economical hybrid, but it must derive rather than assume its conjugate variables, stationary equations, response, and additive constant. A smaller closed model may be computationally attractive, but it must demonstrate rank convergence of the susceptibility and vertex and reproduce the observables for which it will be used.

Any candidate should then pass independent thermodynamic checks:

  • field-path and coupling-path independence where both exist;
  • agreement between the field integral and temperature-integrated interaction energy;
  • equality of mixed derivatives or closed-loop work;
  • consistent fixed-applied-field stationary points; and
  • a controlled absolute integration constant or a legitimate relative matching condition.

A useful certification sequence would be: verify the single-ion derivative identity and response/Jacobian correspondence; test local mixed-derivative consistency on the solved manifold; only if it passes, choose a legitimate gauge and close loops in \((h,\rho,T)\); compare the field and coupling routes where both share an anchor; then continue all stationary branches and compare the fixed-applied-field potential. A failure at any earlier stage prevents a later free-energy label even if the final quadrature is numerically stable.

15.1 An anchor-free Maxwell test proposed by the review

One review suggestion gives a sharper first test that does not require the missing field interval. Let \(\rho\) uniformly scale the physical interaction, let \(\delta\langle\partial_\rho\mathcal H\rangle\) be the interaction- energy difference from the consistently referenced mean-field theory, and let \(\chi_0=dm/dh\). At fixed \((h,T)\), the bare single-ion moment is independent of \(\rho\). A common differentiable constrained potential would then require

\[ \boxed{ \partial_h\!\left[ \frac{1}{\chi_0(h)} \partial_h\, \delta\!\left\langle\partial_\rho\mathcal H\right\rangle \right] =\partial_\rho r }. \]

The extra \(h\) derivative removes the unknown slice anchor because \(\partial_h\partial_\rho\delta h=\partial_\rho r\). Every stencil derivative must be a total derivative along the solved manifold: the closure must be re-solved at every \((h,\rho)\) point, and all routes must use the same mean-field subtraction. A resolved violation at one certified stencil would disprove this common-potential condition without any missing-area quadrature. Agreement on finitely many stencils would be supporting evidence, not proof, and the differentiated test is blind to a discrepancy constant in \(h\) at fixed \(\rho\); a fold-gauged undifferentiated check would still be required for that case.

This test, the analogous temperature-route test, loop holonomies, split coupling homotopies, and the predicted fold Puiseux exponents have not been run. They are research proposals, not part of the verified checkpoint-27 conclusion. Closed-loop periods become topological invariants only after local curvature has first been shown to vanish.

Recommended terminology Until those obligations are met, use fold-anchored relative constrained-potential diagnostic or effective potential proxy. Avoid calling it the Gibbs free energy, Helmholtz free energy, or a certified equilibrium free-energy difference without the stated qualification.
