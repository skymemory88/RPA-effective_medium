## Assessment

The diagnosis is sound and the mechanism is correctly identified: the ω=0 medium is a Stieltjes transform of the coupling multiset evaluated at an argument that migrates into the pole cloud, and the amplification $C=\partial\Sigma(0)/\partial K(0)\approx 7.8\times10^2$ turns that into a non-contractive map. The refusal to regularise, to interpolate failed nodes, or to promote a gate-failed candidate on convergence evidence is all correct. Four places where I think the record is wrong or incomplete:

**1. The static closure's root structure is fully known, and the physical root always exists.**
At fixed $G_{\rm stat}$, $S_N(y)=\frac1N\sum_i(J_i-y)^{-1}$ has $S_N'=\frac1N\sum(J_i-y)^{-2}>0$ everywhere. $S_N$ is therefore Herglotz: strictly increasing on each of the $N{+}1$ pole intervals, sweeping $(-\infty,+\infty)$ on interior ones. So $S_N(y)=G_{\rm stat}<0$ has **exactly one root per interval**, $N$ roots total, all with `resid_static`$\to$0 — which is §2.6 in closed form — and the physical one is unambiguously the rightmost, $y>\max_q J_\nu$, since $D_q=G_{\rm stat}(J_q-y)$.

That root exists for *every* $G_{\rm stat}<0$. §9.3's "the resummed closure has no root on the physical branch" is too strong. What actually happens deep in the ordered phase is worse and more diagnosable: for $|G_{\rm stat}|$ large the rightmost root is pinned at $y-\max_q J_\nu\approx 1/(N|G_{\rm stat}|)$ — about $6\times10^{-8}$ meV at 1 T, 0.31 K — so $K_0\to\max_q J_\nu + 1/G_{\rm stat}$. A quantity that is supposed to be $O(1/z)$ relative to $\mathcal J(0)$ saturates at $\max_q J_\nu\approx\mathcal J(0)$. **$K_0/\mathcal J(0)\to 1$ is the cleanest single statement that the medium has left the $1/z$ regime**, and it is grid-determined.

**2. `omit_max` is not a valid error estimate inside the support.**
From (16)+(31), $K=x+1/S(x)=\mu_1+\mu_2/x+\mu_3/x^2+\dots$ with $x=(1+\Sigma)/\chi_0^{cc}$. That series has radius of convergence $\max_q|J_\nu|$ — the *same* support condition. Inside it the asymptotic term ratio is $\max J/|x|>1$, while the implemented first ratio $\mu_3/(\mu_2x)$ stays small: at 0.31 K/3.6 T, $x=5.65$ μeV against $\max J_\nu=5.99$ μeV (asymptotic ratio 1.06) yet `omit`$=0.0668$. This reconciles §3.1 with §10.1's refusal to conclude control, and it means §9.4(viii)'s temperature re-reading of clause (c) does *not* rehabilitate the strict scheme inside the support. Report $\max_q|J_\nu|/|x|$ alongside `omit_max`; it is free.

**3. The QCP deliverable's ordered coverage is bounded by the Γ-exclusion sliver.**
Sliver width $=\big(\mathcal J(0)-\max_qJ_\nu\big)/|dx/dB|$; the measured gaps (0.961/0.601/0.439/0.339 μeV at 8/12/16/20³) scale as $\sim N^{-1.1}$ and extrapolate to zero with no constant offset. All 19 accepted Jensen columns in the verified window sit within 0.093 T of $B_c^{1/z}=4.6925$ T, against an estimated ~0.29 T sliver at 16³. The verification checklist requires three grids for continuation-domain claims but **not** for the QCP observable. That gap should be closed before the spectrum is compared with experiment.

**4. Candidate B has better provenance than it is given.** Framework §10's own recipe says the BZ averages are done "via a density-of-states representation of $\mathcal J(\mathbf q)$". The empirical 16384-term sum is the departure. Also: the measured edge-mode scaling implies $\rho(J)$ vanishes at the top edge, so $S_\infty(J_{\rm top}^+)$ is **finite** — meaning the continuum theory has a genuine, grid-independent threshold $|G_{\rm stat}|=|S_\infty(J_{\rm top})|$ where the outer branch terminates. Order of magnitude: $|S_\infty|\sim$ few hundred meV$^{-1}$, i.e. right where the measured $|\tilde G_0|$ values cross.

## Proposals, cheapest first

**P1 — Locate the two degenerate roots in the pole ladder (hours).** For §11.2's pair at $h=0.011717\ldots$ meV ($r=0.768$ vs $0.822$), compute $y=K_0-1/G_{\rm stat}$ for each and find its index in sorted $J_\nu$. Different intervals ⇒ branch identity is an *integer* and the static branch-selection problem is solved constructively. Same interval ⇒ the multiplicity lives in the $\Sigma$/$\xi$ coupling and the medium is exonerated. Either outcome is worth more than another continuation campaign.

**P2 — Replace the inner Picard with a branch-indexed monotone solve.** Bisect/Newton $S_N(y)=G_{\rm stat}$ on a declared interval (default: rightmost). Exact algebra, unconditionally convergent, flag-gated ⇒ respects I1–I5. Report $y$, interval index, and $\delta=y-\max_qJ_\nu$ against the local level spacing. This converts chaotic `node_failed` into a rigorous statement: *the physical root exists but is grid-pinned, therefore this column is not computable in this scheme*. It does not manufacture physics — the pinned root must be rejected — but it makes the failure exact rather than inferred.

**P3 — Grid gate on the QCP window.** Rerun 4.60–4.6925 T at 12³/20³/24³; report accepted-ordered count and the peak curve. Cheap, and it is the load-bearing check on your headline result.

**P4 — Edge-Stieltjes extrapolation.** Compute $S_N(J_{\rm top}+\delta)$ for $\delta$ above the level spacing across grids, extrapolate to $S_\infty(J_{\rm top})$, and compare with $|\tilde G_0(0;T,B)|$. If it converges you get a grid-independent computability boundary $B^\dagger(T)$ — a two-line criterion replacing the deferred $(T,B)$ map. If it diverges with $N$, that too is decisive.

**P5 — Continue in $B$, not in $h$.** With P2 in place, track the paramagnetic outer-branch $\Sigma_{PM}(0;B)$ downward from $B>B_c$. This is 1D continuation in a *physical* parameter with a monotone inner solve — far better conditioned than auxiliary-$h$ continuation — and it locates $B^\dagger$ empirically while supplying $\mathrm{crit}(0;B)$, the seed the Jensen path is currently missing.

**P6 — Recast the Jensen root condition as an area rule.** Differentiating (45) with $g\mu_BH_0=\mathcal J(0)\langle J_x\rangle$: $F_{\rm path}'(h)=1+\Sigma(0;h)-\mathcal J(0)\chi_0^{cc}(h)=\mathrm{crit}(h)$, so $F_{\rm path}(h)=\int_0^h\mathrm{crit}$ and $h^*$ is an equal-area condition on the inverse mass. Worth checking whether the code forms it this way rather than as a difference of two large integrals — if not, conditioning of $h^*$ improves for free. It also localises the singular demand to one number, $\int_0^{h_{sp}}\mathrm{crit}$, and $\mathrm{crit}$ is even in $h$, so $[0,\epsilon]$ admits a two-term expansion with $O(\epsilon^5)$ remainder. That is a controlled quadrature, not the withdrawn R3 interpolation — but it is contingent on $\mathrm{crit}(0)$, i.e. on P5.

**P7 — Elastic-sector discriminator (low expected yield, near-zero cost).** Split $G_{\rm stat}$ into the Dyson piece and $-m^2\xi h(0)$ and report the ratio per node. $h(0)=\beta(1-n_{01}^2)\approx18$ meV$^{-1}$ at 0.31 K/1 T but $\sim10^{-2}$ at 0.10 K/1.5 T, so I expect this to explain part of the 0.31 K behaviour and none of the current 0.10 K failures. Note also that $\xi$'s denominator in (42) is a **third** independent zero-crossing, distinct from the $q$-poles and from `gstat_local_denom`; a branch audit tracking only the first two can misattribute multiplicity.

One supporting note for the exclusions list: quasi-Newton/Anderson would not rescue the outer map either — the obstruction is that the map is *discontinuous* across poles, not that it is stiff. P2 removes the discontinuity by construction instead.
