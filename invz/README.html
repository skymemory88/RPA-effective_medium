The invz module 1/z effective-medium expansion for the dipolar Ising ferromagnet LiHoF4 — user manual & mathematical reference

This module computes the magnetic susceptibility of LiHoF4 one order beyond mean-field/RPA, by resumming the leading 1/z single-ion self-energy self-consistently with a scalar effective-medium lattice sum. It covers both phases — the paramagnetic transverse-field boundary Bc(T) and the real-frequency response χ″cc(q,ω), including the soft mode that dips at the quantum phase transition and hardens into the ferromagnet below Bc — following Rønnow et al., PRB 75, 054426 (2007) ("R 2007"), which applies Jensen's high-density expansion (J. Jensen, PRB 49, 11833 (1994)) to this material.

ScopeParamagnetic and ferromagnetic phases; scalar (cc-channel) self-energy — §1.7 explains how the full 3×3 dipolar tensor reduces to this single channel; transverse field B∥a. The ordered phase uses the bare mean-field order parameter with the elastic-sector self-energy (HTML eqs 37–38); the applied-field magnetization curve (eqs 41–43) and free-energy consistency (eq 44) are deferred. The full symbolic derivation lives in jensen_1z_framework.html at the repository root (equations there are cited in the code as "HTML eq N"). This page is the working manual: what to run, what it returns, and the equations each function evaluates. See Scope & limitations for what is deliberately left out.

1 · Physical picture & mathematical foundations

LiHoF4 is an Ising dipolar ferromagnet. Each Ho3+ ion has a crystal-field ground doublet (an effective two-level system along the c axis) that is split by a transverse field into a gap \(\Delta\), and a strongly hybridized 165Ho nuclear spin (\(I=7/2\)). At the temperatures of interest the electronic and nuclear states are treated together as one larger single-ion Hilbert space (17 electronic × 8 nuclear = 136 states). The magnetic transition is driven by the cc (longitudinal) component of the dipolar + exchange coupling \(\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\).

The theory has four moving parts, evaluated in the order below.

1.1 Single-ion two-level response

The dominant electronic doublet contributes a two-level response \(g\), with occupation difference \(n_{01}\) and static value \(g_0\):

$$g(z) = \frac{2\,n_{01}\,\Delta}{\Delta^{2}-z^{2}}, \qquad n_{01}=\tanh\frac{\Delta}{2k_BT}, \qquad g_0\equiv g(0)=\frac{2n_{01}}{\Delta}.$$

The bare local (cc) Green function is \(G_0=-\chi_0^{cc}\). In the pure two-level limit \(G_0=-M^{2}g\), where \(M^2=|\langle0|J_z|1\rangle|^{2}\) is the transition matrix element; in the code, \(G_0\) is the full electronuclear \(-\chi_0^{cc}\) (so hyperfine enhancement enters non-perturbatively), while the doublet parameters \((\Delta,M^{2},n_{01},g_0)\) set the self-energy.

1.2 Effective medium — the lattice sum

The self-consistent effective-medium coupling \(K(\wn)\) is the fixed point of (R 2007 eqs 7–9; \(\langle\cdot\rangle=\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q,\nu}\) over the Brillouin zone and the four cc sublattice branches):

$$G=\frac{G_0}{1+\Sigma+K\,G_0}, \qquad G_{\mathbf q}=\frac{G}{1+(\Jc_\nu-K)\,G}, \qquad K=\Big\langle\frac{\Jc_\nu\,G_{\mathbf q}}{G}\Big\rangle.$$

At fixed \(\Sigma\) this is not iterated: substituting \(G\) into \(G_{\mathbf q}\) cancels \(K\) (\(G_{\mathbf q}=G_0/(1+\Sigma+\Jc_\nu G_0)\)), leaving a linear equation for \(K\) that invz_emt_scalar solves in closed form,

$$K=\frac{A\,(1+\Sigma)}{1-A\,G_0}, \qquad A=\Big\langle\frac{\Jc_\nu}{1+\Sigma+\Jc_\nu\,G_0}\Big\rangle, \qquad G=\frac{G_0}{1+\Sigma+K\,G_0}.$$

So each medium solve is one direct evaluation per \(\Sigma\) (no inner fixed-point loop; frequencies may be blocked via opts.freq_block to bound peak memory), and at the true fixed point \(\langle G_{\mathbf q}\rangle=G\) exactly (the med.closure diagnostic is then machine-zero). A vanishing denominator \(1-A\,G_0\to0\) is the ordered-side instability where the paramagnetic medium ceases to exist; \(K\)/\(G\) go non-finite and med.converged=false reports it. Only the outer \(K\!\leftrightarrow\!\Sigma\) coupling (below) is iterated.

1.3 The 1/z self-energy

The Matsubara moments of \(K\) weighted by powers of \(g\),

$$\lambda_p=\frac1\beta\sum_n K(\wn)\,g(\wn)^{p},$$

fix the self-energy \(\Sigma=\alpha+\gamma\,g\) (Jensen; HTML eqs 21–27 / J 2.17–2.19):

$$\alpha=\frac{M^{2}}{n_{01}^{2}}\Big[\lambda_2-\half\big(g_0+\beta(1-n_{01}^{2})\big)\lambda_1\Big], \qquad \gamma=\frac{M^{2}}{n_{01}^{2}}\Big[\lambda_1-(1-n_{01}^{2})\,K\Big].$$

Equations 1.2 and 1.3 are coupled (\(K\) depends on \(\Sigma\), \(\Sigma\) depends on \(K\)); invz_solve_point iterates them to a fixed point at each \((T,B_x)\).

1.4 Renormalized susceptibility & criticality

On the real axis the self-energy divides the whole cc component, and the observable is a mode RPA over the coupling branches (HTML eqs 22–23, 29–30):

$$\Sigma(\omega)=\alpha+\frac{M^{2}}{n_{01}^{2}}\big[\lambda_1-(1-n_{01}^{2})K(\omega)\big]\,g(\omega+i\eta),\qquad \tilde\chi_0=\frac{\chi_0^{cc}}{1+\Sigma},\qquad \chi(\mathbf q,\omega)=\frac{\tilde\chi_0}{1-\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\,\tilde\chi_0}.$$

The paramagnetic instability (phase boundary) is where the uniform mode diverges,

$$\Jc(0)\,\chi_0^{cc}(0)=1+\Sigma(0)\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \texttt{crit}\equiv 1+\Sigma(0)-\Jc(0)\,\chi_0^{cc}(0)=0,$$

and exactly at zero field the critical self-energy has the closed form (R 2007 eq 10)

$$\Sigma_c(0)=\frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q}\frac{\Jc_{\mathbf q}}{\Jc(0)-\Jc_{\mathbf q}}.$$

1.5  Ferromagnetic phase (m ≠ 0)

Below \(B_c\) a spontaneous longitudinal moment \(m_0=\langle J_z\rangle\) develops: invz_single_ion (opts.order) solves the extra ordering mean field \(h_z=\Jc_{cc}(0)\langle J_z\rangle\) for its symmetry-broken branch. Spectral weight rotates from the transition matrix element into the diagonal moment, \(M^2+m^2=M_0^2\) fixed, and the self-energy gains an elastic correction (HTML eqs 37–38):

$$\begin{aligned} \Sigma(\wn)&=(\alpha-\alpha_m)+\Big[\gamma(\wn)-\tfrac{2m^2}{M^2}\gamma(0)\Big]g(\wn), \\[.3em] \alpha_m&=\frac{m^2}{n_{01}^2}\Big[\lambda_2-g_0\lambda_1+\tfrac{4}{g_0}\lambda_3-(1-n_{01}^2)\big(1+\tfrac12\beta\Delta\,n_{01}\big)K(0)g_0\Big]. \end{aligned}$$

This reduces to the paramagnetic \(\Sigma=\alpha+\gamma g\) exactly at \(m=0\). The elastic weight \(h(0)=\beta(1-n_{01}^2)\) is exponentially small at low \(T\) (\(n_{01}\to1\)), so the \(\xi\,h\) static correction (eq 39) collapses to the ordinary Dyson form and \(\alpha_m\approx m^2[\lambda_2-g_0\lambda_1+(4/g_0)\lambda_3]\). The mode softens toward \(B_c\) from below (the ordered \(\texttt{crit}\to0\)) and hardens deeper into the ordered phase, tracing the soft-mode "V" of the quantum phase transition.

Option A boundary caveat The moment is the bare mean-field order parameter (the eq-43 \(H_{\rm MF}\!\leftrightarrow\!H\) self-consistency is deferred), so it onsets at the mean-field boundary, slightly above the 1/z boundary. Near \(B_c\) the FM and PM soft-mode branches therefore meet with a small step (~15 μeV at 0.31 K), not perfect continuity; deep in each phase the result is unaffected.

1.6  Uniform-mode coupling & sample shape

Ordering is driven by the uniform (\(\mathbf q\!=\!0\)) cc coupling \(\Jc(0)\), which carries the intrinsic dipole lattice sum, the exchange \(4\Jc_{12}\), and the Lorentz cavity term — always present, not a shape-dependent toggle:

$$\texttt{info.Jcc0}=\Jc_{\rm dip}^{cc}(0)+4\Jc_{12}+\underbrace{\frac{4\pi}{3V_c}\,g_{\rm fac}}_{\text{Lorentz cavity}}.$$

The sample shape — ion.demag and the spheroid aspect ratio ion.alpha (ellipsoid_demagn) — does not enter this expression at all: per R 2007, the demagnetizing field cancels from the critical condition (ordering occurs at \(\mathbf q\to0^+\), not strict \(\mathbf q=0\)), so info.Jcc0, the branch array Jnu, and the ordering-channel contribution to criticality are demag-invariant; \(T_c(B=0)\) is exactly demag-invariant for the same reason (the transverse moment vanishes at \(B=0\)). invz_jq_modes instead exports the shape dependence separately, in exactly the two places it can physically act:

  • info.Jshape_cc — the strict-\(\mathbf q=0\) observable correction (the shape-dependent share that an earlier version of this code folded directly into \(\Jc(0)\), now split out so the critical coupling stays untouched). Applied only in invz_chi_realaxis, as \(\chi_{\rm meas}=\chi/(1+\texttt{Jshape\_cc}\,\chi)\) — algebraically identical to a shifted-pole form \(\tilde\chi_t/\big(1-(\texttt{Jcc0}-\texttt{Jshape\_cc})\tilde\chi_t\big)\), just written so the demag-invariant pole \(\texttt{Jcc0}\) is never itself shape-dependent. The strict-\(\mathbf q=0\) soft mode saturates at \(1/\texttt{Jshape\_cc}\) instead of diverging; a finite-\(q\) probe (§4, invz_spectra_qpath) never applies this term (the \(q\to0^+\) limit is the intrinsic one).
  • info.Jaa0 — the demag-aware transverse (\(aa\)) coupling, Jaa0_dipole(ion.demag,ion.alpha) \(+\,4\Jc_{12}\) (same lattice-sum machinery as Jcc0_dipole, run on the \(aa\) tensor block; see invz_jq_modes for the exact per-component formula). This is now wired all the way into the transverse mean field — passed as opts.Jxx0 to invz_single_ion, invz_twolevel/invz_twolevel_ordered and invz_chi_realaxis — the one channel through which \(B_c(T)\) vs. applied field can still shift with sample shape (the internal-vs-applied transverse-field relation), even though the ordering criticality itself is shape-blind.

Because invz_jq_modes is the single source for info.Jcc0, info.Jshape_cc and info.Jaa0, and all three are threaded consistently through the mean field, the RPA denominator and the 1/z criticality, setting the shape (or any coupling parameter) propagates everywhere it is physically allowed to act — and nowhere else.

Demag semantics (canonical)
  1. info.Jcc0, Jnu, and the ordering-channel contribution to criticality are demag-invariant.
  2. \(T_c(B=0)\) is exactly demag-invariant (the transverse moment vanishes there).
  3. \(B_c(T)\) vs. applied transverse field can shift through the demag-aware info.Jaa0 (internal-vs-applied field relation).
  4. q-path calculations omit the strict-uniform Jshape_cc transform (finite-q probe = intrinsic) but can still change through info.Jaa0.
Per Rønnow et al. 2007 (who note this contradicts a suggestion of Chakraborty et al.). Never write "\(B_c\) and \(T_c\) are demag-invariant" unqualified — only the zero-field, ordering-channel piece is.
Cache bump The invz_jq_modes on-disk cache schema changed when Jshape_cc/Jaa0 were added (key prefix jq_ → jq2_, plus a trailing schema-version tag in the cache key). Old invz/cache/jq_*.mat files are orphaned (never matched, never reused) and safe to delete.

1.7  The 3×3 dipolar tensor vs. the scalar 1/z theory

The dipole–dipole coupling is a full Cartesian tensor \(\Jc_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q)\), \(\alpha,\beta\in\{a,b,c\}\) — in the code a [3,3,4,4] array over sublattice pairs from MF_dipole — so all three spin components participate in the interaction, whereas Jensen's HoF3 theory is written for a scalar coupling in a single channel (\(J_xJ_x\) in his notation). This is not a discrepancy between the two materials that had to be worked around: HoF3 is also dipole-coupled (it is in Jensen's title), and in both cases the scalar \(\Jc(\mathbf q)\) of the 1/z machinery is the projection of the same 3×3 tensor onto the one component that has matrix elements inside the low-energy two-level subspace. The channel is selected by the single-ion level scheme, not by the interaction. In LiHoF4 the Γ3,4 ground doublet carries only the \(c\)-axis component of \(\mathbf J\), so the fluctuation operator in the two-site Green function is \(\tilde J_z\) alone — R 2007 eq. 5 expands \(G(ij,\tau)=-\langle T_\tau\,\tilde J_{iz}(\tau_1)\,\tilde J_{jz}(\tau_2)\rangle\) — and the only tensor block that can attach an interaction line to those fluctuations at both ends is \(\Jc_{cc}(\mathbf q)\). That is the dip(3,3,:,:) extraction in invz_jq_modes, whose 4×4 sublattice block is then diagonalized into the four scalar branches \(\Jc_\nu(\mathbf q)\), matching R 2007 eqs. 7–9, where \(\Jc(\mathbf q)\) is defined as the eigenvalues of the 4×4 (cc) coupling matrix.

The remaining tensor components are not discarded — each enters through the channel where it can act, and the neglected remainders are bounded by R 2007's own estimates:

  • \(\Jc_{aa}\) → static transverse mean field. The \(aa\) block generates \(h_x=\Jc_{aa}(0)\langle J_x\rangle\), solved self-consistently in invz_single_ion (default ion.Jxx0 \(=\Jc^{aa}_{D\cdot D}(0)+4\Jc_{12}=3.512\) μeV at demag=0; demag-aware calculations pass the shape-corrected info.Jaa0 instead, as opts.Jxx0 — §1.6), which renormalizes the doublet parameters \(\Delta\) and \(M^2\) at every \((T,B_x)\). Only its 1/z fluctuation corrections are dropped: the internal transverse field is ∼5% of the applied field at the largest fields, so renormalizing the transverse moment "should be of no importance" (R 2007 Sec. II B).
  • Off-diagonal blocks & higher crystal-field levels → bare susceptibility only. \(\Jc_{ac},\Jc_{bc},\dots\) multiply operators with no matrix elements inside the doublet (statically they act like internally generated transverse fields, R 2007 Sec. I); dynamically they reach the cc channel only via doublet↔higher-level transitions (first excited CF level at 11 K). Those transitions are included in the module's \(G_0=-\chi_0^{cc}\), which is the full 136-state electronuclear susceptibility (invz_chi0z; the c0(3,3,:) component in invz_solve_point), but they are excluded from \(\Sigma\), which is built from the two-level sector. R 2007 bounds their share of \(\chi_0^{cc}(0)\) at <1% at zero frequency at maximum field — "the singlet–singlet determination of the renormalization effects is acceptable".
  • Renormalize the dominant channel only. Dividing just \(\chi_0^{cc}\) by \(1+\Sigma\) is Jensen's own prescription for realistic level schemes (J 1994 Sec. III; jensen_1z_framework.html §10): the 1/z renormalization applies to the susceptibility components deriving from the dominant two-level transition; the weak transitions stay at their bare/RPA values.

What genuinely stays outside the scalar theory is full-tensor mode mixing — an RPA in which \(\Jc_{ac}(\mathbf q)\) hybridizes the cc soft mode with the gapped transverse excitons at general \(\mathbf q\). That is the "tensor observables" item of §8 (hook: MF_RPA_Yikai.m); for the ordering instability, the soft mode, and \(\chi_{cc}\) it enters at the same <1% weak-transition level quoted above.

Conventions \(\chi=-G\); ferromagnetic \(\Jc>0\); energies in meV; bosonic Matsubara \(\omega_n=2\pi n/\beta\), \(n\ge0\), with weights \([1,2,2,\dots]\). \(\Sigma(0)\) is not monotonic on cooling: it rises from its high-\(T\) decay, peaks near \(k_BT\sim\Delta\), and saturates to a finite zero-point value.

2 · Data flow & call structure

2.1  Driver call structure — what you run, and what it calls

The two scripts you run are the drivers; everything below them is called to carry out the task. Both fan into one shared solver core, and the only phase-dependent fork is the paramagnet / ferromagnet solve pair — every other function is shared between the phases.

2.2  Data flow through the layers

Each layer consumes only the outputs of the layers above it. The self-consistent point solve (layer 6) is the loop that ties the effective medium (4) to the self-energy (5).

3 · Quick start

Prerequisites MATLAB (developed on R2025a). Repo-root MF_dipole.m, exchange.m, qVec_generator.m (tracked) are the only external dependencies. The Parallel Computing Toolbox is optional — the drivers use it if present and run serially otherwise.

From the repository root (the path contains spaces — quote it):

% one-off: reproduce the field-vs-frequency spectrum at T = 0.31 K
run invz/invz_run_spectra.m

% the full phase boundary, two regimes  (parallel over all boundary points)
run invz/invz_run_phase_diagram.m

Same driver, ferromagnetic-mode q-path view — set qpath non-empty near the top of invz_run_spectra.m (cf. R 2007 Fig. 3):

qh = linspace(1, 2, 51).';  qpath = [qh zeros(numel(qh), 2)];   % (1,0,0) -> (2,0,0)
Bq = [3.6 4.24 6.0];                 % T; several fields -> E_peak(q) dispersion overlay
wq = (0:0.004:0.85).';               % meV; 0.85 avoids clipping the R2007 Fig. 3 branch
dispScale = 1.15;                    % R 2007 scale their calculated energies by 1.15 (Fig. 3)
Physical mode, not neutron intensity By default invz_spectra_qpath follows the uniform ferromagnetic-mode coupling \(J(\mathbf q)=v^{\mathsf T}\Jc_{cc}(\mathbf q)v\), \(v=[1\,1\,1\,1]/2\) (invz_jq_path P.Juni) — the same effective coupling MF_RPA_Yikai.m uses, and the physical single mode under the "all four Ho sublattices equivalent" mean-field/RPA approximation both codes make. Along \((1,0,0)\to(2,0,0)\) it reproduces R 2007 Fig. 3's monotonic softening end to end (this module: 1/z 0.54→0.23 meV, RPA 0.53→0.21 meV; ×1.15 scaling → ∼0.61→0.24 meV vs. the paper's ∼0.6→0.2 meV). Do not pass opts.branch = 4 ("max eigenvalue") expecting the same curve: away from Γ the uniform mode stops being an eigenvector of the 4×4 cc block, so the four sorted branches cross, and the largest-eigenvalue branch selects a different physical sublattice combination on each side of the crossing — for this path it mirrors the dispersion about \(h=1.5\). opts.branch = 1..4 remains available as an exploratory sorted-eigenvalue view (branch index, not a mode identity tracked through crossings) but is not the R 2007 Fig. 3 comparison. It is still not a neutron cross-section either way — there is no eigenvector / structure-factor / polarization / magnetic-form-factor weighting — and it inherits three open issues from the rest of the module (§8): closed-grid BZ quadrature bias, possible negative spectral weight from the real-axis continuation, and the bare mean-field FM/PM handoff (invz_solve_auto). The direction-aware Γ-limit guard (invz_jq_path) covers Γ-equivalent points only — near non-Γ-equivalent integer points (e.g. \((1,0,0)\), zero structure factor) the coupling keeps truncated-sum quality.

Programmatic use of the core solve:

ion = invz_ion();
[qv,~,~] = qVec_generator(ion.a,'mode','grid','grid',[16 16 16],'range',[-0.5 0.5]);
qv = qv(any(abs(qv)>1e-12,2),:);
[Jnu,info] = invz_jq_modes(ion, qv, struct('dpRng',30,'cache',true));
pt  = invz_solve_point(ion, 0.31, 4.5, Jnu(:), struct('J0eff',info.Jcc0));   % one (T,Bx)
out = invz_chi_realaxis(ion, 0.31, 4.5, pt, (0.01:0.005:0.7)', struct('Jsel',info.Jcc0));
plot(out.chi_cc_q(1,:));   % chi(q=0, omega)

4 · Driver scripts

invz_run_spectra.m — susceptibility spectra

Computes \(\chi''_{cc}(\omega)\) at the uniform mode versus transverse field at fixed \(T\), and chooses its view automatically from the number of fields:

  • Few fields (numel(fields) ≤ sliceMax, default 8) → 1-D line-slice overlay, 1/z solid vs bare-RPA dashed, one colour per field (cf. R 2007 Fig. 2).
  • Many fields → 2-D field-vs-frequency colormap: \(B_x\) on x, \(\omega\) on y, \(\log_{10}\chi''\) in colour, with 1/z and RPA panels side by side (cf. Kovačević 2016 Fig. 3d).

Edit the block at the top of the file: T, fields (e.g. [3.6 4.2 4.8 5.4 6.0] for slices, linspace(0,6,61) for a map), w, eta, sliceMax, and useParallel; set ion.demag/ion.alpha just after invz_ion() for a sample-shape correction (§1.6). eta is the real-axis Lorentzian half-width — a display broadening, not physical damping (the model's intrinsic linewidth is negligible at these temperatures), so keep it \(\gtrsim\) the w spacing or the peaks alias. The heavy result S replots for free via invz_plot_spectra_map(gca, S, S.chiz, '1/z').

Both phases, automatically At each field invz_spectra_map first tries the ferromagnetic (ordered) solve; if the point has a spontaneous moment it uses that, otherwise the paramagnet. So a sweep across \(B_c\) produces one continuous soft-mode map (the "V" dipping at the transition), FM below and PM above, labelled per field in S.phase (1 = FM, 2 = PM, 0 = no solution). Only genuinely intractable columns (the degenerate doublet at \(B_x\!\to\!0\) where the two-level treatment breaks down in both phases) come back NaN and masked.

Setting qpath (an [nq×3] r.l.u. array) switches the same driver to the ferromagnetic-mode q-path view (invz_spectra_qpath / invz_plot_spectra_qpath) at fixed field(s) Bq: a single field renders 1/z + RPA path-vs-frequency colormaps with a censored peak overlay; several fields (R 2007 Fig. 3 uses Bq = [3.6 4.24 6.0] T) render an \(E_{\rm peak}(q)\) dispersion overlay instead, one colour per field. The dispersion defaults to the uniform FM-mode coupling (opts.branch = 0), the physical mode that reproduces Fig. 3's monotonic \((1,0,0)\to(2,0,0)\) softening; wq sets the frequency grid — widen past its default 0.85 meV if peaks clip at the top edge, since the peak picker censors (never interpolates over) a boundary maximum; dispScale rescales the plotted dispersion only (R 2007 scale their calculated energies by 1.15 to match experiment, Fig. 3). See Quick start for a worked snippet and the physical-vs-exploratory-branch caveat.

invz_run_phase_diagram.m — phase boundary \(B_c(T)\)

Traces the R 2007 Fig. 1 paramagnetic-side boundary in two regimes: the critical field \(B_c(T)\) bisected at each temperature in Ts (invz_critical, vertical cuts), and the critical temperature \(T_c(B)\) bisected at each field in Bs (invz_critical_T, horizontal cuts). The horizontal cuts own the high-\(T\) end, where the boundary runs nearly parallel to the field axis and a fixed-\(T\) cut crosses it at a glancing angle (brackets fail, tiny \(T\) errors give huge \(B_c\) errors). The \(T_c(B)\) bisection window is fixed in the script at [Tlo, Tmax] = [1.0, 2.0] K. Keep Bs entries between 0.5 T and \(B_c(T_{lo}) \approx 2.8\) T: below 0.5 T the paramagnetic solve develops non-convergence patches near the boundary that bias \(T_c\) upward (the 0–0.5 T segment spans only ~4 mK and is represented by the closed-form zero-field \(T_c\) endpoint); above \(B_c(T_{lo})\) the bracket assert fails cleanly to NaN. Full single-core run ≈ 2–3 h; see the next section for the parallel speedup.

5 · Parallelism

Two independent axes can be distributed over a parallel pool; the bisection within a single invz_critical cannot (each bracket halving needs the sign of the previous EMT solve).

DriverParallel axisHow to enable
invz_run_phase_diagramboundary points (one flat parfor over the Ts and Bs jobs)useParallel = true (default)
invz_spectra_map / invz_run_spectrafield (parfor over fields)opts.parallel = true / useParallel = true

Both use parfor (k = 1:n, nWorkers) with nWorkers = 0 forcing a serial run — so the same code runs with or without the Parallel Computing Toolbox, and falls back to serial automatically when it is absent. A local pool is auto-created on first use (~10–30 s once). The \(\Jc(q)\) lattice sum is computed once up front, so workers do no disk I/O and never contend on invz/cache/. Progress lines interleave across workers — expected. With an 8-worker pool the phase boundary drops from ~2–3 h to roughly 20–30 min.

Boundary speckle Right at the marginal phase boundary a field/temperature point can fail to converge and come back masked, next to converged neighbours. That is the para fixed point going marginal (not a bug). Widen max_outer / soften mixing there, or accept a couple of masked edge points.

8 · Scope & limitations

In scope: both phases — single-ion response, the scalar (cc-channel) 1/z self-energy and effective-medium lattice sum, the paramagnetic-side boundary, the real-axis cc-susceptibility \(\chi(\mathbf q,\nu,\omega)\), and the ferromagnetic phase via the spontaneous-moment mean field + elastic-sector self-energy (\(\alpha_m\), \(\lambda_3\); HTML eqs 37–38), giving the soft mode on both sides of the transition; the uniform-mode coupling always carries the Lorentz cavity term (demag-invariant), plus an optional sample-shape correction split into the two channels where it can physically act — the strict-uniform observable term info.Jshape_cc and the demag-aware transverse coupling info.Jaa0 (§1.6) — consistently shared by the mean field, RPA and 1/z; and a q-path susceptibility (invz_spectra_qpath) whose default dispersion is the physical uniform ferromagnetic-mode coupling \(v^{\mathsf T}\Jc_{cc}(q)v\), reproducing R 2007 Fig. 3's monotonic softening end to end (not a neutron-intensity calculation; see the Quick-start caveat) — an exploratory sorted-eigenvalue branch (opts.branch=1..4) remains available but is not the Fig. 3 comparison.

Out of scope / future work:

  • Ordered-phase field & thermodynamics (deferred part of the ferromagnetic extension) — the applied-field magnetization curve / \(H_\mathrm{MF}\!\leftrightarrow\!H\) self-consistency (HTML eqs 41–43), which would make the moment onset exactly at the 1/z boundary (removing the small FM/PM step near \(B_c\)), and the free-energy consistency test (eq 44). The elastic \(\xi\,h\) static correction (eq 39) is included only in its low-\(T\) (ordinary-Dyson) limit.
  • Tensor observables — the full 3×3 / 4-sublattice RPA (neutron cross-sections, transverse components) lives in MF_RPA_Yikai.m; invz_jq_modes's per-sublattice \(\Jc_{cc}(q)\) matrix is the hook point. See §1.7 for why the scalar cc reduction is quantitatively safe for the ordering channel (the neglected tensor mode-mixing enters below the 1% level).
  • Thermodynamics — free energy, \(\delta U\), heat capacity (HTML eqs 34–35, 44). Note \(\delta U(T\!\to\!0)\neq0\) (zero-point shift).
  • Ewald acceleration of the dipole sums — brute-force + on-disk caching is enough at the 12–24³ grids here.
Small-Bx caveat Near zero field the doublet splitting \(\Delta\) can drop below the 165Ho hyperfine scale (\(A\approx3.36\) μeV). There the two-level determination of \(\Sigma\) over-suppresses \(T_c\) (mirroring R 2007's own comparison with quantum Monte Carlo). Results near the degenerate-doublet limit are directional only — which is why invz_twolevel errors (invz:degenerateDoublet) rather than returning a value for \(\Delta<10^{-4}\) meV.
