ODD Extension for the invz 1/z Module — Implementation Plan

Off-diagonal dipolar (ODD) terms in the LiHoF4 1/z effective-medium calculation, via (Tier 1) an ODD-mediated fluctuation-channel correction to the cc coupling and (Tier 2) internal-transverse-field renormalization of the single-ion doublet. Physics target: the mechanism of Dollberg, Andresen & Schechter, PRB 105, L180413 (2022), realized inside the controlled 1/z expansion of Jensen (PRB 49, 11833) / Rønnow et al. (PRB 75, 054426, “R 2007”). Companion documents: invz/README.html, jensen_1z_framework.html. Prepared July 2026.

1 · Physics summary & conventions

The full dipolar + exchange Hamiltonian is $$\mathcal H \;=\; -\tfrac12\sum_{ij}\sum_{\mu\nu\in\{a,b,c\}} \mathcal J^{\mu\nu}(ij)\, J_{i\mu} J_{j\nu},$$ of which the current module keeps only the longitudinal block \( \mathcal J^{cc} \) (the four sublattice branches Jnu) plus a diagonal transverse mean field (ion.Jxx0). The ODD blocks \( \mathcal J^{c\alpha},\ \alpha\in\{a,b\} \) are discarded at the invz_jq_modes projection. By the C2-about-c element of the S4 Ho-site symmetry, every element of the 4×4 sublattice matrix \( \mathcal J^{c\alpha}(\mathbf q{=}\mathbf 0) \) vanishes, so ODD contributes nothing at mean-field level in either phase of the pure system: its entire effect on \(T_c\) lives in the fluctuation channel — exactly what the 1/z machinery is built to resum.

1.1 Tier 1 — mediated coupling (the Dollberg three-spin term, resummed)

The transverse response of Ho3+ is Van Vleck: the first excited CF level sits at \( \varepsilon_1 \approx 10.8\,\mathrm K = 0.931\,\mathrm{meV} \) (README §7 benchmark), far above \(k_BT\) at the transition. Integrating out the transverse channel at Gaussian (adiabatic) level converts the ODD blocks into an additive correction to the cc coupling, a 4×4 Hermitian sublattice matrix at each \( \mathbf q \): $$\boxed{\;\delta\mathcal J^{cc}(\mathbf q) \;=\; \sum_{\alpha\beta\in\{a,b\}} \mathcal J^{c\alpha}(\mathbf q)\;\big[\chi_\perp(0)\big]_{\alpha\beta}\;\mathcal J^{\beta c}(\mathbf q), \qquad \mathcal J^{\beta c}(\mathbf q) = \big[\mathcal J^{c\beta}(\mathbf q)\big]^\dagger,\;} \tag{E1}$$ with \( \chi_\perp(0) \) the static 2×2 transverse single-ion susceptibility (hyperfine-dressed, from invz_chi0z at \(z=0\); units meV−1, so \( \delta\mathcal J \) is in meV, consistent). The physical content is Dollberg’s three-spin term — an anticorrelated \(J_c\) pair polarizing the transverse moment of an intermediate ion — resummed into a pairwise coupling.

Two structural facts drive the entire acceptance logic:

  • \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q) = A\,X\,A^\dagger \) with \(X=\chi_\perp(0)\succ 0\), hence \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q) \succeq 0 \) at every \( \mathbf q \) (mediated interactions lower energy);
  • \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf 0) = 0 \) by symmetry before the self-site subtraction — but the subtraction (E4) then shifts the whole spectrum, including the uniform mode, by the constant $$ d \;=\; \frac1{N_q}\sum_{\mathbf q'}\big[\delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q')\big]_{ss} \;=\; \sum_{\alpha\beta\in\{a,b\}} \big[\chi_\perp(0)\big]_{\alpha\beta}\, \sum_{j(\neq i)} \mathcal J^{c\alpha}(ij)\,\mathcal J^{\beta c}(ji) \;>\;0 , \tag{E5}$$ an absolutely convergent \( \sim r^{-6} \) real-space sum (equal on all four sublattices by symmetry). The physical uniform pair coupling therefore decreases: \( \mathcal J(0) \to \mathcal J(0)-d \). This is not an artifact — it is exactly the mean-field ODD reduction of Dollberg–Schechter (DS2023, arXiv:2308.10095) Eq. (7), whose last term evaluates to \( -B_{xz,xz} \) because the shape sums \( A_{xz}=A_{yz}=0 \): the mediated interaction has an overall antiferromagnetic preference for the uniform mode. Their MF numbers set its scale: a 5% \(T_c\) reduction already in the 3-state model (2.27 → 2.18 K), larger with the full CF spectrum.
  • The q-dependent remainder \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q)+d \succeq 0 \) is the pure fluctuation channel: extra coupling weight at \( \mathbf q\neq\mathbf 0 \) raises $$\Sigma_c(0) \;=\; \frac1N\sum_{\mathbf q,\nu}\frac{\mathcal J_\nu(\mathbf q)}{\mathcal J(0)-\mathcal J_\nu(\mathbf q)} \tag{E2}$$ (README §1.4 / R 2007 eq. 10, evaluated with \( \mathcal J_\nu \to \) modes of \( \mathcal J^{cc}+\delta\mathcal J \) and the shifted \( \mathcal J(0) \)), and the critical condition \( [\mathcal J(0)-d]\,\chi_0^{cc}(0)=1+\Sigma(0) \) then requires a larger \( \chi_0 \), i.e. lower \(T_c\). This is the part DS2023’s MF cannot see (their mirror-symmetry cancellations, Fig. S1) and their spin-½ MC does — the MF↔MC gap in their Fig. 2 is the target observable for the Σ-driven piece here.

Cross-validation of (E1). In the 3-state, \(T\to0\), \(B_x=0\) limit, (E1) with \( \chi_\perp \to (2\rho^2/\Delta)\,\mathbb 1 \) reduces exactly to the first (classical) term of DS2023’s three-body Hamiltonian \(H_{3B}\), with the end-site \( \alpha^2 = M^2 \) factors carried by \( \chi_0^{cc} \) in the 1/z bookkeeping. (E1) is thus the all-CF-state, finite-temperature, hyperfine-dressed, and (Phase T2) retardation-capable generalization of their result. Their two-body emergent terms \( \varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{ij} \) — the transverse–transverse mediated channel \( \propto \mathcal J^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal J^{\gamma\delta}/\Delta \) — are quantified by DS2023 as two orders of magnitude below the exchange scale, which justifies restricting Tier 1 to the single-excitation \( c\!\leftrightarrow\!\alpha \) channel.

1.2 Tier 2 — internal-field renormalization of the doublet

The fluctuating internal transverse field also renormalizes the single-ion problem — Dollberg’s second effect (variable moments). Its covariance (energy units, conjugate to \(J_\alpha\)) is $$ C_{\alpha\beta} \;=\; \big\langle h_\alpha h_\beta \big\rangle \;=\; \frac1{4N}\sum_{\mathbf q}\operatorname{tr}_{\rm subl}\!\Big[ \mathcal J^{\alpha c}(\mathbf q)\; S_{cc}(\mathbf q)\; \mathcal J^{c\beta}(\mathbf q) \Big], \qquad S_{cc}(\mathbf q) = \tfrac1\beta{\textstyle\sum_n}\,\chi_{cc}(\mathbf q, i\omega_n), \tag{E3}$$ i.e. the equal-time cc structure factor of the converged 1/z solution contracted with the ODD blocks (trace over sublattices, per-site normalization 1/4). The single-ion doublet parameters \( (\Delta, M^2, n_{01}) \) are then averaged over a Gaussian field of covariance \(C\) (Gauss–Hermite quadrature) before entering \( \Sigma \). The site-diagonal coupling removed from Tier 1 by the self-site subtraction (E4 below) is a constant in the strict two-level limit; its physical residue — the internal field renormalizing \( (\Delta, M^2, n_{01}) \) — is precisely what Tier 2 owns. No double counting.

Framing of the physics outcome (read before writing any acceptance test) The scientific question is whether Tier 1 + Tier 2 inside the 1/z expansion reproduces the ODD suppression that Dollberg et al. obtain by classical MC — their zero-field gap is \(T_c^{\rm MF}-T_c^{\rm exp} \approx 1.79-1.53 = 0.25\) K; the module’s no-ODD 1/z baseline is 1.74 K, so the missing suppression in this framework is ≈ 0.21 K. Whether the two tiers deliver ~0.05 K or ~0.2 K is the result, not a pass/fail criterion. The comparison is also inexact in both directions: their MC excludes inter-doublet quantum fluctuations (valid \(B_x \lesssim 1\) T) but captures non-Gaussian field statistics; the 1/z treatment is Gaussian in the transverse channel but quantum in the cc channel. Report, don’t tune.

Per q: \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q) = V_{ca} X_{aa} V_{ca}^\dagger + V_{ca}X_{ab}V_{cb}^\dagger + V_{cb}X_{ba}V_{ca}^\dagger + V_{cb}X_{bb}V_{cb}^\dagger \) (E1), then enforce \( \delta\mathcal J(ii)=0 \): $$ \delta\mathcal J_{ss}(\mathbf q)\;\leftarrow\;\delta\mathcal J_{ss}(\mathbf q) \;-\;\frac1{N_q}\sum_{\mathbf q'}\delta\mathcal J_{ss}(\mathbf q'), \qquad s = 1..4 \;\text{(diagonal only)}. \tag{E4}$$

Bookkeeping rule so the shift is applied exactly once everywhere: the finite-q grid matrices receive the post-subtraction \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q) \) (which already contains the −d diagonal), and Jcc0 receives the explicit −d; no other q=0 special-casing.

7 · Deferred — Tier 3: genuine multi-channel 1/z not tasked

The rigorous version replaces Tiers 1–2 (do not stack them with it — the mediated coupling and the field variance are both contained in a full tensor treatment; stacking double-counts). Scope notes for a future plan:

  • Matrix effective medium \( K_{\mu\nu}(i\omega_n) \) over the 12×12 (Cartesian ⊗ sublattice) coupling — Jensen’s 1984 EMT and the tensor-RPA generalization sketched in framework §10 are the templates.
  • The single-channel identities are lost: \([\mathcal H_1, J_c]\neq0\) once ODD vertices enter, so the sum rule (framework eq. 31), the energy formula (eq. 34), and the even-in-\(\omega_n\) structure of \(G_0\) all require rederivation.
  • A two-level ion cannot host the ODD vertex at all (\( \langle 0|J_{a}|1\rangle \approx 0 \) within the Ising doublet); the minimal single-ion sector is a ground quasi-doublet + remote CF level — i.e. the three-level anisotropy-Ising scheme of the Part II framework, which is the natural host. The mixed fourth-order semi-invariants generalize framework eq. (20) to standard-basis operators of the three-level ion.
  • CF-state convergence (DS2023 Fig. S3): a single excited level (3-state) underestimates the ODD effect by ~30–50% (their 17-state \( \Gamma \approx 1.5\,\alpha^2\rho^2/\Delta \)); ≈ 6 states are converged. For the Part II three-level scheme this bounds the truncation error of the minimal sector — while Tiers 1–2 sidestep it entirely by using the full electronuclear χ⊥.
  • Cross-validation target: Tier 3 at Gaussian truncation must reproduce Tier 1 + Tier 2 — a strong mutual check and, independently of the LiHoF4 numbers, a publishable methodological result.

8 · Pitfalls ledger

Double counting. Three distinct traps: (i) the on-site part of (E1) vs Tier 2 — handled by (E4), keep the comment trail; (ii) Tier 1+2 vs Tier 3 — never enable together with a future tensor EMT; (iii) the diagonal transverse MF (ion.Jxx0) already contains the aa-diagonal lattice coupling — the ODD blocks are strictly \(c\alpha\) off-diagonal, so no overlap, but verify no aa terms leak into invz_odd_blocks.
Units / prefactors. \( \delta\mathcal J \propto (\mathcal J^{c\alpha})^2\chi_\perp \): any slip in the \((g_L\mu_B)^2\) convention enters squared relative to the cc benchmark. T1.5’s hand-estimate cross-check is mandatory, not optional. Same for μeV↔meV: the 6.82 μeV benchmark is the anchor.
q=0 handling. The grid excludes q=0 (drivers filter it); the ODD blocks are conditionally convergent as q→0 like every dipole sum but the C2 symmetry kills the limit — never special-case q=0 by extrapolation: the only q=0 modification is the explicit, absolutely convergent constant −d applied to info.Jcc0 (E5) — never a grid-extrapolated \( \delta\mathcal J(\mathbf q\to0) \).
Comparing against DS2023 numbers. Their curves contain two ad hoc ingredients the 1/z calculation must not import: (i) the longitudinal interaction is rescaled by 0.805 to absorb c-axis fluctuations into MF — in the 1/z framework that job belongs to Σ, so adding the factor would double-count fluctuations; (ii) hyperfine coupling enters as a temperature-dependent renormalization of the transverse field, whereas invz_chi0z carries it non-perturbatively in the 136-state single ion. Their unrescaled MF anchors (2.27/2.18 K, 3-state) and lattice sums (Suppl. Table I) are convention-free and safe to assert against; their phase-boundary curves and MC \(T_c\) are qualitative anchors only.
Boundary speckle. ODD raises Σ near criticality and will widen the known marginal non-convergence patches (README §5); apply the same mitigation (soften mixing, widen max_outer) before suspecting the new code.
χ⊥ near level crossings. At high Bx (≫ Bc) CF levels approach; if invz_chiperp is ever evaluated there (spectra maps to 6 T), watch for near-degenerate denominators in χ0 — the existing invz_chi0z handling applies, just don’t bypass it.

Appendix A · Explicit multilevel tensor 1/z route optional

Optional scope — not part of P0 → V4. This appendix records a research-grade alternative to Tiers 1–2. It has no required task IDs and is deliberately absent from the JSON manifest. Start it only after an explicit user decision. Replacement rule — staged, not blanket. The full program A2+A3 contains both the ODD-mediated interaction and the local transverse-field feedback, so it must replace, never be stacked with, the Tier-1 \(\delta\mathcal J^{cc}\) and Tier-2 field-variance corrections. The A0+A1 bridge, however, contains Tier 1 only (with the T2 retardation included for free): Tier 2's field-variance dressing of the doublet is a local mixed-cumulant effect (\(\langle T\,J_cJ_cJ_aJ_a\rangle_c\)-class) that first appears at A3. Tier 2 and A1 are disjoint diagram classes — running A0+A1 with Tier 2 enabled does not double-count at first order in \(1/z\) (the Gauss–Hermite dressing resums higher orders, so overlap first enters at \(O(1/z^2)\)), while running A0+A1 instead of Tiers 1–2 silently drops the variable-moments half of the Dollberg mechanism (the effect L180413 singles out as not capturable by mean-field-like analyses). Rule: A0+A1 replaces Tier 1/T2 only; keep Tier 2 additive until A2+A3 replaces everything. Where §7's deferred-scope summary states the blanket form of this rule (Gaussian truncation ↔ Tier 1+2), the staged statement here governs within the appendix route: Gaussian truncation reproduces Tier 1 / Phase T2, and Tier 2 is recovered only by the static-Gaussian limit of the A3 mixed cumulants.

A.1 · Feasibility and the actual missing layer

A three-level ion resolves the minimal Hilbert-space obstruction: the first excited crystal-field state gives \(J_a\) and \(J_b\) nonzero matrix elements and therefore permits explicit \(J_cJ_a\) and \(J_cJ_b\) ODD vertices. It is not quantitatively converged for LiHoF4, however. DS2023 finds that one excited state underestimates the dominant emergent interaction by roughly 30–50%, while about six electronic CF states reproduce the 17-state result. The repository already contains the ingredients needed to avoid that truncation at the two-point/RPA level; the missing object is the multicomponent local four-point cumulant required by the first \(1/z\) self-energy.

A.2 · Recommended staged route

  1. A0 — full-tensor RPA parity layer. Add a cached tensor builder returning \(\mathcal J(\mathbf q)\) as [12,12,nq] and evaluate $$\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm RPA}(\mathbf q,z) =\big[\boldsymbol\chi_0^{-1}(z)-\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}(\mathbf q)\big]^{-1}.$$ With ODD blocks disabled, its uniform mode must reproduce the existing Cartesian RPA after mirroring that code's two reductions — sublattice averaging and the diagonal renorm projection (set ion.renorm = [1 1 1] or apply identical factors; see the A.1 table) — and its \(cc\) sublattice branches must reproduce invz_jq_modes. In the three-state, \(B_x=0\) limit, eliminating the transverse block by a Schur complement must give (E1) with \(\chi_\perp(0)=2\rho^2/\Delta\) and hence DS2023’s leading \(H_{3B}\). This is the decisive convention and double-counting test.
    Small-q pitfall (shared with P0.3/T1.1). The C2 symmetry kills the ODD blocks only at the exactly-\(\mathbf q{=}\mathbf 0\) spherical lattice sum; the \(\mathbf q\to\mathbf 0\) limit is direction-dependent, carrying a macroscopic term \(\propto \hat q_c\hat q_\alpha\) of magnitude \(4\pi g_{\rm fac}/V_c \approx 3.7\,\mu\)eV (element scale up to ≈ 1.8 μeV on tilted rays — a quarter of Jcc0), and the smallest grid shells sit in the sharp-cutoff crossover of MF_dipole (\(q_{\min}R = O(1)\): for the standard 16³ grid at dpRng = 30, \(q_{\min}R\approx2.3\) along \(a^*\) and ≈ 1.1 along \(c^*\)), so their ODD blocks are dpRng-sensitive. Treat this exactly as the cc channel treats its own shape physics: intrinsic convention (no grid extrapolation at \(\Gamma\); diagonal-only Lorentz broadcast there), decay assertions on high-symmetry rays only, and the smallest-shell dpRng sweep in the validation ladder below. The assertions written into P0.3/T1.1/T1.3 (\(\le10^{-6}\)/\(10^{-4}\cdot\)Jcc0 on the smallest shells) hold on-axis only and must be corrected the same way before implementation.
  2. A1 — practical full-spectrum / projected-\(1/z\) bridge (recommended first deliverable). Decompose the exact local response into a dominant longitudinal low-energy sector plus the remaining transitions, \(\boldsymbol\chi_0=\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm dom}+\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm rest}\), using an explicit transition mask rather than a Cartesian-component shortcut. Apply the existing \(1/z\) self-energy only to the dominant sector, $$\widetilde{\boldsymbol\chi}_0(z) =\frac{\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm dom}(z)}{1+\Sigma_c(z)}+\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm rest}(z),$$ then use \(\widetilde{\boldsymbol\chi}_0\) in the full \(12\times12\) RPA inversion. Recompute the scalar return propagator entering \(K_c\) and \(\Sigma_c\) from the site-diagonal \(cc\) block of that full tensor lattice response. This retains all 136 states and all ODD blocks in the Gaussian propagation while applying the controlled non-Gaussian correction only where the present Jensen formula is valid. It is the multilevel version of the “dominant transition renormalized, weak transitions kept at RPA” approximation used by Jensen/Rønnow.
  3. A2 — matrix effective medium. Promote the impurity and medium to Cartesian matrices and impose the local closure $$\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm lat}(\mathbf q,z) =\big[\{\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm imp}^{(12)}(z)\}^{-1}+\mathbf K^{(12)}(z)-\boldsymbol{\mathcal J}(\mathbf q)\big]^{-1}, \qquad \boldsymbol\chi_{{\rm imp},s}(z)=\frac1{N_q}\sum_{\mathbf q} \boldsymbol\chi_{\rm lat}^{ss}(\mathbf q,z),\quad s=1,\dots,4.$$ Here \(\boldsymbol\chi_{\rm imp}^{(12)}=\operatorname{diag}_s\boldsymbol\chi_{{\rm imp},s}\) and \(\mathbf K^{(12)}=\operatorname{diag}_s\mathbf K_s\) are block-diagonal embeddings of the local \(3\times3\) objects in the lattice space. Begin with four independent local \(3\times3\) blocks and reduce them only after the expected Ho-site symmetry relations have been demonstrated numerically. Unlike a cluster self-energy, the medium remains site-local; the \(12\times12\) dimension belongs to lattice propagation. The scalar\(\to\)matrix template is Jensen's own multi-channel effective medium, J. Jensen, J. Phys. C 17, 5367 (1984); in the single-channel limit the closure above is equivalent to the \(K\) definition of the scalar theory (framework §5).
  4. A3 — genuine tensor \(1/z\) self-energy. For external components \(\mu,\nu\), the first correction has the schematic form $$\delta G_{\mu\nu}(i\omega_n)=-\frac1{2\beta}\sum_{m,\rho\sigma}K_{\rho\sigma}(i\omega_m) \!\int d\tau\,d\tau_1\,d\tau_2\, e^{i\omega_n\tau+i\omega_m(\tau_1-\tau_2)} C^{(4)}_{\mu\nu;\rho\sigma}(\tau,0;\tau_1,\tau_2),$$ where \(C^{(4)}\) is the connected single-ion four-point function built from the actual component sequence, not four copies of one scalar operator. Extract the irreducible matrix self-energy through a Dyson equation; matrix division by \(G_0\), the scalar \(\alpha\)-shortcut, and assumptions of componentwise evenness are forbidden.
    Derivation base — Part II. The machinery for \(C^{(4)}\) already exists at framework level in three_level_1z_extension.html (Part II): its path-sum representation (Part II eqs. 11–12) generalizes directly to component-labelled vertices — the printed eq. 11 is written with four \(J_x\) insertions, but the complete-set-insertion derivation applies verbatim when each vertex carries its own operator's matrix elements — and is precisely the mixed-cumulant engine required here; its KMS boundary-layer counting sizes the mixed sectors a priori (each remote-level excursion costs one factor \(1/\bar\Delta\) weighted by the launching-state population — how ODD physics survives \(T\to0\)); its one-point-shift closure (Part II eq. 10, verified analytically in the Blume–Capel sector) is the derivation behind ladder item (v) below, replacing the forbidden \(\alpha\)-shortcut; and its Blume–Capel and \(\Delta\to0\) limits are ready-made regression anchors for A4. What Part II does not yet provide — it is single-channel by construction — is the matrix medium (A2), the mixed-sector catalogue for two operator patterns, and the coefficient assembly it already earmarks for symbolic automation; those three items are the actual new derivation content of A3.
  5. A4 — controlled state-space convergence. Validate the tensor cumulant engine first on the physical three-state matrices, then on 6 and 17 electronic states. Add hyperfine progressively as 3×8 = 24, 6×8 = 48, and finally 17×8 = 136 states. The final 136-state calculation must retain all virtual intermediate states. Initial-state truncation by population tolerance is not automatically KMS-safe: Part II's thermal-circle identities (its eq. 18) show that an exponentially small initial-state weight can be the boundary-layer image of an \(O(1)\) term and contribute equally after the \(\tau\) integrations. Truncate initial states only after a KMS/cyclic regrouping of the path sums yields an explicit bound on the discarded weight; otherwise retain all 136 initial states. Report convergence of \(T_c\), the ODD shift, the tensor sum rule, and runtime at every level. Note the ceiling of this ladder: a larger local basis does not repair the mean-field-like critical exponents (the \(d=3\) marginal-dimensionality logarithms persist at every rung — Part II Sec. 8); near-critical universal precision must be benchmarked against a critical method such as QMC, not against more states.

A.3 · Numerical strategy for 136 states

A literal storage of \(C^{(4)}_{\mu\nu;\rho\sigma}\) over four state indices and two Matsubara frequencies is not viable: \(136^4\approx3.4\times10^8\) state paths before operator and frequency indices. The feasible route is to contract with \(K_{\rho\sigma}\) while constructing the cumulant. Work in the converged eigenbasis, retain all 136 levels in the intermediate resolvents, exploit Hermiticity/KMS/site symmetries, discard matrix elements only below a recorded tolerance, and use matrix products or resolvent actions rather than explicit four-index loops. Cache only the contracted \(\delta G_{\mu\nu}(i\omega_n)\), never the raw rank-four object.

Validation ladder. (i) \(\Sigma=0\): exact agreement with the full tensor RPA; (ii) ODD blocks zero: agreement with the current scalar \(cc\) implementation in its domain; (iii) physical three-state \(B_x=0\): Schur complement and tensor cumulant reproduce (E1) and the leading Schrieffer–Wolff \(H_{3B}\); (iv) \(\Delta\to\infty\): ODD correction vanishes; (v) tensor reciprocity \(\chi_{\mu\nu}(i\omega)=\chi_{\nu\mu}(-i\omega)\), positivity on the Matsubara axis, and the component-resolved equal-time sum rule close at the chosen numerical tolerance; (vi) 6→17 and 48→136 state changes are smaller than the reported physical uncertainty; (vii) smallest-|q|-shell ODD blocks stable under dpRng 20→40 within the quoted error bars (the small-q pitfall of A0).
Recommended decision point. A0+A1 is feasible with the present code and is the preferred optional bridge: it propagates the full 136-state and full ODD tensor content through RPA while preserving the existing, tested longitudinal \(1/z\) correction — and it subsumes Phase T2, since \(\chi_\perp(i\omega_n)\) retardation is automatic in the tensor RPA. It does not contain Tier 2's physics (see the scope box at the top of this appendix): keep the Tier-2 dressing alongside A1 if the variable-moments effect is wanted before A3. A2+A3 is also feasible in principle, but it is a separate research-scale derivation and numerical implementation, not a small extension of invz_sigma — with three_level_1z_extension.html as its derivation base (see A3). Use the physical three-state system as its exact validation model, not as the final quantitative truncation.
